## 1. Core Identity

### Primary Claim
> *"Amplify the impact of your video content to increase revenue and user engagement using Minute's video technology."*
> — minute-ly.com

### One-Line Value Proposition
> *"Unlock the potential of videos with the power of AI, transforming websites into dynamic content experiences that are highly engaging and create diversified revenue streams."*
> — minute-ly.com/about

### What They Actually Do
Minute.ly operates as a **backend AI video layer** for professional content distributors — not a production or editing tool. Their technology plugs into existing video infrastructure via SDK/API and enhances it with AI-driven thumbnails, highlights, and recommendations.

**Verified client verticals:**
- Sports media (MLB, Fox Sports, Infront.sport)
- News publishers (AccuWeather)
- Digital broadcasters and content owners

**They do NOT position for:** video production, creative editing, consumer social media, or L&D. Any comparison to tools like Wave.video or TubeBuddy is a category error — those serve creators; Minute.ly serves publishers' backend infrastructure.

## 3. Product Portfolio

### 3.1 Core Product Stack

| Product | What It Does | Status |

| Auto Preview Video | Replaces static thumbnails with dynamic AI-powered video previews to boost click-through rates | Active — launched ~2017 |

| Stories by Minute.ly | Mobile-first storytelling capturing peak moments from live or existing video for sites, apps, and social | Active — launched 2019 |

> ⚠️ **Critical gap:** No new products announced since 2019. In a market where competitors ship quarterly updates, a 6+ year product freeze is a significant competitive liability. This is the single most important risk signal in this report.

### 3.2 Technology Layer
Minute.ly's core IP is **deep-video understanding** — frame-level AI analysis of existing video streams to identify key moments, faces, and action peaks without requiring re-encoding or manual tagging. This is a genuine technical differentiator for sports and news publishers who have large archives and live streams but no resources to manually curate highlights.

**Integration model:** SDK/API, not a standalone interface. Clients embed Minute.ly into their existing CMS or video player. This means Minute.ly is invisible to end users — a strength for adoption, a weakness for brand recognition.

## 8. Competitive Landscape

> **Methodology note:** The previous version of this report compared Minute.ly to Wave.video, TubeBuddy, and Switcher Studio. These are creator/consumer tools and are not relevant competitors. Minute.ly competes in the **AI video infrastructure for publishers and broadcasters** segment. The correct competitive set is below.

| Competitor | What They Do | Core Strength vs Minute.ly | Threat Level |
|------------|-------------|---------------------------|--------------|
| **Wibbitz** | AI video creation for publishers (text-to-video for newsrooms) | Stronger newsroom integration, active product development, acquired by Vimeo | 🔴 HIGH |
| **Wochit** | Video creation platform for media companies and publishers | Broader feature set, stronger sales team, transparent pricing | 🔴 HIGH |
| **Grabyo** | Cloud video production and clipping for live sports/news | Real-time clipping from live feeds — direct overlap with sports clients | 🔴 HIGH |
| **Peach (formerly Peach.ly)** | Video workflow automation for broadcasters | More modern stack; broadcaster-native integrations | 🟡 MEDIUM |
| **Thuuz Sports** | AI sports highlight generation | Direct overlap in sports highlight use case | 🟡 MEDIUM |
| **AWS MediaConvert + Rekognition** | Cloud video processing with AI analysis | Infinite scale, zero marginal cost for existing AWS clients — existential if bundled | ⚫ EXISTENTIAL |
| **Google Video AI** | Scene detection, content moderation, video intelligence API | Same underlying capability, bundled with GCP at near-zero cost for tech-forward publishers | ⚫ EXISTENTIAL |

**Most dangerous competitive dynamic:** AWS and Google offer overlapping AI video analysis capabilities as part of their existing cloud contracts. A publisher already on AWS who discovers Rekognition can do frame-level analysis does not need Minute.ly. This is the existential threat — not a direct SaaS competitor, but platform bundling.

## 9. Win / Loss Summary

### Minute.ly Likely Wins When…
- Buyer is a sports broadcaster or publisher who needs AI highlights from live feeds — Minute.ly has years of domain-specific training data here
- Client is already using a legacy video stack and needs a non-disruptive AI layer — SDK model is genuinely low-friction
- Speed to integration matters more than feature breadth — smaller scope means faster deployment vs. Wochit or Grabyo
- Existing relationship or referral from Infront.sport / current client network

### Minute.ly Likely Loses When…
- Buyer evaluates using G2/Capterra → Minute.ly is invisible; competitors capture the deal
- Modern AI capabilities required → 6-year product freeze leaves Minute.ly behind current benchmarks
- Buyer is on AWS or GCP → cloud-native video AI available at near-zero marginal cost
- Pricing transparency required early in evaluation → no pricing page creates immediate friction
- Company stability is a procurement requirement → no recent funding is a red flag for enterprise contracts

- No product updates in 6+ years — credibility gap in fast-moving AI market
- No review presence — acquisition funnel effectively closed to inbound discovery
- Funding plateau since 2019 — vendor stability risk for long-term contracts

## 10. Regulatory & Exit Context

### EU AI Act — August 2026
EU AI Act requires synthetic media labeling by August 2026. Minute.ly's technology analyzes and repurposes existing video rather than generating synthetic content, which may partially exempt it from the strictest requirements. However, AI-generated thumbnails and auto-edited highlights could fall within scope depending on final implementation guidance.

**Status:** No C2PA watermarking or EU AI Act compliance positioning found on minute-ly.com. This is a gap for any European publisher clients.
