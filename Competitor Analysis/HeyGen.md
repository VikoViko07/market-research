### 5.2 Hidden Costs (Verified)

- **Premium Credit Packs:** $15/month or $150/year per 300 credits — required for Avatar IV, lip-synced translation, Sora/Veo B-roll. Credits expire monthly, no rollover on monthly plans.
- **Additional custom avatar slots:** $29/month per Video Avatar, $49/month per LiveAvatar
- **API access:** Completely separate subscription — web plan does NOT include API
- **Failed renders:** Verified user reports of credits consumed on failed renders
- **Translation reductions:** "Unlimited" translation was retroactively capped at 120 min/month for existing annual subscribers (verified Capterra/Trustpilot, 2025)

> ⚠️ The gap between advertised "unlimited" and actual credit-gated behavior is the #1 complaint category across G2, Capterra, and Trustpilot. This is a structural trust issue, not a feature gap.

## 9. Win / Loss Summary

### HeyGen Likely Wins When…
- Buyer prioritizes avatar realism — Avatar IV is the current benchmark across independent tests
- Budget matters: HeyGen Creator ($24/mo, unlimited Avatar III) dramatically undercuts Synthesia's Creator ($64/mo, 360 min/year)
- Quick self-serve onboarding — no account friction on free tier is weaker than Synthesia, but Video Agent makes the first video genuinely impressive
- Video translation at scale — 175+ languages, lip-synced, available at Creator tier
- Interactive video agents needed now — Video Agent 2.0 is live; Synthesia's agents are not yet GA
- Developer / API use case — LiveAvatar API via WebRTC is unique in the market at this quality level
- Marketing and SMB segment — pricing, avatar volume, and speed align with this buyer

### HeyGen Likely Loses When…
- Enterprise compliance is a procurement requirement → Synthesia wins (ISO 42001, C2PA, audit logs)
- Regulated industry (healthcare, legal, finance) → Synthesia's stricter moderation is the feature, not the bug
- Support SLA is required → 80% Trustpilot negative rate is an unacceptable signal for enterprise procurement
- Pricing predictability is required → credit system complexity and mid-subscription changes are deal-killers for finance-controlled budgets
- Long-term vendor stability clause is needed → Synthesia's Fortune 100 track record outweighs HeyGen's faster growth
- Deep L&D integration (SCORM, branching, LMS) → SCORM on Business tier ($149/mo+) vs. Synthesia's Creator tier ($64/mo)

### Verified Churn Triggers (G2 / Capterra / Trustpilot)
- Retroactive "unlimited" → capped changes with no notice (verified, recurring theme across 3 platforms)
- Customer support AI loops, no human escalation path (Trustpilot: dominant complaint category)
- Credit consumption unpredictability — failed renders, inconsistent rates
- Platform instability after updates — features break, preview ≠ final output
- Video Agent pricing perceived as high for value received (G2, enterprise segment)

## 10. Regulatory & Exit Context

### EU AI Act — August 2026
HeyGen states EU AI Act alignment and consent-verified avatar creation. However, unlike Synthesia, there is **no C2PA watermarking confirmed** on heygen.com as of March 2026. This matters for EU enterprise buyers: after August 2026, platforms without synthetic media labeling cannot serve regulated EU buyers legally.

**Risk for HeyGen:** If EU AI Act enforcement is strict on watermarking standards, HeyGen's current compliance posture may be insufficient for the enterprise segment.
**Risk for buyers:** Any EU-based company standardizing on HeyGen for regulated content should confirm C2PA compliance before August 2026 deadline.
