# Topic: "How Germany Is Losing Its Industrial Edge"

---

## Why This Topic Is Evergreen

This is not a news story. It is a structural story — the slow, decades-long deindustrialization of Europe's largest manufacturing economy. The data will remain relevant for 10+ years because:

- Germany's manufacturing share of GDP has been declining since 2007
- The structural causes (energy costs, China competition, aging workforce, underinvestment) are multi-decade trends
- The comparison countries (France, UK, South Korea, Japan) give the story a permanent global context
- The video answers "why" and "how" — not "what happened this week"

**Comparable evergreen videos on this pattern:**
- Wendover Productions: "The World's Fastest Growing Economy" (structural economic transformation)
- Johnny Harris: "Why Germany Is In Trouble" (structural decline narrative)
- RealLifeLore: "How Germany's Economy Collapsed" (long-run industrial comparison)

---

## Step 1 — Define the Story in One Sentence

> "Germany built the 20th century on manufacturing. It is now losing that edge — and no one agrees on whether it can be rebuilt."

This sentence contains: a historical claim, a current trend, and an open question. That is the template for an evergreen video.

**The three-act structure:**
1. What Germany was (manufacturing giant, 2000–2018)
2. What changed (energy crisis, China competition, production collapse 2018–2024)
3. What comes next (open question — the viewer decides)

**Known limitation:** World Bank data lags by 1–2 years. For 2024 figures use Destatis directly.

**These are real numbers from Destatis official press releases. Verified April 2026.**

**Source:** Destatis / Federal Employment Agency (Bundesagentur für Arbeit)  
**License:** Government data — free  
**Direct URL:** destatis.de/EN/Themes/Labour/Labour-Market/Employment  
**Alternative:** Eurostat — ec.europa.eu/eurostat/databrowser → search "employment by sector"

## Step 4 — Join the Datasets

### The derived metric that tells the story

No single dataset shows "Germany is losing its industrial edge" in one number. You need to create it.

**Derived metric:** Manufacturing Production Index (2018 = 100) for Germany vs South Korea vs France

**How to build it:**

```
File 1: Destatis industrial production index Germany (2015–2024)
File 2: OECD production index South Korea (2015–2024)  
File 3: Eurostat production index France (2015–2024)

Join key: Year (all three files use calendar year)

Normalize: Set 2018 = 100 for all three countries
Formula: (value in year X / value in 2018) × 100

New column: Index_Germany, Index_SouthKorea, Index_France
```

**What the derived metric reveals:** If Germany's index in 2024 is ~85 (down 15% from 2018) while South Korea's is ~110 (up 10%), the slope chart makes the divergence viscerally clear without a word of explanation.

**This is the "Wendover method":** Take public data from two sources, create a derived ratio that no single source publishes, and that derived ratio becomes the unique insight of the video.

## Step 8 — Known Data Quality Issues

**Issue 1 — Revisions:** Destatis industrial production figures are revised multiple times. The December 2024 figure (-2.4%) was itself a revision of an earlier provisional figure. Always use the most recently published figure, not the first press release.

**Solution:** Check genesis.destatis.de for the latest revision before publishing. Add "preliminary data subject to revision" to the on-screen source label if using figures less than 6 months old.

**Issue 2 — 2022 methodology change:** In July 2022, Destatis changed the method for calculating production index in automotive manufacturing to better capture electric vehicles. This creates a break in the series. Data before and after July 2022 is not fully comparable for the automotive sub-sector.

**Solution:** Use the overall manufacturing production index (which was not affected) rather than the automotive sub-index for the long-run chart. Mention the methodology change in the video description.

**Issue 3 — "Energy-intensive industries" definition:** Destatis defines five specific sectors as "energy-intensive." This definition differs from Eurostat's classification. If comparing German energy-intensive output to other EU countries, use Eurostat data for both — not Destatis for Germany and Eurostat for others.

**Solution:** Use Eurostat as the single source for all cross-country energy comparisons.

**Issue 4 — Jobs data complexity:** The 60,000 job cuts figure comes from company announcements aggregated by Bloomberg, not from official statistics. Official employment data from Destatis shows total employment at a record high because service sector job creation offset manufacturing losses. Both facts are true and both belong in the video — they tell different parts of the story.

**Solution:** Use both figures with clear attribution. "Total employment is at a record high (Destatis, 2025) — but all job growth is in services. Major manufacturers cut over 60,000 positions in 2024 (company announcements via Bloomberg)."
