## Topic: "How the Gulf States Built Cities in the Desert"

---

## Why This Topic Is Evergreen

This is a structural transformation story — how six countries sitting on top of oil and gas reserves turned desert coastlines into global financial, tourism, and logistics hubs in under 50 years. The data will remain relevant for 10+ years because:

- The diversification from oil to non-oil GDP is a multi-decade process still actively unfolding
- UAE, Qatar, and Saudi Arabia have announced plans (Vision 2030, Vision 2030 Qatar) extending to 2030 and beyond
- The comparison between countries that diversified (UAE, Bahrain) and those that did not (Kuwait, Qatar) is permanently instructive
- The video answers "how" and "why" — not "what happened this week"

**Comparable evergreen videos on this pattern:**
- Wendover Productions: "The World's Fastest Growing Economy" (Guyana oil transformation)
- Johnny Harris: Gulf state economic transformation videos
- RealLifeLore: "How Dubai Became the World's Most Ambitious City"

---

## Step 1 — Define the Story in One Sentence

> "The Gulf states used oil money to build cities from scratch — but only some of them used that window to make oil unnecessary."

**The three-act structure:**
1. What oil wealth made possible (1970s–2000s: building the infrastructure)
2. Who diversified and who did not (2000–2024: the divergence)
3. What comes next (post-oil futures: open question)

**The visual hook:** Dubai's oil share of GDP fell from ~50% in 1990 to less than 1% today. Oil still accounts for over 60% of Qatar's GDP. Both countries are among the wealthiest on Earth — but they are on completely different trajectories. That divergence is the story.

## Step 3 — Find Each Dataset (5 Minutes Per Source)

### Variable 1: Oil Rents as % of GDP, GCC countries, 1990–2024
**Source:** World Bank Open Data
**License:** CC BY 4.0 — free with attribution
**Direct URL:** data.worldbank.org/indicator/NY.GDP.PETR.RT.ZS?locations=AE-QA-SA-KW-BH-OM
**How to download:** Click "Download" → CSV → open in Excel
**Time to data:** 4 minutes

**Key verified data points:**
- UAE oil rents % GDP: falling from ~30% (1990s) toward single digits by 2020s
- Dubai oil contribution to Dubai GDP: **less than 1%** (Wikipedia / Dubai.com, multiple sources)
- UAE non-oil share of total GDP: **71%** (Wikipedia / Economy of Middle East)
- Qatar oil + gas: **over 60% of GDP**, **over 70% of government revenue** (World Bank Group, via economymiddleeast.com)
- Qatar oil rents % GDP in 2017: **14.2%** (Statista / infoDev — note: this measures oil rents specifically, not total hydrocarbons)
- Bahrain non-oil share: **85.9% of GDP** (Reuters, via sapa-usa.org)
- GCC total GDP 2024: **~$2.37 trillion** (World Bank Gulf Economic Update, December 2024)
- Saudi Arabia: 53% of total GCC output

**Known limitation:** World Bank "oil rents" measures only crude oil, not natural gas. For Qatar — a gas-dominant economy — total hydrocarbon dependency is significantly higher than the oil rents figure alone suggests. Always use "oil + gas rents" or cite the IMF/World Bank country report for Qatar specifically.

## Step 4 — Join the Datasets

### The derived metric that tells the story

No single dataset shows "who diversified and who didn't" across all six GCC countries in one number. The derived metric:

**Non-Oil GDP Share, 2024 vs 2000** — showing the 24-year trajectory for each country

**How to build it:**

```
File 1: World Bank → Oil rents (% of GDP) by GCC country by year
        Indicator: NY.GDP.PETR.RT.ZS
        Countries: AE, QA, SA, KW, BH, OM
        Years: 2000–2024

Derived column: Non-oil GDP share = 100% - Oil rents %
(Note: this is approximate — see Known Limitations below)

Slope chart: 2000 value vs 2024 value for each country
Sort: By 2024 non-oil share (most diversified at top)
```

**What the derived metric reveals:**
- UAE 2000: oil rents ~30% → UAE 2024: oil rents ~5% (non-oil: 95%) — dramatic diversification
- Bahrain: already 85.9% non-oil — most diversified GCC economy
- Qatar: oil+gas still 60%+ of GDP — least diversified of the wealthy states
- Saudi Arabia: moving but slowly — Vision 2030 is mid-journey

**This is the Wendover method:** Two public datasets, one derived column, one slope chart that tells the entire story without a word of narration.

## Step 8 — Known Data Quality Issues

**Issue 1 — "Oil rents" vs "hydrocarbons"**
The World Bank's "Oil rents (% of GDP)" indicator measures only crude oil, not natural gas. For Qatar — where natural gas generates far more revenue than oil — this indicator dramatically understates hydrocarbon dependency. Qatar's oil rents alone may show 14% of GDP, but total oil+gas revenues account for 60%+ of GDP.

**Solution:** For Qatar (and to a lesser extent Kuwait and Oman), supplement World Bank oil rents with IMF country reports or the World Bank Gulf Economic Update which report total hydrocarbon dependency. In the on-screen source attribution write: "Source: World Bank (oil rents) + IMF (total hydrocarbons, Qatar)."

**Issue 2 — UAE aggregate vs Dubai specifically**
UAE is a federation of seven emirates. Abu Dhabi holds 90%+ of UAE oil reserves and remains oil-dependent. Dubai has diversified almost completely. The aggregate UAE figure (71% non-oil) masks a stark internal divergence: Abu Dhabi would show much higher oil dependency, Dubai would show 95%+ non-oil. Using UAE aggregate to tell the "Dubai miracle" story is technically accurate but editorially imprecise.

**Solution:** Be explicit in narration: "Across the UAE as a whole, 71% of GDP now comes from non-oil sectors. In Dubai specifically, that number exceeds 95%." Use both figures.

**Issue 3 — Population denominator for GDP per capita**
GCC countries have unusual demographic structures: 70–90% of residents are expatriate workers with no path to citizenship. GDP per capita figures include all residents — but the wealth is distributed almost entirely to citizens. Qatar's $114,648 per capita PPP figure is technically correct but tells a very different story for the ~300,000 Qatari citizens vs the ~2.8 million expatriate residents.

**Solution:** Add a pull-quote card with this fact: "Qatar has 300,000 citizens and 2.8 million residents. The wealth is not equally distributed." This is one of the most shareable moments in any Gulf video — the gap between the headline number and the reality.

**Issue 4 — "Vision 2030" performance claims**
Saudi Arabia's Vision 2030 targets are extensively cited in media. Some have been met, some have not, and many are aspirational projections. The 2030 target for non-oil revenue share (Vision: 50% non-oil GDP contribution from private sector) should not be presented as achieved.

**Solution:** Always distinguish between stated targets and verified data. Use only World Bank and IMF figures for current performance, not Vision 2030 ministry press releases.

## The Derived Metric — Why It Makes This Video Unique

No single dataset publishes "non-oil GDP share trajectory for all 6 GCC countries from 2000 to 2024" as a downloadable table. It must be constructed:

```
Step 1: Download World Bank NY.GDP.PETR.RT.ZS for AE, QA, SA, KW, BH, OM
Step 2: For Qatar: cross-reference with IMF country report for total hydrocarbons
Step 3: Calculate non-oil share = 100% - oil rents %
Step 4: Build slope chart: 2000 value on left axis, 2024 value on right axis
Step 5: Color: UAE and Bahrain = blue (rising non-oil), Qatar and Kuwait = amber (flat/slow)
```

The slope chart produced by this process shows — at a single glance — which countries used their oil window and which did not. That visual does not exist anywhere in published form. It is the unique editorial contribution of this video.
