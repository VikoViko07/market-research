## PART II. THE MAPS: HOW THEY ARE BUILT

### 2.1 The Production Stack ✅

Reed is the only significant channel in the niche to have publicly stated this:
> *"How I make my videos: Adobe After Effects (GeoLayers3 Plug-in) + Premiere Pro"*

**Adobe After Effects** — the core animation environment:
Professional timeline, keyframe animation, compositing, shape layers, camera tools. The industry standard for motion graphics of any complexity.

**GEOlayers 3** (~$350 one-time purchase):
An After Effects plugin that renders real-world geodata as fully editable shape layers directly inside AE. This is not "inserting a map image" — it is a complete cartographic system embedded inside an animation editor.

**What GEOlayers 3 can do:**
- Scroll, zoom, pitch, and rotate maps through keyframes
- Data sources: MapTiler Cloud, OpenStreetMap, ESRI, Mapbox Satellite, Natural Earth
- One-click auto-labelling of cities and countries
- Animated routes, territory fills, and border outlines
- .CSV / .TSV import for data-driven visualizations
- 3D building extrusion, terrain relief, water masks

**Adobe Premiere Pro** — final edit:
Assembly of AE compositions, audio synchronisation, colour grade, export.

**Additional — the Shorts stack** ✅:
For YouTube Shorts, Reed uses **Google Maps and satellite imagery** — a simpler, instantly recognisable visual language. This is a deliberate split: Shorts rely on familiarity (everyone knows Google Maps), long-form relies on cinematic quality (GEOlayers3 produces Hollywood-grade cartographic animation).

### 2.2 The Visual Language of the Maps — Content Analysis 🔍

Based on visual analysis of publicly available content:

**Base map layer:**
- Clean flat-design base (not photorealistic satellite imagery in most videos)
- Muted colour palette — greys, sandy tones, desaturated blues as backgrounds
- Clear political boundaries
- Minimal label density — only the most critical place names visible at any moment

**Dynamic annotation layer:**
- Country/region fills — territories light up in a colour when narratively activated
- Animated directional arrows for routes and movements
- Pulsing markers on key cities
- Temporal overlays — dates and facts appearing in sync with the narration

**Camera motion layer:**
- Zoom toward the region under discussion
- Pan along a route or border
- Smooth transitions from global to local scale

**Shorts visual logic:**
- Google Maps as the base (recognisability = immediate click)
- Satellite imagery for spatial "wow-factor"
- One fact — one map — one zoom point
- Vertical format optimised for mobile viewing

### 2.3 Five Map Design Rules 🔍

Derived from analysis of Reed's top videos, cross-referenced with publicly documented best practices from his closest niche peers:

**Rule 1:** No more than 5 elements on the map at once
Each new element adds cognitive load. The map must be readable instantly, not studied.

**Rule 2:** Every camera move has a narrative reason
Zoom in because the story is zooming in — not because it looks professional.

**Rule 3:** Colour = character
Each state, region, or territory receives a colour at the start and keeps it throughout the entire video.

**Rule 4:** The voiceover does not describe the map
If the border is visible on screen, the narrator explains WHY it is there — not WHERE it is.

**Rule 5:** The map must work on mute
Test: if the audio is switched off, the spatial story must still be comprehensible. If it is not, the visual layer has failed.

## PART IV. CONTENT STRATEGY

### 4.1 The Core Editorial Formula ✅

**"The Geographic Puzzle": familiar object + unnoticed anomaly + historical answer through a map**

The top video "Why are so many US states split in two?" is the perfect illustration:
1. **Familiar object:** the map of the US, which the viewer has seen thousands of times
2. **Unnoticed anomaly:** states are divided into disconnected parts — but the viewer has never asked why
3. **Historical answer:** borders as artifacts of specific historical and geographic decisions
4. **Map as proof:** GEOlayers animates the process by which those borders formed

This is the "closure gap" cognitive mechanism — the brain wants to resolve the knowledge gap created by the title. The click is the brain resolving it.

### 4.2 The Title Formula ✅

Reed systematically uses a question as the title:
- "Why are so many US states split in two?"
- "Why does X happen?"
- "Why is X located here?"
- "What happened to X?"

This is a direct application of the "cognitive gap title" principle: the title creates a feeling of not-knowing that is uncomfortable — and can only be resolved by clicking.

**Additional SEO effect:** question-format titles rank better in YouTube Search for queries starting with "why does..." and "how does..." — this generates organic search traffic on top of algorithmic Browse distribution.

### 4.3 Topic Clusters 🔍

Based on publicly described videos and the channel's structural organisation:

**Cluster A — Political border anomalies (the strongest)**
States split into non-contiguous pieces. Unusual boundaries. Exclaves. Historically inherited territorial quirks. This is Reed's primary niche — hit videos like "Why are so many US states split in two?" originate here.

**Cluster B — Geographic "strangeness"**
The world's most remote island. Locations with unusual legal statuses. Extreme demographic facts (95% of China's population in the east). This is Shorts territory: one surprising fact + one map.

**Cluster C — Geopolitics through geography**
How the physical environment determines political behaviour. Why countries act the way they do. Historical causes of contemporary conflicts. These are more complex topics suited to long-form.

### 4.4 The Dual Format Strategy: Long-form + Shorts ✅

This is the channel's key architectural decision.

**Long-form (5–15 min) — the GEOlayers3 pipeline:**
- Cinematic maps, complex animation
- Full narrative with historical context
- Generates Watch Time, AdSense revenue, loyal subscribers
- Published 1–2 times per month

**Shorts (up to 60 sec) — the Google Maps pipeline:**
- One fact, one map, one zoom
- Instantly recognisable visual language
- Generates reach, new subscribers, algorithmic impulse in the Shorts feed
- Published significantly more frequently

**Why this is smart:** Shorts attract the viewer; long-form converts them into a subscriber. According to YouTube 2026 data, channels that use both Shorts and long-form simultaneously grow **41% faster** than long-form-only channels. Reed is built into this mechanic.

## PART V. AI TOOLS: WHAT IS LIKELY USED AND WHAT IS NOT

### 5.1 An Honest Disclaimer

Reed has not disclosed his AI workflow in any public source. Everything below is inference from structural analysis, standard edu-creator economy practices of 2024–2026, and indirect signals.

### 5.2 Where AI Is Likely Involved 🔍

**Research and factual foundation:**
At 2–3 videos per month with narratives requiring historical accuracy, an AI assistant accelerates primary data gathering 3–5×. Most likely tools: **Perplexity AI** (sourced search for research), **Claude / ChatGPT** for the initial structural draft of the argument.

Important caveat: with zero public fact-checking exposés over 2.5 years, the final fact-check is done by a human. AI provides the skeleton; Reed fills it with accuracy.

**Scripting:**
AI generates an initial structural draft ("here are 5 reasons why states are split"), Reed rewrites it in his own voice with editorial nuance. This is the standard workflow for edu-channels in 2024–2026.

**SEO and titles:**
Tools like **vidIQ** (AI-assisted keyword selection, title clickability analysis) or **TubeBuddy** are standard practice for growing channels. Reed's formulaic titles ("Why are so many X split in...") look like they are optimised against specific search query patterns.

**Transcription and subtitles:**
Automated subtitle generation — standard YouTube Studio functionality, AI-enhanced. Useful for search indexing and accessibility.

### 5.3 Where AI Is Not Used or Used Minimally 🔍

**Cartographic animation:**
GEOlayers3 + After Effects is handmade keyframe work. AI cannot produce custom narrative cartographic animations at this level. This is the most labour-intensive part of production and the one piece that Reed definitively does himself.

**Voiceover:**
Reed is a real person (Facebook, Twitter, TikTok profiles are public). High probability of his own voice. AI voice synthesis (ElevenLabs, etc.) became indistinguishable to casual viewers by 2025 — but for a channel with a personal brand, an authentic voice is a trust advantage that AI cannot replicate.

**Final edit:**
Assembly in Premiere Pro is manual work. AI may assist with transcription for subclip workflows, but the edit itself remains human.

### 5.4 The Probable Full AI Workflow 🔍

```
Step 1: TOPIC
↳ Reed selects from his list of "geographic puzzles"
↳ Perplexity / ChatGPT: search for primary facts and sources (1–2 hrs vs 6+ without AI)

Step 2: SCRIPT
↳ ChatGPT / Claude: initial structural draft (30 min)
↳ Reed: rewrites in his own voice, adds nuance, verifies facts (2–3 hrs)

Step 3: MAP
↳ After Effects + GEOlayers3: entirely manual keyframe work
↳ Planning which regions to show, in what sequence, with what colour logic (2–4 days)

Step 4: VOICEOVER
↳ Reed's own voice (probable)
↳ Rode / Shure USB microphone, basic acoustic treatment

Step 5: EDIT
↳ Adobe Premiere Pro: assembly of AE compositions + audio
↳ vidIQ / TubeBuddy: SEO optimisation of title, description, tags (30 min)

Step 6: SHORTS
↳ Recut of long-form via CapCut or Premiere
↳ Or a standalone Short with Google Maps (1–2 hrs)
```

**Estimated total production cycle:** 5–10 days per long-form video as a solo operation.
