## BLOCK I. PRODUCTION & VISUALS

### 1. "Dopamine Editing" — 3–8 Seconds Per Shot
**Source:** `[SIMPLICISSIMUS]`  
Average shot length: 3–8 seconds. Continuous visual change through parallax whips, particle transitions, and light leaks. This is the "dopamin-schnitt" — editing that delivers a constant stream of small visual stimuli.  
**What it produced:** viewer retention across 30–66-minute faceless videos. With 60+ team members and 1–2 videos per month, this is one of the best retention rates in the niche.  
**What it will give you:** reduced click-off in the first 5 minutes and higher average view duration — both of which directly influence algorithmic distribution.

> 📝 **NOTE:** The dopamine-editing technique is powerful but carries an inherent scalability trap: it requires a well-staffed post-production pipeline to sustain. For a team of 2–3 people, implementing full parallax/particle transitions on every shot will bottleneck production long before it boosts retention. A pragmatic entry point is to apply this technique selectively — opening 90 seconds and act transitions — rather than attempting it wall-to-wall from day one.

---

### 2. The "Pinboard" Technique — Archival Photos Split into 5–10 Layers
**Source:** `[SIMPLICISSIMUS]`  
Take an archival photo or document → split it into 5–10 depth layers in Photoshop/After Effects → place each layer on a different Z-axis → add a virtual camera (slow push-in, dutch angle, orbit) → overlay dust/grain/smoke.  
**What it produced:** a unique visual style that requires no live-action production yet creates a cinematic feel. 11 years without a visible host — and no one has asked to see one.  
**What it will give you:** the ability to create cinematic content without a crew, location shoots, or actors. Particularly valuable for small teams.

> 📝 **NOTE:** This is one of the most transferable techniques in the guide — low hardware cost, no shooting required, replicable with After Effects and a stock photo subscription. Worth listing as a priority Day-1 skill investment. One caveat: archival photo licensing. Simplicissimus operates under German press law; a new channel should build a clear workflow for public domain or CC-licensed source material from the outset.

### 3. The "Lead with Awesome" Rule — The First 30 Seconds Decide Everything
**Source:** `[VERITASIUM]`  
The first 30 seconds are always a hook: a question, a demonstration, or a shocking fact. No introductions, no "hey subscribers," no title cards. The viewer sees the "impossible" result first — the explanation comes later.  
**What it produced:** "The Ridiculous Engineering Of The World's Most Important Machine" (ASML) — #1 by views in the first 7 days (11.3M). "This Paradox Splits Smart People 50/50" — 5.9M views. The mystery-reveal structure works across any niche.  
**What it will give you:** a sharp reduction in bounce rate in the first 30 seconds — a critical metric for the YouTube algorithm in 2025–2026.

> 📝 **NOTE:** This principle is well-established across all 8 channels in the analysis and should be treated as non-negotiable table stakes, not an advanced technique. If there is one rule to internalize before the first video is published, it is this one. It also maps directly to the 4-Layer structure in Block VIII (Hook layer = first 45 seconds).

### 4. 1,200 Hours = Maximum Quality Per Video as Strategy
**Source:** `[KURZGESAGT]`  
One video = 200+ unique illustrations created from scratch, 8–10 weeks of animation, original music per video, 2–3 animators, scientist-reviewed fact-checking. The studio published "How to Make a Kurzgesagt Video in 1200 Hours" — full process transparency as a content strategy.  
**What it produced:** 4+ billion total views, ~12–15M average views per video — the best in the series. All at just 1–2 videos per month.  
**What it will give you:** each video becomes an "event" that the audience anticipates. Frequency pressure drops as the value of each release rises. Suitable when resources are available; not a viable starting point without a team.

> 📝 **NOTE:** The guide correctly flags this as a resources-dependent model. However, the deeper lesson from Kurzgesagt is not "spend more" — it is "establish a quality floor and never publish below it." Kurzgesagt's 1,200-hour figure is the result of a fully-staffed studio that has been refining one visual language for 12+ years. The actionable principle for a new channel: define your own non-negotiable quality baseline (even if it is a 40-hour video), publish nothing below it, and communicate that standard to your audience through a process-transparency video early on.

### 5. Maps as the Primary Visual Tool — GIS Data in Animation
**Source:** `[NEO]`  
All explanations are built around custom cartographic animations: flyovers, borders, flows, routes. Adobe After Effects + GIS/QGIS data. Maps are hand-crafted for each video, not sourced from stock.  
**What it produced:** "How the U.S. Found Saddam Hussein" — 6.3M views and top-5 Tubefilter Branded Videos. Across 78 videos: 365M total views, averaging ~4.7M per video.  
**What it will give you:** a unique visual language that cannot be replicated without specific GIS skills. Built-in protection against copying. Especially effective for geopolitics, economics, and history.

> 📝 **NOTE:** GIS-driven cartography is highly defensible as a visual signature, but the entry cost is real: QGIS/AE integration has a learning curve of several weeks minimum, and the workflow for each map is substantial. For a German-language explainer channel covering economic and infrastructure topics (Deutsche Bahn, Rhine logistics, Deindustrialisierung), this technique is a direct fit and worth the investment. For a channel with a different topical focus, simpler map styles (Flourish, Datawrapper animated exports) can approximate the effect at a fraction of the production time.

**Source:** `[VERITASIUM]`

For a new channel, a Blackmagic Pocket 6K + a single-person field shoot can produce credible location footage at under $5K total setup cost. **Source:** `[BREAKINGLAB]`

## BLOCK II. CONTENT & TOPICS

### 8. The "Meme Hook" — Choose Topics That Have Already Gone Viral
**Source:** `[NEO]`  
Choose topics that have already become memes or viral images, then explain the real story behind them. Example: the photo of Saddam Hussein in the pit became a meme → NEO makes a documentary about the capture operation → 6.3M views.  
**What it produced:** the video ranked top-5 in the weekly YouTube branded chart. Sponsor Nebula received maximum visibility.  
**What it will give you:** organic virality through pre-existing audience interest. The algorithm picks up the video via meme-topic search traffic + browse traffic from ambient interest.

> 📝 **NOTE:** This technique has a time-window problem: meme cycles move faster than documentary production cycles. By the time a 5-week production is complete, the meme may have peaked. The most reliable version of this strategy is a "pre-prepared evergreen" approach: identify 10–15 topics with durable meme-image associations (e.g. the "arrow pointing to Germany on EU map" genre) and develop them on a rolling basis, rather than chasing fresh viral moments.

### 9. News Trigger + Deep Explanation = Traffic Spike
**Source:** `[WENDOVER PRODUCTIONS]` `[SIMPLICISSIMUS]`  
Wendover: "How Israel's Security Failed" published within days of October 7, 2023. Simplicissimus: reactive content to high-profile lobbying scandals. Formula: current event → video published within 1–2 weeks → traffic flows from news search and algorithm simultaneously.  
**What it produced:** for Wendover — the moment of maximum organic reach in the channel's history. For Simplicissimus — recurring spikes of 1–2M views on timely topics.  
**What it will give you:** the ability to ride a search impulse without a viral video. Requires a ready editorial pipeline for rapid production.

> 📝 **NOTE:** This is the most operationally demanding technique in Block II. To publish within 7–14 days of a news event, a channel needs: a topic backlog of partially-researched scripts that can be accelerated, a production pipeline that can compress without breaking quality, and clear editorial decision authority (who decides to "pull the trigger" on a reactive piece). Without these preconditions, attempting news-reactive content produces either late, mediocre output or missed cycles. Build the infrastructure first.

### 10. "Optimistic Nihilism" — Discussing the Frightening Without Causing Panic
**Source:** `[KURZGESAGT]`  
Topics: death, black holes, the end of the universe, mass extinction. But the tone is: "the world is vast and strange, and that's beautiful." Not anxiety — a philosophy of acceptance. "Optimistic Nihilism" — 18M+ views.  
**What it produced:** unique audience loyalty — Kurzgesagt viewers describe the videos as "giving me an existential crisis, but in a good way." A distinct genre of emotional experience, not just information.  
**What it will give you:** emotional differentiation from competitors. Viewers return not for facts but for a feeling. One of the most powerful long-term retention drivers.

> 📝 **NOTE:** This tonal signature is Kurzgesagt's single most defensible competitive asset — and the hardest to replicate, precisely because it is not a technique but a philosophy embedded across every creative decision. A channel covering German economic and social topics has an equivalent opportunity: the "curious adult who refuses to panic" voice. German public discourse defaults to either political alarm or bureaucratic detachment; a channel that explains structural problems with intellectual calm and genuine curiosity fills a real tonal gap.

### 11. Two Topic Categories: "Universally Frightening" or "Everyone Uses It, Nobody Understands It"
**Source:** `[KURZGESAGT]`  
- Type A: "Will a black hole kill us?", "Are we alone in the universe?" — universal fear  
- Type B: "How does the immune system work?", "Why do plane tickets cost what they do?" — hidden systems everyone relies on  
Both categories guarantee search demand regardless of audience language.  
**What it produced:** 4+ billion views across ~310 videos.  
**What it will give you:** a systematic approach to topic selection instead of intuitive guessing. Each topic is vetted against one of the two criteria before being greenlit.

> 📝 **NOTE:** This two-category framework is the most portable analytical tool in the entire guide and should be used as a literal checklist at the top of every editorial planning session. One addition worth making: a third screening question — "Is this topic better served by a 10-minute explainer or a 30-second Short?" Type A topics (existential fear) tend to generate stronger long-form engagement; Type B topics (hidden systems) often work well as a Short teaser → long-form payoff funnel.

### 12. Serialization Within the Channel — "Mapped," "Portraits," "Places"
**Source:** `[NEO]`  
Structure content into named series with clear rules. Mapped: geopolitics through maps. Portraits: biographies. Places: locations of historical events. Each series gets its own playlist and its own algorithmic traffic stream.  
**What it produced:** viewers subscribe to a series, not just a video. Playlist traffic is a separate algorithmic signal. NEO sustains ~4.7M views/video at 1 video every 5–6 weeks.  
**What it will give you:** growth in session watch time through autoplay playlists. A viewer who starts one video in a series is highly likely to continue to the next.

### 13. Research Standards — All Sources Listed, Every Claim Verified
**Source:** `[BREAKINGLAB]` `[KURZGESAGT]`  
BreakingLab: research links directly in the video description. All experts cited are credited. Kurzgesagt: every script is reviewed by scientists; "no editorial influence from sponsors" is written into every contract.  
**What it produced:** BreakingLab received the DPG Medaille (Germany's first physics award to a YouTube channel). Kurzgesagt maintained audience trust even after accusations of being "bought off by billionaires."  
**What it will give you:** long-term reputation — the only durable protection against AI-generated competition. Channels with trusted reputations command 3–5x higher CPMs from advertisers.

> 📝 **NOTE:** In the current media environment, where AI-generated content with plausible-sounding but unverified claims is proliferating rapidly, a public and consistent research standard is not just an ethical choice — it is a monetization strategy. Advertisers in the German market (pharma, automotive, financial services, public sector) pay significant CPM premiums for "brand-safe" channels with verifiable editorial standards. A published editorial charter (even a single page on a channel website) is a competitive asset, not overhead.

## BLOCK III. MONETIZATION

### 14. Merchandise as the Primary Revenue Source (40% of Revenue)
**Source:** `[KURZGESAGT]`  
65% of all income from viewers (merch 40% + Patreon 9% + AdSense 13%). The shop sells posters, mugs, and bird plushies — all in the signature design. Merch is IP monetization, not algorithm monetization.  
**What it produced:** financial independence from the YouTube algorithm. Even as AdSense dropped 44% over 19 months (2024–2025), the studio remained stable. No EVP, no KKR, no venture capital.  
**What it will give you:** protection against algorithmic changes. Each merch buyer is a direct customer with no platform intermediary. With a recognizable visual IP, launching a shop becomes viable.

> 📝 **NOTE:** The 40% merch figure represents a mature outcome, not a starting condition. Kurzgesagt's shop succeeds because its visual IP (the bird characters, the color palette) is immediately recognizable and deeply affectively associated with the channel. A new channel cannot launch a merch line in Month 1 and expect meaningful revenue. The realistic sequencing: establish a visual signature in Year 1 → test a limited-edition item (poster, print) in Year 2 → build a permanent shop once the design language is stable and the audience is large enough for organic social proof. Skipping straight to a full shop is a common and costly mistake.

## BLOCK IV. TECHNOLOGY & AI

### 21. AI for Topic Generation — One AI-Suggested Video Reached 10M+ Views
**Source:** `[ELECTRIFY / SIMPLICISSIMUS]`  
EVP CEO Ian Shepherd publicly stated: one AI-suggested video in a portfolio channel reached 10M+ views within 3 months. The visual production itself remained handmade.  
**What it produced:** Simplicissimus + EVP — a systematic, data-driven approach to topic selection, replacing intuition with pattern analysis across the full portfolio.  
**What it will give you:** a data-driven editorial process. Instead of guessing topics — analysis of engagement patterns, search demand, and viral triggers. Achievable without EVP using ChatGPT + YouTube Research + Google Trends today.

> 📝 **NOTE:** The claim that a single AI-suggested video reached 10M+ views is a marketing-adjacent statement from a portfolio company CEO and should be treated with appropriate skepticism — it is impossible to isolate the AI suggestion as the causal variable vs. the production quality, timing, thumbnail, or SEO. The more reliable takeaway: AI tools are useful for systematizing what good editors already do intuitively — tracking search volume, identifying underserved questions, spotting topic clusters. Frame it as augmentation of editorial judgment, not replacement of it.

### 22. AI-Assisted Scripting + Manual Final Edit
**Source:** `[WISSENSWERT]` `[BREAKINGLAB]` `[VERITASIUM]`  
Wissenswert: at 3 videos/week, AI scripting is almost certainly used for first drafts. BreakingLab: Jacob Beautemps wrote a dissertation on AI-optimization of educational video. Veritasium: EVP uses a data dashboard for SEO and A/B title testing.  
**What it produced:** for Wissenswert — the ability to sustain 3 videos/week with a team of 3–8. For Veritasium after EVP — growth from 13M to 20.5M subscribers in 2.5 years (+58%).  
**What it will give you:** 3–5x acceleration in scripting. Critical point: AI produces structure and first draft — final voice and facts always come from a human. Wissenswert demonstrates the anti-example: when AI output skips quality control, it destroys reputation.

> 📝 **NOTE:** The Wissenswert attribution here is qualified ("almost certainly") — this is the second instance in the guide where inference is presented near-equivalently to confirmed practice. The genuine lesson from Wissenswert is the opposite: their reputational problems appear to be at least partially downstream of a quality-control failure in the scripting process, whether AI-related or not. Use Wissenswert as a caution, not as a model.

### 25. Process-Transparency Video as Content Strategy
**Source:** `[KURZGESAGT]`  
In 2020, Kurzgesagt published "How to Make a Kurzgesagt Video in 1200 Hours" — full process disclosure. The video reached 12M+ views and became one of the channel's most popular.

> 📝 **NOTE:** This type of meta-content is most effective when published at a moment of genuine audience curiosity — either at a milestone (100K, 1M subscribers), at launch ("here is how we make videos"), or as a direct response to a controversy. **Source:** `[WENDOVER PRODUCTIONS]`

**What it will give you:** algorithmic predictability → higher CTR from subscribers → higher probability of Browse feed placement → a self-reinforcing cycle.

### 28. A Parallel Light-Format Channel (Half as Interesting) as an Algorithmic Lead Magnet
**Source:** `[WENDOVER PRODUCTIONS]`  
Half as Interesting: 5–10 minutes, humorous tone, 6–7 videos/month. Attracts viewers who then migrate to the main Wendover channel. The two channels function as a funnel: the light channel captures a broad audience, the main channel converts them into loyal viewers.

## BLOCK VI. BUSINESS STRUCTURE

### 32. The EVP Model — Outsource Operations, Retain Creative Control
**Source:** `[VERITASIUM]` `[SIMPLICISSIMUS]`  
EVP handles: hiring, logistics, compliance, taxes. The creator retains: equity share and full creative control. Investment flows into the team (Veritasium: costs increased 4x). EVP raised $85M in 2023.  
**What it produced:** Veritasium: +58% subscribers (13M to 20.5M) over 2.5 years. The three most-watched videos in channel history — all made after EVP. Simplicissimus: strategic entry into the EN-language market via fern.  
**What it will give you:** the ability to scale production without building a full company independently. Risk: EVP influences topic selection through its data-driven approach. Appropriate when seeking growth without surrendering creative control.

## BLOCK VII. ANTI-EXAMPLES — WHAT NOT TO DO

### 36. ❌ Clickbait Without Quality Destroys CPM and Reputation Simultaneously
**Source:** `[WISSENSWERT]` — anti-example  
Headlines "Die traurige Wahrheit über X," "Das dunkle Geheimnis," FLIMMO red-rating for Halbwahrheiten and disinformation. AdSense dropped from $3,696/month (Feb 2024) to $2,446–3,351/month (Jan 2026) — minus 30% in two years. JustNero published an exposé video.  
**Lesson:** advertisers pay premium CPMs only to channels with clean reputations. Clickbait maximizes CTR short-term and kills CPM long-term. Wissenswert: 1.2 billion total views, but stagnation and declining revenue.

> 📝 **NOTE:** The Wissenswert case is the most instructive anti-example in the guide and deserves more detailed treatment than it receives here. The channel demonstrates a specific failure mode that is increasingly common: high-frequency production optimized for clicks, progressive reputational erosion through quality shortcuts, and a CPM/advertiser confidence collapse that cannot be reversed without a full editorial reset. The 1.2 billion view figure is a trap — it creates the impression of success while the underlying business health metrics (subscriber growth velocity, CPM trend, brand advertiser confidence) have been declining for 2+ years. Volume is not the same as momentum.

### 38. ❌ No TikTok/Shorts = Missed Growth Wave
**Source:** `[WISSENSWERT]` `[NEO]` `[KURZGESAGT]` — anti-examples

> 📝 **NOTE:** This is the clearest structural gap shared by multiple channels in the analysis and the most actionable. All three cases (Wissenswert, NEO, Kurzgesagt) demonstrate that even technically excellent channels with large audiences are leaving measurable growth on the table. For a new channel, Shorts should be treated not as an afterthought but as a separate editorial product: 60-second distillations of the core insight from each long-form video, published the same day. This requires either a dedicated editor or a templated post-production workflow. The Kurzgesagt case is particularly telling — at 25M subscribers, 0.46%/month is a plateau signal, and Shorts neglect is one of the identifiable causes.

## BLOCK VIII. EDITORIAL PLAYBOOK: "EVERGREEN CORE + NEWS HOOK" FORMULA

> This block is based on analysis of the editorial topic-ideas document for a German-language channel and translates it into systemic principles applicable to any niche.

### 52. The "4-Layer" Structure — A Repeatable Script Template for Every Video
**Source:** `[EDITORIAL PLAYBOOK]` — adapted through Wendover + Veritasium  
Every video is built on the same skeleton, regardless of topic:

| Layer | Timing | Function | Example |
|-------|--------|----------|---------|
| **Hook** | 0:00–0:45 | Viewer's specific pain — a number, a question, a visual | "Why does my apartment cost €1,200 for 50m²?" |
| **Background** | 0:45–7:00 | Historical + structural explanation — "how did we get here" | Zoning, Mietspiegel, interest rates, construction history |
| **Now** | 7:00–9:00 | Connection to this week/month — latest data, decisions, conflicts | New rent index data, Bundestag Mietendeckel vote |
| **Takeaway** | 9:00–10:00 | "Was bedeutet das für dich?" — bills, work, mobility, security | What specifically changes for a Munich renter |

**What it produced:** Wendover has used this structure since 2015 — result: 803M views across 273 videos (~2.9M/video). Veritasium calls it "mystery-reveal": viewer sees the problem → doesn't know the answer → watches to the end.  
**What it will give you:** a repeatable script template that reduces scripting time from zero to template-refinement. With a 2–3 person team, this is the difference between 1 video/2 weeks and 1 video/week.

### 53. Dual Topic Classification: Type A and Type B — Applied to a German Audience
**Source:** `[EDITORIAL PLAYBOOK]` + `[KURZGESAGT]`  
Two clear topic clusters emerge from the document, mapping directly to the framework from item 11:

**Type A — "Universally Worrying"** (personal pain, persistent search demand):
- Housing and rent → "Why is renting an apartment in Germany so expensive?"
- Inflation → "Why did everything suddenly get more expensive?"
- Pflege and aging → "Who will take care of us?"
- Work and AI → "Which jobs are under threat first?"

**Type B — "Everyone Uses It, Nobody Understands It"** (low competition, high shelf life):
- Deutsche Bahn → "Why are the ticket prices so confusing?"
- Autobahn → "Why does Germany have no speed limit?"
- Rhine/Elbe → "How do rivers drive the German economy?"
- Digital Euro → "What changes if the Euro goes digital?"

**What both types deliver:** Type A generates search traffic through personal relevance — people Google "warum ist Miete so hoch." Type B generates sustained Browse traffic through curiosity — the algorithm suggests it as an "interesting fact." The optimal editorial calendar alternates: 2 Type A videos + 1 Type B video per month.

### 54. The "German Lens" Method — Localizing a Global Topic to Personal Experience
**Source:** `[EDITORIAL PLAYBOOK]` — "Was bedeutet das für uns in Deutschland?"  
The final block of every video always answers: *what does this specifically mean for someone living in Germany?* — not abstractly, but through prices, jobs, mobility, health, security.

**Application examples:**
- Topic "Chinese EVs" → "What does this mean for the German auto industry and for you as a car buyer?"
- Topic "Low water levels on the Rhine" → "What does this mean for supermarket prices?"
- Topic "AI in the workplace" → "Which German professions are threatened first?"

**What it produced:** BreakingLab uses the same technique — every video ends with a concrete conclusion applicable to the viewer's life. Result: Instagram 123K (the best social media reach among German-language channels in the series) — the audience shares precisely the final takeaway.  
**What it will give you:** the final block is what viewers forward to a friend. "Look, this explains why our rent is so expensive" — that is organic sharing that cannot be bought.

### 55. Topic Prioritization by Three Production-Reality Criteria
**Source:** `[EDITORIAL PLAYBOOK]` — adapted through production analysis of the series  
Not all topics are equally feasible. Before adding any topic to the editorial calendar, evaluate it on three axes:

| Axis | Question | Low-effort example | High-effort example |
|------|----------|--------------------|---------------------|
| **Production** | How many days will it take? | "Autobahn history" — archives + maps, 5 days | "Deindustrialisierung" — factory visits, interviews, 3–4 weeks |
| **Shelf life** | Will this video age out within a year? | "Warum Mieten hoch sind" — relevant for 5+ years | "New inflation data" — obsolete within a month |
| **Competition** | Has someone already done this better? | "How rivers manage the economy" — almost no one | "AI and Jobs" — hundreds of videos already exist |

**Prioritization rule:** favor topics with *high shelf life + low competition + moderate production cost* — these form the editorial calendar core. Topics with low shelf life (news-reactive) are launched as opportunistic reactive content layered on top of the plan.

**Top-3 topics from the document by this matrix:**
1. **Housing** — high shelf life, high personal pain, moderate production. No dominant competitor with a definitive video.
2. **Deutsche Bahn / Deutschlandticket** — Wendover-type "hidden system," 3–5 year shelf life, low competition in explainer format.
3. **Pflege und Altersgesellschaft** — Type A with growing urgency, 10+ year shelf life, significantly underserved in YouTube format.

### 56. Thumbnail and Title: "Personal Question, Not Topic Description" Formula
**Source:** `[EDITORIAL PLAYBOOK]` — gap resolved through items 30 + 3  
The document describes topics but not titles. This is the critical gap: the YouTube algorithm starts with thumbnail and title — not content.

**Rule:** title = a personal question or shock fact, not a topic description.

| ❌ Topic description (weak) | ✅ Personal question / shock fact (strong) |
|-----------------------------|--------------------------------------------|
| Wohnungsmarkt in Deutschland | Du zahlst 900€ für 40m². Hier ist warum. |
| Geschichte des Autobahns | Warum hat Deutschland kein Tempolimit — und was das über uns aussagt |
| Elektroautos vs. Verbrenner | Deutschland baut das beste Auto der Welt. Und verliert trotzdem. |
| Klimawandel und Hitzewellen | Warum deine Stadt jetzt 4°C heißer ist als 1970 |
| Migrationsrecht in Deutschland | Wer darf bleiben? Das steckt hinter dem deutschen Einwanderungsrecht |

**What it produced:** Veritasium with "This Paradox Splits Smart People 50/50" — 5.9M views. Simplicissimus with "Die mächtigste Familie der Welt" — 1.4M in the first weeks. A title framed as a question creates a cognitive gap — the brain wants to close it by clicking.  
**What it will give you:** 20–40% higher CTR with no change to the video content — a direct algorithmic gain in the first 48 hours after publication.

### 57. Serial Structure for a German-Language Channel — Three Named Blocks
**Source:** `[EDITORIAL PLAYBOOK]` + `[NEO]` — Mapped / Portraits / Places  
The 15 topics in the document naturally group into 3 series. Each series = its own playlist, its own algorithmic traffic stream:

**Series 1: WIRTSCHAFT** *("Hidden Systems of the German Economy")*  
Topics: Inflation, Housing, Deindustrialisierung, Deutsche Bahn, Digital Euro  
Format: 12–18 min, maps + infographics, Wendover-style

**Series 2: GESELLSCHAFT** *("How We Live and Who We Will Become")*  
Topics: Pflege, Migration, Einsamkeit, AI und Arbeit, Home Office  
Format: 10–15 min, studio + location shoots, BreakingLab-style

**Series 3: HEIMAT** *("Germany as a Physical and Cultural Place")*  
Topics: Autobahn, Flüsse als Wirtschaftswege, Hitzewellen in Städten, Fußball und Geld  
Format: 8–12 min, location footage + archive, Tom Scott-style

**What it will give you:** a viewer who watched one WIRTSCHAFT video will receive the next via autoplay — that is session watch time, which the algorithm rewards separately. Named series create return expectations ("when is the next HEIMAT episode?").

### 58. The "News Hook" as a Standalone Production Trigger
**Source:** `[EDITORIAL PLAYBOOK]` + `[WENDOVER]` — "How Israel's Security Failed"  
An evergreen video about housing exists on its own terms — and receives an additional traffic spike each time a news trigger appears (a new Mietspiegel report, a Bundestag vote on Mietendeckel, a construction company crisis).

**Application mechanics:**
- Publish evergreen videos on a planned schedule
- When a news trigger appears — within 48–72 hours publish a **Short/Community post** linking to the main video: "We already explained why — here's the video"
- The algorithm surfaces the older video in search results connected to the news event

**What it produced:** Wendover's D-Day logistics video resurfaces reliably at every anniversary. Simplicissimus's sugar industry lobbying video was re-activated by every subsequent food industry scandal.  
**What it will give you:** evergreen + news trigger = a free "second release" of the video without re-shooting. One video — multiple traffic waves across news cycles.
