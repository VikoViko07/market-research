## 1. Channel Overview

Breaking Lab is a German-language science communication YouTube channel examining current topics through a scientific lens — using studies, experiments, and expert knowledge. The channel is hosted by **Dr. Jacob Beautemps** (born September 24, 1993, Essen) — a physicist, science journalist, PhD (Universität zu Köln, 2024), Spiegel-bestselling author, and keynote speaker. Breaking Lab is **the first channel in our analysis series featuring a live on-camera host**.

The channel is co-produced with **i&u Studios GmbH** (formerly i&u TV Produktion GmbH until September 1, 2025) — one of Germany's leading television production companies with 150 permanent employees, owned since 2019 by investment fund **KKR**. Office: Hohenzollernring 89-93, 50672 Köln.

Channel motto and description: **"Auf Breaking Lab nehme ich aktuelle Themen wissenschaftlich unter die Lupe. Dabei nutze ich Studien, Experimente und das Wissen von Expertinnen und Experten"** ("On Breaking Lab, I examine current topics through a scientific lens using studies, experiments and expert knowledge"). Jacob Beautemps' personal motto adapts an Einstein quote: "If you can't explain something simply, you don't understand it."

## 2. Video Formats and Production

### Format

Breaking Lab is a **studio-based science pop-culture channel with a live on-camera host** — fundamentally distinguishing it from all other channels analyzed in our series (Simplicissimus, NEO, Wissenswert — all voiceover-only). The host appears on screen, conducts experiments, interacts with props, and invites expert guests.

**Structure of a typical video:**
- **Opening hook** (30–90 sec): provocative question or surprising fact — "Is glass actually liquid?", "Are e-scooters really bad for the climate?"
- **Scientific explanation** backed by research and sources (all sources transparently listed in the video description)
- **Experiments / Visualizations** — Jacob frequently conducts physical demonstrations on camera
- **Expert perspectives** — researcher interviews for complex topics like nuclear fusion or AI
- **Summary and conclusion** — Jacob always ends with a concrete takeaway

**Primary thematic formats:**

| Format | Examples | Length |
|--------|---------|--------|
| **Myth-busting** | "Are aluminium deodorants really dangerous?", "Is steel more elastic than rubber?" | 8–20 min |
| **Future technologies** | "Can hydrogen save us?", "How does nuclear fusion work?" | 15–30 min |
| **AI & Digital** | "Does AI really make us more creative?", "How do we learn with YouTube?" | 10–20 min |
| **Climate & Sustainability** | "Can we stop climate change by planting trees?", "Can we adapt to climate change?" | 10–25 min |
| **Physics of Everyday Life** | "The heaviest object in the world," "The rarest element" | 8–15 min |
| **Livestreams** | YouTopia, Leibniz-Forschungsmuseen series | 60–180 min |

Average video length: **10–20 minutes** — moderate compared to peers: longer than Wissenswert (8–15 min), shorter than Simplicissimus (20–66 min).

### Editing and Visual Style

Breaking Lab operates with a **studio format backed by professional TV production from i&u Studios**. This is not a self-produced YouTube project but a broadcast-quality product.

**Technical characteristics:**
- Studio shoot in Cologne (i&u Studios): professional lighting, multiple cameras, studio background
- Location shoots: laboratories, museums (Leibniz-Forschungsmuseen), outdoor environments
- Infographics and animation: moderate, used to explain complex concepts (heat pumps, nuclear fusion)
- Editing: dynamic with quick cuts in active segments, slower pacing in explanatory passages

**Team (from the YouTube channel description):**

| Role | Members |
|------|---------|
| Editorial | Jacob Beautemps, Jonasz Gula, Sophia Klein, Annika Munkler, Tabea Desch, Max Esser, Johannes Polotzek, Anna Schreiber, Yvonne Zißer, Annick Goergen |

Total: **~15 people** in the editorial-production team. Significantly smaller than Simplicissimus (60+) but larger than NEO (1–3). All staff work within the i&u Studios GmbH framework.

### Production Workflow and Timelines

The regular schedule of 2 videos/week (Tuesday + Thursday, 6:00 PM) requires a tight editorial calendar. With a team of ~15 and 10–20 minute videos, an estimated single-video production cycle runs:

- Monday–Tuesday: topic → research (editorial team)
- Tuesday–Wednesday: script, review with Jacob, source verification
- Wednesday–Thursday: filming (one day in studio + possible location visit)
- Thursday–Friday: rough edit
- Friday–Monday: final edit, animation, fact-check, SEO packaging
- Publication: Tuesday or Thursday at 6:00 PM

Cycle: **5–8 working days per video** — faster than Simplicissimus (4–8 weeks) due to simpler animation requirements.

## 3. Topics and Content

## 4. Visual Identity and Technology

**Branding:** logo — laboratory flask, blue color scheme. The name Breaking Lab is a wordplay: "breaking news" + "lab" (laboratory). Thumbnails: Jacob Beautemps' face with an amazed or intrigued expression + large text + a scientific object.

**Thumbnail strategy:** a human face with emotion is one of the most effective CTR triggers on YouTube. Breaking Lab leverages this fully. Unlike Wissenswert (shock clickbait), the emotional expression here corresponds to the actual content — this is "honest emotional thumbnailing."

**SEO and algorithm:** Breaking Lab sits at the intersection of search and recommendation traffic. Topics like "Wärmepumpe" (a politically charged topic in Germany during the 2023–2024 energy debate), "KI," or "Kernfusion" have high search demand. The "live face + emotion" format also performs well in Browse (YouTube's recommendation feed).

**The host's research background as an SEO asset:** Jacob Beautemps wrote his doctoral dissertation on "how students best learn from YouTube videos" and developed 17 rules for a successful science video. This is unique — the channel host simultaneously conducts scientific research into what makes such a channel effective, directly influencing every video's structure.

**AI usage:** Jacob Beautemps has publicly discussed researching AI for educational videos. He stated plans to develop an AI system that "analyzes viewing behavior and learning gains" when watching videos, creating a catalog of what makes a good educational video. AI-assisted research tools are almost certainly already in use.

## 6. Why the Channel Is Popular

Breaking Lab grew from several parallel factors.

**Timing:** the channel launched in 2018, when the niche of "science explained by someone who actually knows it" in German YouTube was nearly empty. maiLab existed but focused on chemistry. Breaking Lab occupied the broad-science niche.

**COVID-19 algorithmic boost:** "Wir gegen Corona" in March 2020 — with federal politicians — elevated Breaking Lab to a new level of public recognition. The channel became a "trusted source" at a moment of maximum media anxiety.

**"Positive" science in a skeptical country:** Germany has traditionally high levels of fear around heat pumps, nuclear energy, and GMOs. Breaking Lab provides a scientifically grounded counterweight — "here's what the research actually says." This is a valued social function.

**Audience:** 16–35 years old, educated, interested in technology and science. The same demographic as Simplicissimus, but with different value orientation — not "dark truth about the world" but "reasons for optimism."

## 12. Systemic Risk Analysis

### Structural Risks

**1. Single-host dependency (critical)**
Breaking Lab = Jacob Beautemps. This is the fundamental structural risk of all creator channels with a live presenter. Illness, sabbatical, life changes, creative burnout — everything stops production. Furthermore, the channel belongs legally to i&u Studios, not Jacob — in the event of a relationship breakdown, an acute conflict over brand control is possible.

**2. i&u / KKR corporate partnership (high)**
i&u Studios is owned by KKR — a PE fund with a planned exit horizon. A potential sale of i&u Studios to a new owner could change the strategy for digital channels. Beautemps does not fully own his channel — this fundamentally limits his negotiating position.

**3. Competition from public media (medium)**
maiLab/MAITHINK X (ZDF Neo), Terra X, funk, Quarks — all operate with near-unlimited budgets. For them, YouTube is not the core business, meaning they can invest without direct ROI requirements. Breaking Lab must compete in this environment as a commercial operation.

**4. German-language audience ceiling (medium)**
~130M German speakers — against NEO's global market. At current growth rates (+100–200K subscribers/year), Breaking Lab will reach 1 million in 2026–2027. The realistic ceiling for high-quality German science content is 2–3M — far below Simplicissimus (2.42M in an adjacent niche).

**5. TikTok/Shorts — missed opportunity (medium)**
Breaking Lab content is ideal for Shorts: quick experiments, surprising facts, live face. The absence of a TikTok strategy is a structural gap that competing channels (maiLab is more active on social media) are already exploiting.

**6. Host burnout (low-medium)**
2 videos/week + keynote tours + TV shoots + YouTopia + book + academic career — this is an exceptionally high workload for one person. In 2025, Jacob simultaneously completed his PhD and published a bestseller. Sustaining this pace long-term without burnout is a genuine concern.

## 13. Future Plans and AI

### Strategic Vectors

**1. Scaling the personal brand**
The book "Unsere Zukunft neu denken" (2025) signals a transition from "YouTuber" to "public intellectual." Jacob Beautemps is building a career where YouTube is one of several platforms (alongside TV, books, keynotes). This is a smart diversification that reduces dependence on YouTube's algorithm.

**2. International expansion**
Breaking Lab currently operates exclusively in German. A logical next step would be English subtitles or a separate English-language channel for global reach. i&u Studios could support this through their international format experience.

**3. YouTube vs. ARD/ZDF rebalancing**
Since 2022, ARD Mediathek (Science for Future) and ZDF Neo (indirectly through competition with maiLab) have become increasingly important platforms. i&u Studios' 2025 rebranding explicitly moves toward crossmedial content. Breaking Lab may evolve from "YouTube channel with TV backup" to "TV format with YouTube distribution."

### AI in the Roadmap

This is a unique case: **the Breaking Lab host personally researches AI for education**.

Jacob Beautemps' dissertation (2024) focused on "how adults learn with YouTube videos" and developing an AI system to optimize educational videos. He has publicly stated plans to create AI that analyzes "viewing behavior and learning gains" to improve content quality.

**What this means for Breaking Lab:**
- Breaking Lab will be among the first channels with data-driven content optimization based on a proprietary AI system
- Video structure (hooks, pacing, timing of introducing complex concepts) will be optimized based on real retention data
- Jacob Beautemps is arguably the most academically equipped person in German YouTube to apply AI to educational video content

## Summary

Breaking Lab is a **model of sustainable science-communication YouTube with maximally diversified revenue**: multiple income sources, a strong personal host brand, academic credibility, and TV backing through i&u Studios.

**Key structural takeaways:**

1. **Jacob Beautemps is the most "complete" media professional** in our series: physics PhD, TV guest, keynote speaker, bestselling author, YouTube host, and YouTube pedagogy researcher simultaneously. This creates exceptional protection against algorithmic risks.

2. **i&u Studios is a dual asset.** TV-quality production and institutional backing are genuine strengths. But KKR ownership and the corporate structure limit creative flexibility and Jacob's negotiating position.

3. **Scaling remains an unresolved challenge.** 724K subscribers is an excellent result for a science channel at this quality standard. But the path to 2M+ runs into the German-language ceiling and the absence of a TikTok/Shorts strategy.

4. **YouTopia as a unique competitive moat.** No one else in German YouTube has created a multi-day live-media-charity-event with politicians, scientists, and celebrities. This narrative asset is not replicable.

5. **AI research as a strategic edge.** Jacob Beautemps is the only YouTuber in our series who has defended a dissertation on YouTube pedagogy. His own AI system for educational video optimization could give Breaking Lab a structural quality advantage over competitors.
