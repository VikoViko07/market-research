## Who Is TVOne

**TVOne** is Indonesia's largest news television broadcaster, founded in 2008. Owned by **Bakrie Group** through holding company Visi Media Asia (VIVA). Part of the same media group as the news portal **viva.co.id**. Annual revenue ~$19.6M (2025). Headquartered in East Jakarta.

Positioning: *"Memang Beda"* ("Truly Different") — focused on news, sports, and current affairs analysis.

---

## AI Timeline

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| **April 2023** | Launch of Indonesia's first AI avatar anchor — a digital clone of Editor-in-Chief **Karni Ilyas**. First of its kind in ASEAN |
| **April 2023** | Launch of two additional digital presenters — **Sasya** and **Nadira** (female avatars targeting Gen Z audiences on Instagram and TikTok) |
| **July 2023** | CEO Taufan Eko Nugroho at **The Convergence Indonesia** conference announces machine learning integration for ratings analysis |
| **May 2024** | Launch of **tvOne.ai** — Indonesia's first AI news portal with a chat interface for searching and summarising news |
| **2024** | Partnership with **Pilkada.AI** for AI-assisted coverage of regional elections |

---

## Strengths

✅ **First in ASEAN** — TVOne was the first mainstream TV broadcaster in Southeast Asia to launch an AI news anchor. Their only global peers at the time: India and China (Xinhua).

✅ **Systemic approach, not a PR stunt** — they did not stop at one format. Avatars for social media + AI news portal + machine learning for ratings analysis = genuinely integrated AI adoption across multiple functions.

✅ **Clear audience targeting** — avatars Sasya and Nadira were deliberately launched on Instagram and TikTok for Gen Z. Strategically sound: TikTok grew from 11% to 34% as a news source among Indonesian youth between 2021 and 2025.

✅ **tvOne.ai as a real product** — a portal with a chat bar where readers ask questions and receive news summaries drawn from verified sources (viva.co.id + tvonenews.com). A concrete D2C product, not just an experiment.

✅ **Academic recognition** — their case is studied at multiple Indonesian universities and was included in the **Wiley Handbook of Artificial Intelligence and Journalism** (2025) — the only Indonesian media case in that international publication.

✅ **Ecosystem partnerships** — collaboration with Pilkada.AI demonstrates willingness to integrate external AI startups rather than only build in-house.

---

## Weak Points

❌ **Avatar quality is inconsistent** — the CEO publicly acknowledged that voice-to-image synchronisation is still imperfect. The gap between the real presenter and the digital double is visible.

❌ **No transparency on the technology stack** — it is unknown which models power tvOne.ai and the avatars. Could be third-party solutions (HeyGen, D-ID) or proprietary development — not publicly disclosed. Critical for assessing scalability.

❌ **AI portal has no monetisation model** — tvOne.ai exists as an engagement tool, but no public monetisation mechanism has been announced (subscriptions, ads inside AI answers, B2B API).

❌ **Difficult operating environment** — Indonesian media are losing advertising revenue to Meta, Google, and ByteDance. Public trust in news fell to 36% in 2025. TVOne operates under significant economic pressure.

❌ **No international presence** — despite being genuine pioneers, TVOne is absent from global AI-in-journalism platforms: JournalismAI Festival, INMA Global Media Awards, WAN-IFRA World News Media Congress.

❌ **Hallucination risk in the chat portal** — tvOne.ai generates summaries from only two sources (viva.co.id and tvonenews.com), but the internal fact-verification mechanism is not publicly described.

## What Is Interesting

🔍 **Cloning the real editor-in-chief** as an AI avatar was a bold move that immediately raised questions of trust and transparency. TVOne's response was correct: they opened every broadcast with a clear disclosure — *"the person you see is an avatar."* This matters in the context of the EU AI Act and global synthetic media debates.

🔍 **The Pilkada.AI partnership** is an example of a traditional broadcaster becoming a distribution platform for a specialised AI product. This is a business model very few media organisations have tried.

🔍 **Restricting tvOne.ai to verified in-house sources only** is the right architectural instinct for fighting hallucinations. The implementation is not yet transparent, but the principle is sound.

## 1. Monetisation and Advertising — The Real Picture

### Indonesia's ad market: large, but not for TVOne

Indonesia is one of Southeast Asia's largest advertising markets. In 2025 total ad spend reached **$6.4 billion** (GroupM), with **75% going to digital**. Television accounts for only **16%** of total spend — roughly $1 billion split across all broadcasters in the country.

The problem:

| Platform | Market share |
|---|---|
| Google (Search + YouTube) | ~35% |
| Meta (Instagram + Facebook) | ~15% |
| TikTok (ByteDance) | ~8% |
| **Three global platforms combined** | **~58% of total digital market** |

News media get an ever-shrinking slice. The Reuters Institute 2025 report notes that **government advertising — historically a major revenue source for Indonesian media — collapsed** following budget cuts by President Prabowo Subianto's administration in 2024. This directly hit TVOne.

### How TVOne currently earns revenue

**Active revenue streams:**
- 📺 **Broadcast TV advertising** — traditional commercial spots. Historically the primary source. Now under pressure as audiences migrate online.
- 💻 **Digital display advertising** on tvonenews.com — Google AdSense + Adswizz (confirmed in their tech stack), programmatic.
- 📱 **Social media monetisation** — YouTube Partner Program revenue; possibly TikTok Creator Fund for the tvOne.ai account.
- 🤝 **Partnership projects** — such as Pilkada.AI, where an external AI startup pays for distribution reach through TVOne's broadcast.

**What is absent:**
- ❌ Subscription model (paywall, premium newsletter)
- ❌ Monetisation of the tvOne.ai portal
- ❌ B2B API or content licensing
- ❌ Events business (conferences, summits)

### The core problem

Digital video advertising is growing fast (34% share of all digital ad spend in 2025), and **short-form video is the fastest-growing segment** (projected $528M by 2029). TVOne produces the right content format, but monetisation flows through the platforms — not directly to the publisher. As the managing editor of the Jakarta Globe put it: *"Advertisers gain exposure from viral news stories, but they don't pay the journalists who create them. Instead, they pay the platforms."*

## 2. The Editorial Day: How It Works

No public description of TVOne's internal workflow exists. However, from academic research (Wiley Handbook 2025, IAFOR 2024) a picture can be reconstructed.

### Top-down implementation

The Wiley study describes TVOne as having a **top-down AI adoption strategy** — leadership (the CEO) makes decisions, journalists adapt. This contrasts with Narasi, another Indonesian outlet, where AI adoption was driven bottom-up by journalists themselves.

**The consequence:** TVOne's AI integration is described as more "symbolic" — AI is used for visible external products (avatars, portal) but deep integration into the daily editorial workflow remains limited.

### How journalists use AI at TVOne

From IAFOR research (2024) based on interviews with journalists at Indonesian TV broadcasters including TVOne:

- ✅ **ChatGPT** — for ideation and first drafts. But journalists report frustration: it struggles to adapt to the nuanced requirements of specific news segments and requires significant human editing.
- ✅ **ML for ratings analysis** — the CEO confirmed: daily ratings and audience share data are fed into an in-house machine learning system.
- ✅ **Avatars** — operate autonomously on Instagram and TikTok without journalist involvement at the moment of publication.
- ✅ **tvOne.ai** — for news summarisation, but positioned as a reader-facing tool, not an internal editorial tool.

**Journalists' view on AI:**
> *"AI is convenient, but it cannot replace the role of a journalist"* — the consistent consensus from interviews. Especially in breaking news and live coverage, where empathy and interpretation are required.

### How stories are selected

No public data exists on TVOne's editorial selection process. Based on context:
- Focus on politics, economics, and sport — standard priorities for a news broadcaster
- Avatars Sasya and Nadira deliver a daily digest — meaning selection is done by the editorial team, the avatar only narrates
- tvOne.ai generates answers exclusively from verified content across the two group portals (viva.co.id + tvonenews.com) — a built-in filter by default

## 3. Newsletters

**Short answer: TVOne has no public newsletter.**

This is a significant gap — but one that requires cultural context. In Indonesia, the newsletter habit simply does not exist the way it does in Europe or the US. The main news distribution channels in the country are WhatsApp (43%), YouTube (41%), Facebook (39%), and TikTok (34%). Email newsletters do not feature as a meaningful channel.

**What this means:**
The Indonesian audience consumes news differently from European audiences. The functional equivalent of the newsletter in Indonesia is the **WhatsApp broadcast list and Telegram channel**. Several Indonesian media organisations already use WhatsApp Business API to send morning digests to subscribers.

**What TVOne could do:**
- A WhatsApp digest with 3–5 morning headlines + link to full video
- A Telegram channel for a more engaged, deeper-reading audience
- Email only for B2B purposes (advertisers, commercial partners)

## 4. Forward Plans

No formally announced strategic plan for 2025–2026 exists. Based on CEO public statements and academic analysis, the likely direction is:

**Already in motion:**
- Avatar quality improvements — the CEO committed to showing visible progress every six months
- tvOne.ai as a permanent entry point for time-poor audiences
- Topic-specific AI partnerships (elections model proven; sports and finance are logical next steps)

**Logical next steps (not yet announced):**
- **Regional languages** — Javanese and Sundanese each have 100M+ speakers. No major broadcaster has yet launched an AI avatar in either language.
- **Audio formats** — podcasts and AI-narrated audio for WhatsApp/Telegram. Indonesia's podcast market is growing at 7.4% annually with high listener retention.
- **tvOne.ai monetisation** — branded answers (advertising inside AI responses), a B2B version for corporate clients.

---

## 5. Why Indonesian Media Are Moving Into AI So Fast

The paradoxical answer: **crisis, not prosperity.**

- 1,200 media workers laid off in 2023–2024
- Government advertising collapsed after federal budget cuts
- Private advertising migrating to Meta, Google, TikTok
- Public trust in news down to 36% in 2025

AI at TVOne is not a luxury innovation — it is **cost-cutting dressed as innovation**. One avatar replaces some of the shift presenters. One AI portal replaces some of the digest editors. This is the honest context for understanding their motivation.

## 6. What This Means for Someone Building Their Own Solution

| Observation | Takeaway |
|---|---|
| Top-down AI = shallow integration | Any solution must be designed for journalists, not imposed from above |
| No AI product monetisation | Built-in monetisation from day one is a competitive advantage |
| No newsletter / WhatsApp channel | In Asian markets, messenger-based distribution matters more than email |
| Avatars without a news workflow | Avatar + automated text-to-video pipeline from CMS = a gap no player in the region has closed |
| Indonesian language only | Regional languages (Javanese, Sundanese) are an unclaimed niche with enormous reach |
| Crisis as an AI driver | Any solution must demonstrably reduce operational costs, not only improve quality |

---

## Summary

TVOne is the boldest AI experimenter among Asian news broadcasters. They were the first in ASEAN to do what European media still only discuss at conferences: launch a real AI anchor in a live feed, build an AI news portal, and embed machine learning into editorial operations.

Their weakness is not in ideas but in depth of execution and international visibility. The technology stack is opaque, monetisation is not built, and they are virtually unknown on the global stage. The context is harsh — Indonesian media are losing both advertising and audiences simultaneously.

**For someone building their own solution:** TVOne is valuable as **proof of demand** for AI anchors and AI news portals in high-mobile, developing-market audiences. Their mistakes — opaque tech stack, no AI product monetisation, no international narrative — are a checklist of what to solve from the very beginning.
