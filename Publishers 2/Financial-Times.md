## 1. Context — The Best Subscription Story in Business Media

Financial Times in March 2026 is one of two or three publishers in the world where a paid digital subscription functions as the foundation of the business — not a supplement to advertising. This is not luck. It is the result of 25 years of decisions that went against the market trend at the moment each one was made.

The honest version of this history includes the pain, not just the result. In 2002, when the entire internet was free, FT put up a paywall. For the first eight years the model looked wrong by contemporary standards: by 2010 FT had roughly 207,000 digital subscribers at around £200 per year — a number that looked embarrassing against the traffic volumes Google and Facebook were generating for free. Most observers at the time said FT was making a mistake.

In 2009, as the rest of the industry chased digital reach, CEO John Ridding articulated the strategy that has not changed since: *"We want to be out of the volume game. The volume game is dominated by digital natives like Google and Facebook. Our money is in the direct relationship with the content reader."*

The editorial trade-offs were real and deliberate. No viral content optimisation. No SEO for broad audiences. No platform integration for reach. Every decision filtered through one question: does this help the reader make better financial and business decisions?

The reason FT could be wrong for eight years and survive is that Nikkei — an employee-owned Japanese company without external shareholder pressure — provided patient capital. That patience is the actual competitive moat, not just the technology decisions. Most media owners would have abandoned the paywall model by 2007.

In 2014 FT invented the RFV metric. In 2019 it reached 1 million digital subscribers, a year ahead of plan. In 2023 it posted over half a billion pounds in revenue for the first time in its history. In 2024 — a record £540M.

## 2. FT Group Structure

FT is not one newspaper. It is a holding of five interconnected businesses that together maximise Global Paying Audience across different price points, geographies, and reader types.

**Core:** FT.com (flagship newspaper and website), FT Weekend, FT Print

**FT Specialist** — 17 niche B2B publications including The Banker, Investors' Chronicle, Pensions Expert, Professional Wealth Management, The Fund Strategy, Endpoints News (biopharma, acquired 2023). Revenue +11% YoY, audience +18% YoY. Each brand serves a narrow but highly solvent professional audience at high ARPU — FT's version of a vertical media strategy, executed at quality rather than volume.

**FT Professional** — corporate subscriptions to FT.com and company services. Growing +12% YoY. Major emerging source of new clients: India, where FT has found what Ridding described as *"the right formula"* — an institutional rather than retail market, with significant subscription agreements with large globally oriented Indian organisations.

**FT Live** — events and conferences. Over 200 events per year. Revenue £37.3M (+11%). Operating profit on events +29% YoY. Key events: Global Banking Summit, Future of the Car, Future of Asset Management North America, Business of Luxury Summit. The strategic loop that most analyses miss: events bring senior executives to the stage, FT journalists extract exclusives from those conversations, those stories justify subscription renewals, renewals fund the next round of events. FT Live is not a separate business — it is part of the editorial machine.

**FT Strategies** — media consulting for other publishers. 800+ clients in 60+ countries. Revenue £15M+. Partnership with Google News Initiative. Launched AI Launchpad in 2025. Unusual competitive asset: competitors study FT case studies at business schools — FT monetises that directly. Important risk to note: a significant portion of FT Strategies revenue flows through the GNI partnership, meaning a Google policy change on GNI terms would materially contract this business line.

**FT Ventures** — investment fund launched 2024. Early investments include a future-of-work company and The Logic (Canadian business media). FT's mechanism for accessing technology before it reaches the open market.

**FT Chinese** — Chinese-language version for Chinese-speaking audiences.

**Longitude** — B2B thought leadership and research for corporate clients (not for individual readers).

## 3. How FT Thinks as a Publisher

### 3.1 Quality of Reader, Not Quantity

The strategy articulated in 2009 has remained operationally consistent: *"We want to be out of the volume game."*

The visible trade-offs are real, not rhetorical. FT's monthly unique browser numbers are a fraction of BuzzFeed or even The Guardian. FT's social media following is unremarkable for a publication of its prestige. FT does not publish sponsored listicles. These are not omissions — they are deliberate structural choices that make £400 ARPU possible. Every decision is filtered through one question: does this help the reader make better financial and business decisions?

### 3.2 North Star Metric — GPA, Not Subscribers

In 2023 FT introduced Global Paying Audience (GPA) as its new North Star metric, replacing the simple subscriber count. GPA counts everyone who has paid for any FT Group product: FT.com, FT Specialist, FT Chinese, FT Live events, services and apps.

The strategic implication is important: FT is no longer maximising subscribers to one newspaper — it is maximising the paying audience of an ecosystem. This is the correct shift for a group with five distinct revenue streams at different price points. The 3 million GPA target by 2028 implies continuing growth from 2.83 million in 2024 — achievable but requiring acceleration, as the pace of new subscriber addition is visibly decelerating.

### 3.3 RFV — The Loyalty System That Changed the Industry

In 2014 FT developed the RFV metric (Recency, Frequency, Volume):

- **Recency** — how recently the reader opened FT
- **Frequency** — how often they read
- **Volume** — how much they read per session

RFV calculates an engagement score for each subscriber. It is a predictive model: RFV correlates strongly with renewal probability, conversion from trial to paid, and lifetime value.

Practical application: FT proactively works with "at-risk" subscribers (low RFV) — targeted offers, personalised recommendations, re-engagement campaigns — before they cancel. This drives churn significantly below the industry average of 4–5%.

Today RFV is used by hundreds of publishers worldwide. FT shared the methodology through FT Strategies. This is strategic generosity: the more publishers adopt RFV, the more FT appears as the industry standard and the more clients FT Strategies attracts. It is a commercial flywheel, not philanthropy.

**Operational metrics from FT Strategies (January 2025) that rarely appear in public analyses:**
- Involuntary churn recovery: 66% — achieved through payment retry logic, card updater tools, and timing optimisation
- Save rate in win-back flow: 6% (up from 3%)
- The FT model calculates that a 2 percentage point reduction in churn has approximately three times the lifetime value impact of an equivalent increase in conversion rate — which is why retention is operationally prioritised over acquisition

## 4. The Editorial Kitchen — How It Actually Works

### 4.1 The Daily Editorial Cycle

**Morning:** Audience Engagement Team — a dedicated unit modelled on FT's approach and now copied by Nikkei for its Japanese newsrooms — reports overnight data. Metrics: "Quality Reads" (percentage of an article actually read, not just clicked), RFV movement by reader segment, conversion from registered to paying.

**Editorial meeting:** Topics are evaluated not only by news importance but by audience relevance. Four reader archetypes shape prioritisation:

| Archetype | Characteristic | Editorial response |
|---|---|---|
| The Analyst | Deep reading, long sessions, broad perspective | Long analytical pieces, investigations |
| The Global Manager | Business decisions, time-sensitive | Briefings, FT Professional, alerts |
| The Competitive Specialist | Professional niche, high ARPU | FT Specialist — The Banker, Investors' Chronicle |
| The Political Insider | Power, regulation, geopolitics | Political reporting, FT Professional |

**Format:** text primary, audio growing (podcasts), video through FT Live events rather than through an autonomous video studio. FT is a deliberately non-video-oriented publisher — a strategic choice with a growing risk profile addressed in the weaknesses section.

**AI layer:** AI Summaries (on-request, transparently labelled), AI Playground (internal), Ask FT (for subscribers), dataset analysis tools for investigative reporting.

### 4.2 The Transparency Principle in AI

FT's position on AI differentiates it clearly from Axel Springer (Politico arbitration scandal where AI summaries published without review contained factual errors) and Washington Post (AI podcast launched with known quality problems).

Liz Lohn, Director of Product, AI and Editorial Tech: *"We will never risk reader trust by using AI to create reader-facing content until we are confident in quality after sufficient testing."*

The concrete mechanism: AI summaries are available via an on-article button — the reader actively chooses to see them. The button is green and explicitly labelled "AI-drafted, edited by FT journalist." This is trust design, not engagement design. The distinction matters commercially: FT's £400 ARPU depends on readers trusting every piece of content. Anything that erodes that trust erodes ARPU. Engagement design optimises for clicks; trust design optimises for the long-term subscriber relationship.

### 4.3 What Is and Is Not Automated

| Layer | Tool | Status |
|---|---|---|
| AI article summaries | On-button, labelled "AI-drafted, edited by journalist" | Production — transparent |
| AI in investigative reporting | Dataset analysis (who are the largest UK landlords?) | Active |
| Ask FT | Semantic search + LLM over archive from 1888 | Limited beta → early access |
| AI Playground | Internal tool for journalists (AccelerateAI team) | Production — tens of users |

| Personalisation | RFV-based recommendations | Production |
| Transcription | AI tools | Active |

| AI-written articles | Never | Hard prohibition |

## 6. Monetisation — How FT Revenue Is Built

### 6.1 Five Revenue Streams

**1. Consumer subscriptions (individual) — £110M, ARPU ~£400/year.** Individual subscribers make up approximately 20% of total paying audience by headcount, but they pay at full market rate. Three tiers: FT.com Standard, FT.com Premium, FT Professional.

**2. Corporate subscriptions — FT Professional, +12% YoY.** The majority of the paying audience by headcount is corporate. Banks, consulting firms, law firms, government organisations. India is a rapidly growing new institutional market.

**3. FT Specialist — 17 B2B niche titles, +11% YoY revenue, +18% YoY audience.** The Banker, Investors' Chronicle, Pensions Expert, Endpoints News. Each brand is a high-margin niche with a narrow but very solvent professional audience — FT's version of BurdaForward's vertical strategy, but at higher ARPU and lower advertising dependency.

**4. FT Live — events, £37.3M, +11%.** 200+ events per year. Operating profit on events +29% YoY. The strategic loop: events bring CEO and CFO speakers to the stage, FT journalists break news from those conversations, those stories justify subscription renewals. Events are part of the editorial machine, not a separate business.

**5. FT Strategies — consulting, £15M+.** 800+ media companies in 60+ countries. AI Launchpad product launched 2025. Partial dependency on Google News Initiative is a risk that must be noted alongside the revenue figure.

### 6.2 Revenue Structure — Why This Matters

| Stream | Share | Trend |
|---|---|---|
| Reader revenue (all formats) | ~55–60% | Growing |
| Advertising | ~25–30% | Stable — best year since 2012 |
| FT Live events | ~7% | Growing |
| FT Strategies + other | ~5–8% | Growing |

For comparison: BurdaForward advertising ~95%, Washington Post advertising ~70–75%. FT's reader revenue majority is not an aspiration — it is the operational reality that makes FT structurally resistant to the advertising market cycles that are damaging every other publisher in this analysis. When the German display market falls or US programmatic CPMs decline, FT's core economics are largely insulated.

### 6.3 Why Retention Is Operationally Prioritised Over Acquisition

George Montagu, Subscriptions Strategy Lead, FT Strategies: *"Content companies have extremely high churn — typically 4–5% — compared to other subscription businesses. Spotify, for example, has maximum churn of 1.7%. Churn must be a strategic priority for almost all publishers — as opposed to acquisition."*

The underlying maths: a 2 percentage point reduction in churn has approximately three times the lifetime value impact of an equivalent increase in conversion rate. FT's involuntary churn recovery of 66% — through payment retry logic, card updater services, and timing optimisation — is the unglamorous operational machinery behind the subscriber numbers that everyone admires but few replicate.

## 7. Conferences 2023–2026 — Three Distinct Roles

**Role 1 — As a journalist:** FT editors and reporters speak at external forums, shape industry narratives, and gather sources. Editorial function.

**Role 2 — As an organiser:** FT Live runs 200+ events per year as a commercial business that simultaneously feeds the newsroom with exclusives. Global Banking Summit, Future of the Car, Future of Asset Management North America, Business of Luxury Summit.

**Role 3 — As a consultant:** FT Strategies runs its own forums for the media industry. AI Launchpad is the newest. These are B2B events for other publishers, not for FT readers.

These are different audiences, different revenue models, different strategic goals. Conflating them misrepresents how FT actually uses the conference channel.

## 8. Strengths

**The best subscription model in the industry — with 25 years of proof.** £540M revenue, £41M operating profit, ARPU £400, reader revenue ~55–60%. These numbers are 2024 actuals that competitors treat as targets for 2030. The paywall was installed in 2002. The first eight years looked wrong. The next 17 years proved the decision correct.

**RFV as an exportable industry standard.** FT did not just build the best engagement metric in media — it monetised it through FT Strategies. When competitors implement RFV, they pay FT to teach them how. This is a flywheel that simultaneously generates consulting revenue and reinforces FT as the benchmark that others measure themselves against.

**Cookie-independent first-party advertising stack.** FT's verified subscriber identity database is a structural CPM advantage that compounds in value every year as third-party cookies disappear. Financial services advertisers pay premium rates for verified professional audiences. Most publishers cannot offer this. FT can.

**FT Live — events as an editorial machine, not just revenue.** The strategic loop is closed: events generate exclusives, exclusives justify subscriptions, subscriptions fund events. £37.3M revenue and +29% operating profit are the financial results of what is fundamentally an editorial investment.

**Ask FT — architecturally correct AI product.** Semantic search over the archive from 1888. Answers grounded exclusively in verified FT reporting, with no external hallucination risk. The correct architecture for a financial audience that makes decisions based on data — the only AI product in this space that structurally cannot fabricate from non-FT sources. Currently in limited beta; the architecture is right even if the pace is slow.

**OpenAI deal on FT's terms.** Ridding: *"It's right, of course, that AI platforms pay publishers for the use of their material. OpenAI understands the importance of transparency, attribution, and compensation."* FT negotiated from strength — attribution in ChatGPT responses, compensation, and being inside the development cycle as these products take shape. This is a commercial transaction, not value extraction.

**FT Strategies — B2B consulting as a second business.** Competitors study FT case studies at business schools. FT charges them for the privilege, directly. 800+ clients, AI Launchpad product, Google partnership. The underlying asset — FT's accumulated expertise — is durable even if the GNI dependency creates concentration risk.

**Editorial independence from Nikkei — eight years of clean record.** Zero publicly documented editorial interference in eight years. This contrasts directly with Washington Post (Bezos blocks presidential endorsement, loses 300,000+ subscribers in weeks) and Axel Springer (Döpfner sends political instructions to Bild editors). The absence of this problem is an active competitive advantage in subscriber trust and ARPU maintenance.

## 9. Weaknesses

### 9.1 No Video Strategy — Deliberate Choice With Growing Generational Risk

FT is not a video-oriented publisher. No SSAI video player. No AI video production tool comparable to Schibsted's Videofy or BurdaForward's Magic AI Tool. This is a strategic decision — the FT audience reads and listens rather than watches, and the current subscriber base justifies it.

The generational risk is real. FT Edit (a free app offering eight articles per day) is the product FT built to reach 25–35 year olds — but it is a text product. Audiences under 35 consume financial and business information increasingly through video. The current subscriber base of predominantly 40–55 year olds supports the text-first model today. Over a 10-year horizon, the absence of any video funnel for future subscribers becomes a structural problem. FT Edit is a correct first step — it does not resolve the video gap.

### 9.2 Slow AI Iteration — Caution With a Competitive Cost

The internal AI Playground reaches tens of users — not hundreds. The newsletter automation experiment failed: Liz Lohn at INMA: *"We didn't get the effect we wanted."* Ask FT remains in limited beta in March 2026, more than two years after internal development began.

By comparison: Axel Springer's Hey_ chatbot reaches millions of users. Schibsted's Aftonbladet chatbot answered 600,000 questions about the US election in production. Bloomberg Terminal AI is in production for financial professionals.

The FT philosophy of "test privately, launch only when confident" is correct for trust preservation — FT's trust premium is the asset that makes £400 ARPU possible. But at the current pace it risks losing the AI reader product window before Bloomberg and AI-native competitors fully occupy the verified-answers-for-finance niche.

### 9.3 Decelerating Subscriber Growth

FT added 181,699 digital readers in 2023, 126,162 in 2022, and the trajectory is decelerating. The GPA target of 3 million by 2028 is achievable, but requires either re-acceleration in established markets (UK, USA) or faster scaling in new ones (India, Southeast Asia). The trajectory as of March 2026 suggests the 2028 target may require revision if the current pace continues.

### 9.4 No Bundling Product

An internal FT Strategies publication states: *"Bundling is a wonderful way to increase ARPU and retention. We need to learn from Netflix, Amazon, Disney."* Yet FT itself does not do aggressive bundling. FT.com + FT Specialist (17 brands) + FT Live event access + Ask FT = an obvious premium bundle competitive with Bloomberg Terminal in the corporate price segment. The infrastructure exists across all five business units. The packaged product does not. Jon Slade's commercial background makes this the most likely near-term announcement in 2026.

### 9.5 Young Audience Pipeline Is Insufficient

FT is a publication for established professionals aged 35–55. The pipeline of future subscribers — 25–35 year old MBA students, junior analysts, policy researchers — is underserved by current products. FT Edit is the only dedicated young-audience product and it is a text application. This is a medium-term vulnerability, not a 2026 crisis. But the product development cycle means action is needed now.

### 9.6 FT Strategies Google Dependency Is a Concentration Risk

A significant portion of FT Strategies revenue flows through the Google News Initiative partnership. FT Strategies is not a fully independent commercial business. If Google changes GNI terms or reduces the programme, FT Strategies revenue contracts materially. This risk deserves explicit acknowledgement alongside the £15M+ revenue figure.

### 9.7 The Most Dangerous Competitor Is Not in the Standard Comparison

The typical FT competitive comparison — Bloomberg, WSJ, The Economist — misses the most immediate commercial threat. The corporate client paying £10,000 per year for FT Professional access can today ask ChatGPT Enterprise the same question and receive approximately 80% of the answer for a fraction of the price. Perplexity Pro, Bloomberg Terminal AI, and Reuters AI products are all moving into the verified-answer-for-financial-decisions space.

Ask FT, if launched at scale with real-time data, is FT's structural response to this threat. But it is in limited beta in March 2026 while the competition is in production. The window is open — it is not permanently open.

## 11. What FT Will Genuinely Need Soon — Prioritised Gap Analysis

Not all gaps are equally urgent. Ranked by how quickly the competitive window closes:

**Priority 1 — Critical within 6–12 months: Ask FT with real-time data**

The known failure mode is well-documented: Ask FT believed Nikki Haley was still an active presidential candidate (recorded by Nieman Lab). For a financial audience making time-sensitive decisions, this is not a minor bug — it is a product-category disqualifier. Bloomberg Terminal AI, Reuters AI, and Perplexity Pro all deliver real-time verified answers to professional users. FT needs Ask FT integrated with wire services and live data feeds before it can compete in the corporate AI-answers market. The competitive window is 6–12 months. After that, Bloomberg will have embedded switching costs in its client base that will be very difficult to dislodge.

**Priority 2 — Important within 12 months: FT All Access bundle**

FT.com + FT Specialist (17 brands) + FT Live event access + Ask FT as a single corporate subscription package, priced to compete with Bloomberg Terminal in the £15,000–25,000 per year enterprise segment. The infrastructure exists across all five business units. The packaged product does not. Jon Slade's commercial background makes this the most probable near-term product announcement. NYT All Access demonstrated that the right bundle simultaneously increases ARPU and reduces churn — the two variables FT cares most about.

**Priority 3 — Important within 18 months: AI Playground as external SaaS via FT Strategies**

AI Playground is a strong internal tool for journalist workflows. FT Strategies already consults 800+ publishers. The obvious next step: license the AI Playground tooling to other publishers as a SaaS product through FT Strategies — the same move Burda made with AISSIST. FT has the infrastructure, the client relationships, and the brand credibility. What is needed is a product decision and a pricing model.

**Priority 4 — Operational improvement within 18 months: AI-personalised churn reduction**

FT correctly prioritises retention over acquisition. The next operational level: AI-personalised re-engagement sequences tailored to individual reading history — "You have not read FT for 12 days. Based on your reading pattern, here are three articles matching your profile." Personalisation at the sophistication of Spotify Wrapped, applied to a financial professional audience with an established RFV behavioural baseline. The data already exists. The personalisation engine does not yet.

**Priority 5 — Horizon 2–3 years: Video strategy for young audiences**

FT Edit is a correct first step but it is a text product. A lightweight video format — explainers, 60–90 seconds, vertical orientation for mobile — as a funnel for future paying subscribers (today's MBA students and junior analysts) needs to be built now for it to be in production in three years. A tool like Schibsted's Videofy would be architecturally compatible with FT's editorial workflow and editorial standards. This is not a 2026 launch — it is a 2026 development decision.

**Priority 6 — Horizon 3–5 years: Federated Ask FT + Nikkei Archive**

Ask FT works with English-language FT content from 1888. Corporate clients in Asia need Asian business intelligence plus Western analysis simultaneously. A federated product drawing on both The Nikkei archive and FT's archive in one interface would be unique in the world. The technology exists separately in both companies. What does not exist is the integration and the governance decision at the Nikkei-FT holding level to create it. A 3–5 year horizon is realistic given the organisational complexity.

## 13. Overall Assessment

FT is the best subscription model in business media. This is not hyperbole — it is a conclusion supported by financial results that most publishers treat as aspirational targets. £540M revenue, £41M operating profit, ARPU £400, reader revenue majority — achieved through 25 years of decisions that were often wrong in the short term and right in the long term.

The main challenges do not threaten existence. They threaten pace. Decelerating subscriber growth, a deliberately cautious pace of AI deployment relative to competitors, absence of a video funnel for young audiences, and a bundling product that exists as an intention but not yet as a product.

The leadership change to Jon Slade is the structural variable that will define the next five years. His commercial orientation should accelerate bundling and AI product revenue in ways that Ridding's editorial orientation would have managed more cautiously. The risk is that the internal editorial-commercial balance shifts in ways that gradually erode the trust premium — the intangible asset that makes £400 ARPU possible and that took 25 years to build.

**Overall rating: A−.** The best subscription story in business media. Structurally sound, diversified revenue, eight years of editorial independence under Japanese ownership. The weakness is pace, not direction: AI adoption is too cautious relative to the speed at which Bloomberg and AI-native competitors are moving into the verified-answers-for-finance niche. If Ask FT becomes a full real-time product before Bloomberg fully closes that market, FT extends its leadership into the next decade. If it does not, FT remains excellent but loses the premium positioning that commands £400 ARPU from a new generation of subscribers who have grown up asking questions to machines.

The rating stays at A−, not A. The CEO transition, the decelerating subscriber growth, and the real-time gap in Ask FT are three simultaneous variables, each of which would justify caution individually. Together they argue for a ceiling on the current rating until execution evidence accumulates under Slade's leadership.

## 14. Executive Summary

### Who FT Is

A British business newspaper founded in 1888, published on salmon-pink paper — its global trademark. Owned by Japan's Nikkei since 2015 but operating with full editorial independence. 1.5 million paying readers — one of the strongest results among quality publications anywhere in the world.

### What FT Got Right — Including the Hard Part

FT started charging for content in 2002 when the entire internet was free. For the first eight years it looked like a mistake: in 2010 FT had around 207,000 digital subscribers while Google had billions of free users. Most media observers said FT was wrong. The reason FT could be wrong for eight years and survive is that Nikkei, unlike most media owners, provided patient capital. That patience — not the technology — is the real competitive advantage.

In 2014 FT invented RFV — a way of measuring whether a reader is genuinely engaged with content rather than just clicking and leaving. Hundreds of newspapers now use this metric. Many of them pay FT to teach them how, through the FT Strategies consulting business. FT earns revenue from journalism and also from teaching others to do journalism better. That combination is unusual.

FT Live — 200 conferences per year — completes the strategic loop. Each event brings a dozen executives onto a stage with FT journalists. The journalists leave with exclusive interviews. Those interviews justify subscription renewals. The subscriptions fund the next round of events. It is a closed system that cannot be interrupted without breaking the model.

### How FT Makes Money — The Simple Version

Five ways. Individual subscriptions at around £400 per year each. Corporate subscriptions — banks, consulting firms, governments. Seventeen niche publications including The Banker and Investors' Chronicle. Events and conferences. And consulting for other media companies who want to learn from the FT model. Together: £540 million in 2024.

More than half of that revenue comes directly from readers — not from advertisers. This is the number that matters most. When the advertising market falls, FT's core economics are largely insulated. When it does not, FT earns from both.

### What Is Not Working or At Risk

Ask FT is the right product — asking questions and getting answers drawn from 138 years of FT journalism, grounded in verified reporting with no hallucination from external sources. But it is still in limited testing in March 2026, while Bloomberg, Reuters, and Perplexity are in production serving the same professional audience. The window to own this product category is open. It is not permanently open.

Young people consume financial information through video. FT almost does not make video. This works today because the core audience is 40–55 years old. In ten years it is a problem. The product development cycle is long — action is needed now.

There is no "everything in one" package. FT plus 17 niche publications plus conference access should be one product at one price. It is not yet. Jon Slade as the new commercial-background CEO makes this the most likely near-term announcement.

### One Conclusion

FT is what every other publisher in this analysis is trying to become, and what only FT has actually achieved: more than half of revenue from readers rather than advertisers, a profitable business, an independent newsroom, stable growth. The one genuine question for the next three years is whether Ask FT becomes a real product before Bloomberg and AI-native competitors finish occupying the niche of verified AI answers for financial professionals. If yes — FT leads into the next decade. If not — FT remains excellent but loses the premium positioning it took 25 years to build.
