## 1. Context — A Publisher at Mature Digital Profitability

VG (Verdens Gang) in March 2026 is Norway's largest digital news site and the most visited in the country. Founded in 1945 by members of the Norwegian Resistance movement immediately after liberation from Nazi occupation, VG completed its digital transformation earlier than most European publishers and now operates a stable, profitable model with three distinct revenue streams: advertising, subscriptions and affiliate marketing.

## 1.1 History — From Resistance Newspaper to Digital Media House

### Founding

VG was founded on **23 June 1945** — the day Norway was liberated from Nazi occupation. Its founders were members of the Norwegian Resistance movement who created it as the voice of a free country. This is not merely a colourful origin story: it explains why VG still positions itself as an independent, non-partisan publication for the general public — the values were embedded at the moment of creation.

### Print Peak

Through the 1960s–1990s, VG was Norway's largest tabloid. Format: crime, sport, scandal, politics — everything tabloid audiences love. Peak print circulation: **over 400,000 copies per day** in a country of 4 million. This was one of the highest per-capita penetration figures of any European newspaper.

In **1966**, Schibsted acquired VG, integrating it into its portfolio alongside Aftenposten.

### Digital Pivot — One of the World's Earliest

**1995** — VG launches VG.no. This was not just the first Norwegian news website — it was one of the first news websites in the world. At the time, most newspapers were not even discussing the internet as a serious channel.

### The Defining Decision of the 2000s

In the 2000s, VG's leadership made a decision that many publishers could not bring themselves to take: **do not defend print, accelerate digital**. Rather than slowing the growth of the website to protect newspaper circulation, they invested in digital and let print die at its natural pace.

> **📌 Editorial observation:** VG's trajectory is a rare example of a publisher that did not cling to its print past but deliberately let it go. Most European newspapers spent 2000–2020 on the defensive — cutting digital investment to protect print revenue. VG did the opposite — and arrived in 2025 in a position most competitors only aspire to. The founding story on the day of national liberation is not merely a historical footnote. It functions as a living brand narrative explaining to readers why paying for VG matters: this is not just a tabloid, it is a publication with a historical obligation to the country.

## 2. How VG Thinks as a Publisher

### 2.1 From Print Tabloid to Leading Digital Media House

VG retains the identity of a national tabloid — accessible, popular, oriented to a mass audience — but has entirely reimagined what "tabloid" means in the digital era. The strategy is not deep quality journalism (as at Le Monde or Aftenposten) but maximum engagement with the maximum audience through accessible content, strong video and data.

Editor-in-Chief Steiro articulated this with unusual candour at WAN-IFRA 2025: *"The digital VG with its two million daily users is a kind of print newspaper: our users are ageing, we cannot recruit enough new readers, and everything suggests we are heading into another transition period."* This kind of public self-criticism is rare from a media executive: VG is successful and profitable — and simultaneously recognises it stands before the next inflection point.

### 2.2 Data as the Only Compass

VG is one of the most data-driven publishers in this peer group. Every editorial position on the homepage is justified by real-time analytics. The newsroom uses **Curate** — Schibsted's algorithmic recommendation platform that blends editorial signals with user behaviour data. The key metric: **daily engaged readers** *(the number of active daily readers — a deeper loyalty measure than raw reach)*, not just total audience.

### 2.3 Three Equal Revenue Streams — Advertising, Subscriptions, Affiliate

VG deliberately diversifies revenue so that no single source becomes dangerously dominant. Advertisers (65%) provide scale. VG+ subscribers (35%) provide stability. The affiliate programme ($10M, team of 10) is a new third pillar that monetises the long tail of traffic without adding editorial headcount.

## 3. The Editorial Kitchen — How It Actually Works

### 3.1 Newsroom Structure

VG employs 533 staff. Editor-in-Chief Gard Steiro simultaneously holds the CEO role — editorial and commercial authority combined in one person. This distinguishes VG from Le Monde (where the roles are separated) and The Guardian (which has a separate CEO in Anna Bateson).

The newsroom is organised around three formats: the core news stream at VG.no, the video platform VGTV, and the experimental new product VGX. Each has its own team, but all are built on shared Schibsted infrastructure.

### 3.2 VGTV — Video Division as Centre of Excellence

VGTV, launched in 2011, was Schibsted's earliest major investment in digital video. VG deliberately spun video out into a separate structure that competed with the main site — a classic Schibsted approach to internal spinoff innovation.

By 2025, VGTV reaches more viewers than Norway's two national broadcast television channels combined. Content: news, sport, documentaries, entertainment. Business model: subscriptions (VGTV behind the paywall) + advertising + documentary licensing.

**The key subscription-via-video experiment:** by placing 400+ documentary films behind the VG+ paywall (titles such as *Searching for Sugar Man*, *Amy*, *Catfish*), VG attracted 20,000 new subscribers in a single year. This is a documented precedent: **documentary content as a subscription conversion tool**.

### 3.3 Editorial Conference and Daily Cycle

**Morning conference** — daily story prioritisation. Yesterday's traffic and engagement data feed into the editorial plan. The guiding principle: *"If a homepage headline does not hook in 3 seconds — it is the wrong headline."*

VG publicly acknowledges that 18.7 million homepage loads per day is the primary interaction point with its audience, and optimising the homepage receives as much editorial attention as the journalism itself.

### 3.4 Six AI Tools in the Newsroom (WAN-IFRA, April 2024)

VG maintains a dedicated team of 7 developers working exclusively on AI tools for the newsroom. As of April 2024, six tools were in production:

1. **AI article summaries** — automated summaries for every published piece (OpenAI/GPT). Result: measurable increase in reader engagement.
2. **Videofy** — article → video in minutes with human-in-the-loop (see separate analysis).
3. **Homepage personalisation** — algorithmic ranking of homepage positions.
4. **AI transcription** — automatic subtitles and transcripts for video content.
5. **Journalist tools** — AI assistance for generating questions and structuring stories.
6. **ARIA** — Schibsted's multi-agent analytics system, giving all staff real-time data access across all group brands via Slack.

> **📌 Editorial observation:** 7 developers, 6 live AI tools — this is one of the densest AI deployments in this peer group relative to newsroom size. For comparison: The Guardian has a formal working group and 3 tools in production. El País built Vera — one product. VG builds horizontally: many tools for different tasks across the entire workflow.

### 3.5 Vertical Video — A Strategic Shift

In 2024, VG committed to **vertical video format** (9:16, TikTok-style) for livestreams on its own platform. Key results:

- **833,000 live stream viewers** for the US election coverage in November 2024 — the first major vertical live-video experiment
- **3,500+ real-time audience comments** via an embedded live comment feature
- Adaptive ad formats: the algorithm mixes non-skip and skippable ads based on individual user behaviour

Kristin Nyland, Schibsted video and audio manager (INMA, August 2025): *"We really underestimated how hard it is to shift an entire editorial department to a new format. Journalists are juggling multiple tools and easily forget about the new stuff."*

This quote matters: it shows that even a technically successful innovation requires deep cultural transformation of the newsroom — and VG is doing this publicly, with acknowledged failures and iterations.

### 4.1 VGX — The Most Radical Experiment in This Peer Group

VGX is a new VG product built by a small autonomous team deliberately isolated from the *"established truths that exist in every newsroom."* For inspiration, the team interviewed young people who knew VG but did not read it, and recruited 700 of them as beta users.

**What VGX is:**
- No homepage — one personalised feed
- No clickbait
- No pre-roll advertising
- No articles in the traditional format — content broken into *"journalistic atoms"*
- **One editor** directs AI agents acting as *"reporters, editors and editors-in-chief"*
- Content aggregated from all Schibsted publications and reformatted

Steiro: *"The art now is to versionise the content we already have. And that requires AI."*

> **📌 Editorial observation:** VGX is the most radical experiment in this peer group. Le Monde translates articles into English via AI. El País built the Vera chatbot. Aftenposten personalises the homepage. VG is building a newsroom run by one person with agentic AI. This is qualitatively different automation. The question is not technical — it is editorial: if AI agents are "sub-editing" and "editing" content, who is accountable for errors? VGX is still in beta, and that is precisely what it is testing.

### 4.2 Schibsted–OpenAI Partnership (February 2025)

In February 2025, Schibsted signed an agreement with OpenAI: VG content and other group titles available in real-time within ChatGPT. Schibsted receives licensing revenue. Journalists receive no direct share — unlike the Le Monde agreement (25%).

## 5. Monetisation — The Three-Pillar Model

### 5.1 Advertising (65% of Revenue)

The traditional first pillar, fully migrated to digital. VG.no is one of the few European news sites where digital advertising generates revenue comparable to the peak of print. The reason: unparalleled reach (57% of the population weekly) creates an advertising inventory that cannot be ignored.

Vertical video added a new ad format — adaptive video ads within the scroll feed, targeting younger audiences who historically blocked advertising.

### 5.2 VG+ Subscriptions (35% of Revenue)

**VG+** — the subscription product launched in 2011 (one of Scandinavia's earliest). Includes: exclusive articles and long reads, documentary films (400+ via VGTV), ad-free app experience.

| Metric | Data |
|---|---|
| VG+ subscribers (Dec. 2023) | 270,500 |
| VG+ subscribers (Dec. 2025) | 400,000+ |
| Growth 2023→2025 | ~+48% in two years |
| Subscription price | ~695 NOK/year (~$65) |

> **📌 Editorial observation:** VG+ is cheaper than Le Monde (~€12/month vs. $65/year ≈ $5.40/month). This is a deliberate pricing strategy for a tabloid with a mass audience. VG+'s ARPU is lower, but so is the cost of acquiring a subscriber. Conversion of a large free audience into VG+ is simultaneously a vulnerability and an advantage: 400,000 subscribers from 2M daily users = ~20% conversion rate — far higher than The Guardian (<1%), but below Le Monde or Aftenposten.

### 5.3 Affiliate Marketing — The Third Pillar

The most unusual revenue stream in this peer group. VG has embedded **affiliate links** *(commission-based links to products or services — VG receives a percentage of each resulting sale)* throughout editorial content:

**Three tracks:**
1. **Editorial** — book reviews automatically include a "buy" link. Finance articles include loan calculators from partner banks. Sports content links to betting operators.
2. **Commercial** — a separate team creates branded content built around affiliate products.
3. **Niche sites** — standalone content verticals dedicated to specific consumer categories (insurance comparisons, VPN rankings, etc.).

Team: 10 people. Revenue 2024: $10M. Forecast 2025: $11M.

> **📌 Editorial observation:** $10M with a team of 10 is economics more common in tech companies than in media. Affiliate requires no sales team, no advertiser negotiations, no campaign reporting. Integration is set up once — with Amazon, Booking.com, local retailers — and revenue flows automatically from every qualifying click. The team focuses solely on optimisation: which links convert, which content drives the most purchases. The editorial risk VG does not discuss publicly: if a journalist knows an article with an affiliate link to product X generates revenue, there is an incentive to write about product X rather than product Y that is objectively better. VG says editorial and commercial are separated. This cannot be independently verified.

## 8. Competitive Landscape

### 8.1 Norway — the World's Highest-Penetration Paid News Market

42% of Norwegians pay for online news — the highest figure among 48 markets in the Reuters Digital News Report 2025. This is a unique structural context: competing publishers operate in a market where the culture of paying for news already exists.

| Publisher | Type | Position |
|---|---|---|
| **VG (Schibsted)** | Tabloid, digital-first | Reach leader, 400k+ subscribers |
| **Aftenposten (Schibsted)** | Quality newspaper | Smaller reach, deeper content |
| **Dagbladet** | Tabloid | VG's direct competitor |
| **NRK** | Public broadcaster | No paywall, public, TV/radio/digital |
| **Dagens Næringsliv** | Business press | Niche, high ARPU |
| **Amedia** | Regional newspapers | +Alt bundle, competes with Schibsted's Full tilgang |

**Key dynamic:** Schibsted offers **Full tilgang** *("Full Access" — a bundle of all Schibsted subscription newspapers: VG+, Aftenposten and regional titles)*. This directly competes with Amedia's **+Alt** — both competing for the wallet of a Norwegian reader already willing to pay for news.

### 8.2 The Real Competition for Young Audiences

VG competes not only with Norwegian media but with TikTok, YouTube and Instagram for the attention of audiences under 35. This is why vertical video, VGX and affiliate content are not responses to another newspaper — they are responses to social platforms.

Steiro: *"The digital VG is ageing. We cannot attract enough new readers."*

## 15. Services VG Will Likely Need — Forward-Looking Assessment

### 15.1 Youth Discovery Infrastructure

**Gap:** Steiro has publicly acknowledged: *"We cannot attract enough new readers."* The current attempt is VGX with vertical video. But VGX is a product on the VG website. Audiences under 25 discover news on TikTok, not on news sites.

**What VG needs:** A systematic TikTok/Reels strategy with measurable conversion into VG+. Not "social media presence" — a complete funnel: TikTok video → VG.no → VG+. VG is the only publisher in this peer group with the technical infrastructure (Videofy, vertical platform) to do this — but has no publicly disclosed conversion strategy.

### 15.2 Agentic Videofy — The Next Level

**Gap:** Videofy requires a journalist to press a button. With 2M daily readers and hundreds of published articles per day, this is a scaling constraint.

**What VG needs:** A proactive Videofy that monitors the publication queue, identifies articles with the highest video potential based on historical engagement data, and initiates generation without a manual trigger. This is Horizon 3 in Schibsted's framework — and VGX is already testing analogous logic.

### 15.3 Converting Affiliate Traffic into Subscribers

**Gap:** $10M in affiliate revenue is generated by users who arrive looking for consumer content (reviews, recommendations). These users do not convert into VG+ because they perceive VG as "a site with shopping links," not a news source.

**What VG needs:** Data-driven segmentation of affiliate visitors + personalised VG+ pitch for those who demonstrate high visit frequency and breadth of interest. Affiliate as a subscription funnel, not only as a standalone revenue stream.

### 15.4 Scaling the Single-Editor Model

**Gap:** One editor directs VGX's AI agents. This is a proof of concept. If the model works, scaling requires training dozens of editors in the new role of "AI director."

**What VG needs:** An "editor as AI director" training programme — not technical, but editorial. Which decisions can be delegated to AI agents, and which cannot? How to verify AI-generated content? How to maintain editorial standards under agentic automation? No publisher in this peer group has built this programme yet — because VGX is the pioneer.
