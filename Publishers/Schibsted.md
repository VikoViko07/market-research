## 1. Context — The Most Important Reorganisation in Nordic Media

To understand Schibsted in March 2026, start with one fact: the company that called itself Schibsted ASA a year ago no longer exists in its previous form.

In June 2024 the holding completed its split into two independent businesses:

**Schibsted** (formerly Schibsted Media) — the media business, solely owned by Tinius Trust. CEO: Siv Juvik Tveitnes. Includes VG, Aftenposten, Bergens Tidende (Norway), Aftonbladet, Svenska Dagbladet, Omni (Sweden), podcast platform Podme, and — since July 2025 — TV4 (Sweden) and MTV (Finland).

**Vend** (formerly Schibsted Marketplaces) — a publicly listed company. CEO: Christian Printzell Halvorsen. Four verticals: Mobility, Jobs, Real Estate, Recommerce.

In May 2025 the media business reclaimed the name Schibsted — after the listed company officially renamed itself Vend. CEO Siv Juvik Tveitnes: *"Schibsted is a historic name and brand that carries trust in the Nordics and internationally, and that is associated first and foremost with newspapers and news media."* This is not cosmetic — it is a deliberate signal: the media business is the real Schibsted.

## 3. How Schibsted Thinks as a Publisher

### 3.1 Three Horizons of AI Strategy

Juan Carlos Lopez Calvet, Schibsted's Director of Data and AI, framed the AI strategy through three horizons:

- **Horizon one** — AI makes existing services more efficient (automating routine tasks, accelerating production).
- **Horizon two** — AI helps expand the product portfolio (new formats, new audiences).
- **Horizon three** — AI creates uniqueness in services (products that are simply impossible without AI).

This is not marketing rhetoric — it is an operational roadmap with concrete projects at each level.

### 3.2 Four Principles of AI

Schibsted publicly adheres to four guiding principles for AI use:

1. **Augment, not replace** — AI as a journalist's tool, not their replacement.
2. **Trust as the north star** — nothing works without reliable data and transparency.
3. **Responsible use** — AI must be fair and inclusive.
4. **Transparency to the audience** — readers must know when AI was involved in creating content.

### 3.3 Data as Editorial Compass

The Curate personalisation system dynamically predicts what each reader wants to read and serves personalised content to increase subscription sales in real time. A/B tests showed a 75% increase in subscription sales from the homepage compared to previous models.

### 3.4 Subscription Model — An Important Duality

Two fundamentally different business models coexist within the Schibsted portfolio:

- **VG and Aftonbladet** — the largest brands reaching 50–60% of the population in Norway and Sweden — operate primarily on an advertising model. Subscriptions account for roughly 15% of VG's digital revenue.
- **Aftenposten and Svenska Dagbladet** — operate on a subscription model. At Aftenposten, subscriptions account for approximately 60% of digital revenue.

This matters: when people say "subscriptions work at Schibsted," it is only true for part of the portfolio. For the flagship mass-market brands, advertising still dominates. The advertising market risk is higher than it appears at first glance.

## 4. The Editorial Kitchen — How It Actually Works

### 4.1 VGX — The Most Radical Editorial Experiment in Europe

Gard Steiro, Editor-in-Chief and CEO of VG, introduced VGX — "a completely new VG," a full reimagining of the news product. The team worked autonomously to avoid influence from the "established truths" of the existing newsroom. For inspiration they surveyed young people who knew VG but did not read it, and recruited 700 beta users.

What changed fundamentally: articles in their previous form disappeared. Pre-rolls and click-to-play disappeared. Clickbait and the traditional homepage disappeared. One feed gives overview, a second layer gives depth.

VGX relies on a workflow in which content from other Schibsted publications is broken into "journalistic atoms." AI agents then work as "reporters, editors and editors-in-chief," assembling content into new formats. The entire process is curated by one person — a single reporter manages the agents.

This is not a redesign — it is a paradigm shift. This is what Axel Springer declares, what Burda describes as a goal for 2027. At Schibsted it is already in beta testing.

**Why VGX exists.** Steiro named the reason honestly: *"The digital VG with its two million daily users is a kind of print newspaper — our audience is ageing, we cannot attract enough new readers."* VGX is the direct answer to this structural challenge.

### 4.2 The Real Editorial Decision Cycle

**Morning:** editors see the Curate + ARIA dashboard — which content is converting to subscriptions right now, which topics are gaining traction, what is being read repeatedly.

**Editorial meeting:** topics are evaluated on four criteria — uniqueness, urgency, audience fit, subscription potential.

**"Where can we be present where readers cannot go" filter:** Steiro: *"Everybody can build AI tools, that's not so difficult. The difficult thing is to improve the unique content, such as investigations, but above all, being present where readers cannot go. We try to use AI to free resources to do more of this stuff."*

**AI layer:** Videofy generates video, ARIA answers analytical questions via Slack, Curate personalises delivery.

### 4.4 ARIA — Democratising Data

ARIA is an AI framework of specialised agents. Each agent works with a separate dataset: subscriptions, brand data, editorial metrics. A coordinating agent assembles comprehensive answers. Any Schibsted employee can get real-time analytics simply by asking a question via Slack.

Lopez Calvet: *"This is our way of democratising access to insights — something that was very difficult before. Now it is available to any employee."*

### 4.5 Native Go — A Third Revenue Stream

In March 2025 Schibsted launched Native Go — a self-service native advertising platform with AI content generation. A language model trained on Aftonbladet's editorial style creates advertising material from a template. Schibsted Brand Studio experts review every piece before publication. First: Aftonbladet, then Omni and Svenska Dagbladet. This is a third revenue stream independent of both subscriptions and programmatic display.

## 5. Videofy — In-Depth Analysis

### 5.1 What Videofy Is

Videofy is an AI tool developed inside Schibsted to automatically create short news videos from text articles. Originally built in the VG newsroom, then rolled out across all Schibsted brands.

**Technical pipeline:**
1. Takes a published article from the CMS
2. An AI model generates a short script based on the text
3. The system automatically selects images and video clips from the article and external sources (Reuters, AP)
4. ElevenLabs generates the voiceover (synthetic voice)
5. A proprietary hotspot model (trained on Hugging Face) identifies focal areas on press images for cropping
6. Automatically renders the final video
7. The editor sees the result in the CMS UI, can make edits and approve for publication

The entire process — from article to finished video — takes a few minutes. Previously such video summaries required significant editorial resources and manual production for most of the working day.

### 5.2 Where It Is Used

Videofy is actively used to produce short news videos for:
- Public screens (digital signage, transport)
- Social media and digital platforms
- Mobile apps of Schibsted brands

After several thousand videos produced internally, Schibsted open-sourced the tool on 17 March 2026.

### 5.3 Open Source — Why This Is Strategically Important (17 March 2026)

On 17 March 2026 Schibsted published Videofy on GitHub as open source. This happened the day this analysis was compiled and changes the strategic picture.

Lopez Calvet: *"We are open-sourcing Videofy because we want to give the entire industry access to technology we developed in-house. Video is becoming an increasingly important way to consume news, but many smaller newsrooms do not have the resources to develop such tools themselves. When such technologies are shared openly, the industry can develop better solutions faster. At the same time it is important that such tools are built with editorial control mechanisms and transparency, so that journalistic principles are upheld."*

**What specifically was released:** A fully functional base version (videofy_minimal) is available on GitHub at github.com/schibsted/videofy_minimal. The tool can run on a laptop with OpenAI and ElevenLabs API keys. Schibsted's internal integrations and infrastructure are not included in the base version — developers take this as a starting point for customisation. Schibsted's own hotspot model for identifying focal areas on images is published on Hugging Face and is also available.

**What this means strategically.** Schibsted replicated exactly the strategy Burda applied with Thunder CMS: build a tool, solve your own problem, open it to the market, build an ecosystem around it. This is a community leadership strategy, not merely open source altruism. Three concrete consequences:

First — Schibsted closes the gap that was previously a weakness in this report. The earlier analysis noted: Schibsted has no B2B technology product for the external market, unlike Burda (AISSIST SaaS) and WaPo (Arc XP + Zeus). Videofy open source is not a monetisable SaaS, but it is the beginning of an ecosystem around Schibsted as a technology leader for the media industry.

Second — Videofy was presented by Lopez Calvet from the stage at "Voices — European Festival of Journalism and Media Freedom" in Florence shortly before the release. This means open source is part of a deliberate communication strategy, not simply a technical code dump.

Third — the tool already covers the entire pipeline. It takes content from Reuters, AP or a URL, builds a script, selects media, generates voiceover, renders video with human-in-the-loop review in the CMS. Newsrooms of any size no longer need to build this from scratch.

## 8. Strengths

**Ownership structure — unique protection.** Tinius Trust as a non-profit foundation eliminates the structural conflict of interests. No Bezos, no family with parallel commercial interests, no investors demanding quarterly returns. This builds editorial independence that cannot be bought and is very difficult to destroy.

**Subscriptions — actually working.** VG has achieved what every publisher dreams of: 400,000+ digital subscribers have allowed the company to stop depending on print revenue. Steiro: *"We could close the print edition tomorrow without firing a single reporter."* This is not a strategy — it is an achieved reality.

**Curate — AI subscription machine.** The own real-time ML system increased subscription sales from the homepage by 75%. Not a pilot — this is a production system.

**Videofy — production AI video, now open source.** The first major European publisher to open-source a full production AI video production tool for the entire industry (17 March 2026). The complete pipeline from article to finished video is automated with human-in-the-loop review.

**VGX and agentic AI — not a declaration, a beta.** AI agents genuinely work as "reporters, editors and editors-in-chief" in VGX. One person manages the agents. Burda names agentic AI as a 2027 goal. At Schibsted it is already being tested.

**TV4 + MTV deal — Nordic omnichannel leader.** The only media company in the region combining text, audio, podcasts, live TV and streaming. TV4 Play + MTV Katsomo delivered full SSAI video monetisation that Schibsted did not have before the deal.

**OpenAI deal reinvested in AI.** Schibsted reinvests all revenue from the OpenAI agreement into internal AI activities. This distinguishes them from publishers who take money for content and do not build their own capabilities.

**FAST framework and AI Academy.** Own AI ethics system + 600+ employees enrolled in the training programme. Organisational infrastructure for scaling AI without losing quality.

**Aftonbladet AI chatbot — audience AI product already in production.** 600,000+ user questions about the US presidential elections answered. This is not an "absent" product — it is a working case study.

## 9. Weaknesses

**Geographic concentration.** The entire business is Norway, Sweden, Finland. High levels of trust in media and willingness to pay for subscriptions are an advantage. But any systemic crisis in the Nordic media market hits the entire business simultaneously.

**TV4/MTV integration — a colossal management challenge.** 1,200 new TV4/MTV employees, three markets, different corporate cultures, different advertising models. Integration will take 2–3 years. This is a risk, not only a strategic enhancement.

**Linear TV is declining — the hidden deal risk.** TV4 and MTV as linear broadcasters face their advertising and subscription revenues shifting from linear broadcasting to streaming. Schibsted paid SEK 6.55bn for an asset, part of which is objectively becoming obsolete. TV4 Play is a growing asset. Linear TV4 is shrinking. The debt burden from the deal is a financial risk that must be named honestly alongside the strategic benefits.

**No global B2B technology product.** Unlike Washington Post (Arc XP and Zeus) and Burda (AISSIST SaaS), Schibsted does not monetise its tools on the external market. Curate, ARIA — internal use only. Videofy open source is a step in the right direction but not SaaS revenue. A missed revenue stream.

**Dual subscription model.** VG and Aftonbladet — flagships reaching 50–60% of the population — operate primarily on advertising (subscriptions ~15% of VG revenue). The advertising market risk is higher than it appears on the surface.

**Limited first-party data beyond paying subscribers.** VG's daily audience of 2 million — the majority are anonymous. Curate works with limited data on anonymous users. Lopez Calvet acknowledged: *"This is really about adopting the mindset of selling the strawberries you have at hand"* — no massive datasets like competitors from the app and social media world.

**No unified cross-platform advertising stack.** After the TV4 acquisition, Schibsted has digital publications, television, streaming and podcasts — but no single advertising infrastructure selling inventory across all platforms to one advertiser. Integration has begun but no complete solution exists.

## 11. What Will Change in the Next 12–18 Months

**First: VGX from beta to production.** VGX is currently being tested with 700 young users. A wide launch is expected in 2026 — the first fully agentic-AI media product in mainstream European media. If VGX works, the model will scale to Aftonbladet and other brands.

**Second: unified advertising stack.** The TV4 integration into the digital advertising portfolio has begun. The next step: a single programmatic + premium product for advertisers covering TV4 Play, MTV Katsomo, VG, Aftonbladet and Aftenposten simultaneously. This is a 2026–2027 task.

**Third: unified subscription product — Nordic Bundle.** Separate subscriptions for VG, Aftenposten, TV4 Play, Aftonbladet Plus — the next logical step is a unified "Schibsted Pass." Precedent: NYT All Access turned a utilitarian newspaper subscription into a lifestyle product. Schibsted has unique assets for similar bundling.

**Fourth: scaling agentic AI concierges.** Schibsted is testing AI concierges that learn from user habits and deliver personalised morning briefings. The production version will compete with NYT and Politico morning newsletters.

**Fifth: Videofy ecosystem.** After the open source release — expect a developer community, contributions from other newsrooms, and potentially a cloud-hosted version as SaaS for publishers without their own infrastructure.

## 12. Technology Gaps

**1. Unified cross-platform advertising stack.** After the TV4 acquisition, an infrastructure is needed that sells inventory across all platforms to a single advertiser. Integration has started but no complete solution exists.

**2. Unified subscription product for the entire portfolio.** No "Schibsted Pass" with access to the full Nordic media bundle. The infrastructure for this needs to be built.

**3. Federated AI — knowledge sharing between newsrooms.** ARIA works inside Schibsted as an analytics system. The next level: if VG does not know the answer to a user's question, the system searches Aftonbladet or Aftenposten archives. The architecture Arc XP is building through Ask The News. The content exists — the infrastructure does not.

**4. Videofy SaaS version for external publishers.** Open source is the first step. The next: a cloud-hosted version that newsrooms without a technical team can use as a service. This is a potential B2B revenue stream.

**5. Structured vector archive.** Schibsted signed a deal with OpenAI for access to current news. A full vector archive of all historical content (like Der Spiegel is building) is the foundation for deeper AI products. Without it, Ask-The-News type products are limited to fresh content only.

## 15. Executive Summary — Plain Language

### Who Schibsted Is

A Norwegian-Swedish media company with 180 years of history. Owns the largest news websites in Norway (VG, Aftenposten) and Sweden (Aftonbladet, Svenska Dagbladet). In 2025 they acquired TV4 — Sweden's largest commercial TV channel — and MTV Finland. This is no longer just newspapers: it is text, audio, podcasts, television and streaming in one holding.

The single most important difference from all competitors: the owner is Tinius Trust, a non-profit foundation created specifically to protect independent journalism. No Bezos with personal interests. No family with parallel businesses. No investors who need returns right now. This is structurally the healthiest media company in the comparison.

### What They Do Well

Subscriptions actually work. VG has 400,000 paying digital subscribers — and the editor-in-chief openly says: "We could close the print edition tomorrow without firing a single journalist." That is real digital transformation, not its imitation.

They built their own AI tools that genuinely work — not in pilot, but in production. Curate personalises content and increases subscription sales. ARIA answers any employee's analytical questions via Slack in real time. The Aftonbladet AI chatbot answered 600,000 user questions about the US presidential election.

The freshest event: on 17 March 2026 they open-sourced their Videofy tool for the entire media industry.

### Videofy — Why It Matters

Videofy turns a text article into a finished news video in a few minutes, automatically. A journalist writes a piece — the system writes the script, selects images, synthesises the voice, edits the video. The editor reviews the result with one click and publishes. Previously this took hours of manual work.

Schibsted used this tool themselves and produced thousands of videos for their brands. Then they open-sourced it — any newsroom in the world can now take it for free and use it. This is the same story as Burda with their Thunder CMS: build a good tool, solve your own problem, share with the market, become the ecosystem leader.

This means Schibsted is transitioning from being a Nordic publisher to being a technology company that sets standards for the entire industry.

### What Is Not Working or At Risk

They bought TV4 and MTV for $615 million. Strategically correct — now they have television and streaming. But this is also a major debt burden and a complex integration of 1,500 new employees. Plus linear television is losing audiences worldwide — part of the acquired asset is objectively becoming obsolete.

They operate almost exclusively in Norway, Sweden and Finland. No global ambitions. Good for quality and resilience, but a clear ceiling on growth.

Most of VG's two million daily readers are anonymous. There is little data on them. Without this, building the next generation of personalised products is difficult.

### One Conclusion

Schibsted is the healthiest publisher in this comparison. No crisis like Washington Post. No critical advertising dependency like Burda. No owner conflict of interest from any of them. They are already doing what others only promise: agentic AI works, subscriptions work, Videofy works and is now open to the world. The main risk is not technological but integrative: can they effectively combine newspapers, television and streaming into one functioning business over the next two years — or will the TV4 integration slow everything else down.
