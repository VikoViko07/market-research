## 2. How Le Monde Thinks as a Publisher

### 2.1 From Print Legacy to Digital Necessity and Global Expansion

Maintains quality daily identity while expanding: from French focus to global (Le Monde in English since 2022). Produces deep content that AI/wire cannot replace. Focus: explainers, investigations, international news.

### 2.2 Data and Reader Revenue as the Compass

Subscriber data drives: **ARPU growth** *(Average Revenue Per User — average monthly revenue per subscriber)*, personalization, **bundling** *(combining multiple products into one package: Le Monde + Max + Télérama)*. **Reader revenue** *(income directly from readers/subscribers, as opposed to advertising)* — 66–72%, advertising is secondary.

### 2.3 No 'Third Newsroom' Drama — Integration

Video/podcasts/newsletters are part of the core newsroom. **Explainer videos** *(short videos explaining complex topics in plain language)* by younger journalists serve as a **gateway** *(entry point through which new audiences discover paid subscriptions)* to subscriptions.

## 5. Subscription Funnel & Churn Management

This is one of the most technically advanced aspects of Le Monde's operations — and a key growth driver.

### 5.1 Subscription Funnel — The Reader Journey

Le Monde's funnel is built on data and pricing experiments:

- **Entry:** Free content + limited access (metered paywall). Young audiences attracted via Snapchat (1.4M followers), TikTok, YouTube.
- **Subscription trigger:** A/B testing of the subscription page. Original three-tier horizontal layout overwhelmed users. Redesign with simplified interface improved key conversion metrics.
- **Price sensitivity:** Each +€1 added to the entry price = loss of 1,000+ new subscribers on the Essential plan and >2,000 across all plans.
- **Youth conversion:** Under-30s = 50% of new subscribers. *'Some of them discovered Le Monde on Snapchat 5 years ago'* — CEO Dreyfus. Platform nurturing → subscription.

### 5.2 Churn Management — What Le Monde Learned

Le Monde hired a subscription specialist to build a **churn model** *(predictive model forecasting the probability of a specific reader cancelling their subscription)* on 13 months of historical data — projecting retention through month 47.

- **The tiered pricing trap:** Pricing tiers cannibalized profitable subscribers — those paying €17.90 downgraded to €9.90. By month 47, retention under the tiered model was significantly lower than a steady €9.90 plan.
- **Solution — gradual price increases:** Smooth price increases rather than sudden hikes. *'Those already subscribed tend to stay, even with price hikes'* — Flavia Ferreira, Head of Marketing Retention.
- **Anti-churn campaigns:** Targeted emails for 'at-risk subscribers' emphasizing subscription value over discounts. Le Monde Jeux (games) campaign: +10% active subscribers, +3% activity rate, +10pt game grid completion.
- **Serial canceller tracking:** Le Monde tracks those who subscribe and cancel multiple times — the interval between subscriptions shortens over time. After 4 subscriptions, readers tend to stay permanently.

### 5.3 Unreported Metrics — Critical Gaps

**Not disclosed:** Le Monde does not publicly report exact churn rate, **CAC** *(Customer Acquisition Cost — total marketing spend / number of new subscribers)*, or **LTV** *(Lifetime Value — total revenue from a subscriber over their entire subscription life. Healthy business: LTV should be 3x+ higher than CAC)*. Disclosure would enable a more precise assessment of the business model.

## 16. Global Competitors — Who Shares Le Monde's Strategy

Le Monde's combination of reader-first revenue, editorial independence, quality journalism and international expansion is distinctive — but not unique. Several publishers in other countries operate on closely comparable models. Each offers a useful benchmark.

### 16.1 El País (Spain) — Closest Structural Analogue

El País is the most direct comparison. It is the dominant quality daily in Spain, with 400,000 subscribers as of 2025, pursuing an almost identical strategic logic: subscription-first, quality brand, international expansion via language diaspora.

- **Paywall:** Launched in May 2020 — roughly 10 years after Le Monde. Growth has been rapid but heavily reliant on promotional offers, which risks replicating the 'tiered pricing trap' Le Monde already navigated and corrected.
- **International expansion:** El País launched a dedicated US edition in May 2024 targeting the 60M+ Spanish-speaking population in the United States, with separate editions already running in Mexico, Colombia, Chile and Argentina — a linguistic diaspora strategy parallel to Le Monde in English.
- **Product innovation:** El País Premium (2024) bundles access to business daily Cinco Días, with an agreement allowing NYT subscribers access to El País content — a cross-publisher deal Le Monde mirrors through its own NYT cross-promotion.
- **Behavioural segmentation:** El País uses data-driven segmentation to identify reader archetypes from 'promo seekers' to 'sleeper customers', building targeted retention strategies per segment.
- **Key difference — ownership risk:** El País is owned by PRISA, whose majority shareholder changed in March 2025. This creates potential editorial independence pressure that Le Monde's Independence Pole structure explicitly prevents.

### 16.2 Mediapart (France) — The Radical Version

Mediapart is the most philosophically pure expression of the reader-first model. By early 2025 it had surpassed 245,000 paying subscribers, with 100% of revenue coming from subscriptions — zero advertising, zero public subsidies, zero commercial partnerships. Its official slogan: *'Only Our Readers Can Buy Us'.*

- **Ownership structure:** In 2019, inspired by The Scott Trust (Guardian's owner), Mediapart established the non-profit 'Fonds pour une Presse Libre' to own the company in perpetuity and guarantee independence. No bank loans.
- **Mission:** Purely investigative — hundreds of investigations into political corruption, financial fraud, sexual harassment, police violence. The New York Times called it *'France's leading investigative news site'.*
- **Community model:** Le Club — a blogging platform for subscribers — creates participatory journalism. Mediapart crowdfunded a documentary on the Gaddafi-Sarkozy scandal, deepening subscriber loyalty through active engagement.
- **Key difference — scale and scope:** Mediapart is deliberately niche. Its CEO Cécile Sourd said at WAN-IFRA Krakow 2025 that their model *'might not work as a template that others can copy exactly'*. Le Monde aims for 1M subscribers and international scale; Mediapart aims for depth and purity.

### 16.3 Aftenposten / Schibsted (Norway) — Most Technologically Advanced

Norway is the world leader in news payment willingness — 42% of users pay for digital news. Aftenposten, owned by Schibsted, has shifted since 2009 from advertising to readers, with approximately 80% of its revenue now coming from ~150,000 digital-only subscribers.

- **Subscriber-first KPI:** Two years ago, Aftenposten introduced a single newsroom-wide metric: increase the number of daily engaged subscribers, defined as reading at least 20% of an article. Every department — editorial, product, marketing — received weekly targets tied to this goal.
- **Audience segmentation:** Four reader archetypes — the Analyst, the Empath, the Surfer, the Sporadic — inform content strategy, homepage algorithms and marketing campaigns.
- **Personalisation:** An editorially-controlled front page algorithm ranks stories per reader based on behaviour, while preserving editorial judgment — major news always surfaces regardless of personal preferences.
- **Key difference — holding company risks:** Schibsted is a large media group with 7,000 employees across multiple brands. In 2025, it announced 350 redundancies — the opposite trajectory to Le Monde. The trust structure isolating editorial from commercial pressure is less clear than at Le Monde or Mediapart.

### 16.5 Conclusion — What Makes Le Monde Distinctive

Among quality publishers globally, Le Monde occupies a rare position: it is simultaneously growing its newsroom, scaling international expansion via AI translations, distributing AI licensing revenues directly to journalists, and protecting editorial independence through a structural ownership shield.

**No direct copycat exists.** El País comes closest in structural logic but started a decade later and faces ownership risks. Mediapart is philosophically purer but deliberately smaller. Aftenposten is more technically sophisticated in personalisation but is contracting rather than growing.

The most accurate framing: Le Monde is building the European model of a profitable quality publisher in the AI era — reader-funded, AI-assisted but human-led, structurally independent, and internationally expanding through a second-read strategy rather than direct competition with Anglo-Saxon giants.

**The core bet:** quality journalism + structural independence + reader revenue + AI as a tool (not a replacement) = a sustainable business that can grow without sacrificing what makes it valuable. Whether this holds at 1M subscribers and beyond remains the open question.

Dreyfus explained Le Monde's French market dominance strategy to the UK trade press. Three reasons cited for leading France's digital subscription market:

### 17.6 Key Patterns Across All Appearances

- **Consistent core narrative:** Every public appearance reinforces the same three pillars — invest in journalists, trust readers to pay, use AI as a tool not a replacement. The message is disciplined and never deviates.
- **Role separation:** CEO handles strategy, vision and AI philosophy. Operational directors (Retention, Subscriptions, Marketing) present data-heavy technical cases. This creates credibility at two levels simultaneously.
- **Deliberate thought leadership:** Le Monde presents its model as a template for the industry, not just a company update. This positions it as the European benchmark — which in turn strengthens the brand, aids recruitment, and shapes regulatory conversations around AI licensing.
- **No vanity metrics:** Unlike many publishers, Le Monde presentations consistently include uncomfortable data — the tiered pricing failure, the advertiser slowdown, the gap between 12,000 English subscribers and a 1M target. This candour builds credibility.
