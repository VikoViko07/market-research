## 1. Context — The Most Aggressive Transformation in European Media

Understanding Axel Springer in March 2026 is impossible without one fact: over the last 18 months this is not the same company. In September 2024 the holding completed a split that changed its fundamental nature — and unlocked a cash reserve the company had never had before.

**Media business** (the new Axel Springer, ~98% owned by Mathias Döpfner and Friede Springer): Bild, Welt, Business Insider, Politico, Axios, Morning Brew, Idealo. Expected revenue 2025: approximately €2.2bn.

**Classifieds** (renamed, controlling stake held by KKR and CPP Investments): StepStone, Aviv Group, finanzen.net, Awin. The deal was valued at £11.3bn — and this gave Döpfner a war chest for acquisitions he had never had before.

And immediately — the freshest event, which happened just two weeks ago.

In February 2026 Axel Springer announced the acquisition of Telegraph Media Group for $766M (£575M). CEO Mathias Döpfner: *"More than 20 years ago we tried to buy The Telegraph and failed. Now our dream is coming true. Owning this institution of quality British journalism is a privilege and a responsibility."*

Axel Springer stated it will invest in the Telegraph to make it *"the leading centrist media outlet in the English-speaking world"* and will *"accelerate"* its expansion into the US market.

## 3. How Axel Springer Thinks as a Publisher

### 3.1 "Digital Is the New Print. AI Is the New Digital."

In June 2025, Döpfner demanded that employees use AI *"in all areas of work"*: *"Digital is the new print. AI is the new digital."* This is not a metaphor — it is an operational directive.

The concrete strategy: *"The click-maximisation and advertising business model is over. We must focus on deep, long-term relationships with users. Dependence on search and social is a weakness. Owning the audience is a strength."*

### 3.2 Three Conditions for Journalism's Survival

At the INMA World Congress, Döpfner set out three conditions under which journalism will survive the AI era: first — embrace AI and *"draw the right conclusions"*; second — regulation (*"copyright as we know it is over"*); third — editorial independence and reader trust. *"Technological change is always a challenge, but many have turned it into an advantage. It is not about the technology itself, but about our behaviour and credibility."*

### 3.3 "Find the New Gold"

Döpfner openly acknowledged: after selling the Classifieds business (which was the main profit engine), the company needs a new *"third pillar."* *"We need to find the new gold. As we found it a decade and a half ago with digital classifieds. It must be connected to our core competencies: content, subscriptions, advertising, mass markets, technology."*

## 4. The Editorial Kitchen — How It Actually Works

### 4.1 The "Journalistic Production vs. Journalistic Creation" Model

Döpfner draws a clear internal line:

**Journalistic production** — layout, sub-editing, proofreading, aggregation, reformatting. All of this is being automated. Bild has already closed six of its 18 regional editorial offices and two-thirds of its regional bureaux as part of a restructuring that eliminated around 200 roles.

**Journalistic creation** — investigations, exclusives, opinion columns, content that goes *"where readers cannot go themselves."* This is protected and reinforced.

Döpfner: *"Journalistic production is becoming a by-product, increasingly technically supported and automated. Only those who create the best original content will survive."*

### 4.2 The Real Editorial Decision Cycle (Bild)

**Morning:** editors see the dashboard — direct traffic (Döpfner calls it *"proof that users choose Axel Springer"*), CTR, Bild+ subscription conversion.

**Meeting:** filter — exclusivity, urgency, direct traffic potential. Dependence on Google Search is treated as an institutional risk.

**AI layer:** Hey_ — the AI assistant embedded in the editorial process. SEO headlines are suggested automatically. Summaries, transcription, reformatting — automated.

**Final decision:** the editor. However, this is precisely where the major scandal occurred: at Politico, AI summaries were published on the homepage without editorial review and contained factual errors and style guide violations.

Claudius Senst, CEO of Bild Group, presented Hey_ at DMEXCO 2024 as a product that moves readers from passive content consumption to active interaction. Hey_ combines large language models (LLMs), extensive content archives, web search and retail integrations. Millions of users already interact with the Bild AI chatbot.

Hey_ is positioned as a competitor to ChatGPT for news content: a reader asks a question, receives an answer from verified Bild/Welt content, and follows through to the full article.

## 7. Strengths

**First in the world — the OpenAI deal.** Axel Springer became the first publishing house in the world to sign a partnership with OpenAI for deep integration of journalism into AI technology. This means: training the model on Bild, Welt, and Politico content; early API access; attribution in ChatGPT responses. No competitor received these terms first.

**The broadest AI deal portfolio in the industry.** Beyond OpenAI, Axel Springer has signed deals with Microsoft (Copilot Daily, Microsoft Advertising), ProRata.ai (50% subscription revenue share), and expanded its Microsoft partnership to Azure. No publisher has this breadth of AI partnerships.

**Global brand portfolio.** The only publisher in this analysis with genuine global reach: Germany (Bild, Welt), USA (Politico, Business Insider, Axios, Morning Brew), and from March 2026 — the UK (Telegraph). This is reach that Burda, Schibsted, and the Washington Post outside the US simply do not have.

**M&A as a strategic instrument.** Axel Springer created a dedicated internal M&A team specifically for acquiring AI startups. This is not just interest in AI — this is an institutionalised acquisition machine for the technology sector.

**Morning Brew — a model newsletter monetisation business.** Morning Brew with 4M+ email newsletter subscribers demonstrates how Springer builds direct audience relationships without intermediaries. This is precisely what Döpfner talks about as strategy — and it already works.

**Politico Pro — the best B2B media product in political niche.** Politico Pro's professional subscription costs thousands of dollars per year and is used by governments, lobbyists, and consultants worldwide. This is a high-ARPU, low-churn model.

**Cash reserves after the split.** Selling the Classifieds business for £11.3bn gave Döpfner enormous acquisition capital. The Telegraph purchase for £575M is only the beginning. The company has resources for further acquisitions.

**Hey_ as a reader-facing AI product in production.** Millions of users already interact with the Bild AI chatbot. Unlike the Washington Post ("Ask The Post AI" only recently launched) or Burda (no audience-facing AI product) — at Springer this works at scale.

## 8. Weaknesses

**Döpfner's conflict of interest — structural, not accidental.** Döpfner controls ~98% of the media business, writes internal memos with political positions (*"Please strengthen the FDP"* — correspondence that became public), and manages newsrooms that are supposed to be independent. The scandal involving Döpfner's correspondence ahead of the 2021 Bundestag elections showed that the boundary between the CEO's personal views and editorial policy is blurred.

**The Politico scandal — AI implementation failure.** An arbitrator ruled that Politico's Live Summaries were published on the homepage without editorial review and contained factual errors and style guide violations. The Capitol AI Report Builder generated 500-word reports without editorial oversight, containing *"egregious errors and misinterpretations."* This is the direct consequence of an overly aggressive pace of AI adoption without sufficient guardrails.

**Journalist layoffs without a clear subscription offset.** Bild laid off hundreds of staff and closed six of its 18 regional editorial offices. Unlike the Washington Post, where layoffs triggered a wave of cancellations, Bild has not faced a comparable trust crisis — but the risk remains.

**No unified technology product for the market.** Unlike the Washington Post (Arc XP + Zeus) and Burda (AISSIST SaaS), Springer does not sell a proprietary technology platform to other publishers. All tools are internal. This means no external technology revenue stream.

**Ideological positioning as a commercial risk.** In 2024 Axel Springer faced criticism for anti-Muslim and anti-Palestinian bias in Welt and Bild publications. The company's declared *"core values"* (support for Israel, transatlantic alliance) limit audience and advertiser reach in certain markets.

**Telegraph integration complexity.** The £575M Telegraph acquisition is Springer's largest deal since Politico. The Telegraph is a traditional British newspaper with a conservative audience and a strong editorial culture. Integration with a German media holding without losing editorial identity is a complex task. Plus the debt burden from the deal.

**Business Insider has already cut 20% of staff.** Business Insider lost 20% of its workforce in the Axel Springer restructuring. A newsroom that has lost one in five journalists produces less content — which directly affects traffic and subscriptions.

**The Telegraph acquisition (6 March 2026) — the single most important event.**
Axel Springer announced the acquisition of Telegraph Media Group for £575M. The deal ends the long uncertainty around the Telegraph and outbids a competing offer from Daily Mail and General Trust.

## 10. What Will Change in the Next 12–18 Months

**First: Telegraph integration — the top priority.** Springer will embed the Telegraph into its AI infrastructure and subscription model. Concrete tasks: connect Hey_ to Telegraph content, scale the Politico Pro model to the British market, develop a joint subscription package.

**Second: the "third pillar" — taking concrete shape.** Döpfner publicly announced the search for *"a high-margin, hyper-growth business"* beyond journalism and marketing. In 2026–2027 this third pillar must take concrete form — through AI startup acquisitions or organic development.

**Third: M&A team — first AI startup deals.** The team is built, the capital exists. Expect first AI company acquisitions — most likely in categories: AI tools for journalism, advertising technology, e-commerce AI.

**Fourth: Hey_ on the Telegraph and new brands.** After the Telegraph acquisition — scaling the Hey_ AI chatbot to a British audience. Potential: 700,000 Telegraph paying subscribers + Spectator audience.

**Fifth: resolving the Politico AI scandal.** Following the PEN Guild arbitration ruling, Politico is reviewing its AI policy. Expect a public AI editorial charter for Politico and updated union agreements on AI usage.

## 11. Technology Gaps

**1. Unified CMS and AI platform across the portfolio.** Springer has no unified editorial platform like Arc XP. Each brand runs its own CMS. As the portfolio expands through acquisitions (Politico, Business Insider, Axios, Telegraph), the absence of a unified platform means duplicated costs and difficulty scaling AI tools.

**2. SSAI video player.** No public data on the existence of full server-side ad insertion for video. At the traffic volumes of Bild and Welt, this potentially represents significant losses in video advertising. Washington Post (PoWa), Schibsted (TV4 Play), and even some Arc XP clients have more mature video monetisation.

**3. Federated AI across brands.** Hey_ works on the content of individual brands. There is no product where a Bild user can receive an answer drawing on Politico or Business Insider archives. This is an architectural opportunity that has not yet been realised — despite all the content being available.

**4. AI revenue sharing agreements with journalists.** Le Monde pays journalists 25% of AI licensing income. Schibsted does not, but this will create friction. At Springer, the Politico scandal already demonstrated: without a clear AI social contract with newsrooms, legal and reputational risks arise. A transparent model for sharing AI revenues with journalists is needed.

**5. Unified subscription infrastructure.** Bild+, Welt+, Politico Pro, Business Insider Prime, Axios Pro — each operates separately. No single *"Axel Springer Pass"* exists. With a global brand portfolio, bundling is an obvious opportunity analogous to NYT All Access.

## 13. Overall Assessment

Axel Springer is the most aggressive and globally ambitious publisher in this analysis. The world's first OpenAI deal, the Microsoft partnership, the Telegraph acquisition two weeks ago, a dedicated M&A team for AI acquisitions, Hey_ in production with millions of users — these are real achievements that no competitor can match.

But the aggressiveness is simultaneously the main weakness. The Politico scandal showed: the speed of AI adoption without sufficient guardrails destroys reader trust faster than it builds it. Döpfner's conflict of interest is a structural risk that cannot be eliminated through technology investment. And the absence of a unified CMS platform across a growing global portfolio is an operational vulnerability that will become more expensive with each new acquisition.

| Dimension | Assessment |
|---|---|
| AI maturity | **High** (first OpenAI deal, Hey_ in production) |
| Global ambitions | **Best in group** (DE + US + UK with Telegraph) |
| M&A strategy | **Aggressive and consistent** |
| Conflict of interest | **High** (Döpfner as editorial risk) |
| Technology platform | **Weaker than competitors** (no Arc XP, no unified CMS) |
| AI adoption quality | **Contested** (Politico scandal, aggressive pace) |
| Subscription model | **Strong** — but fragmented across brands |
| Integration risk (Telegraph) | **High** — third major acquisition in five years |
| Leadership | **Ambiguous** — Döpfner is simultaneously the greatest strength and the greatest risk |

**Overall rating: B+.** The most ambitious publisher in the group with genuine global reach and the best AI partnerships. But the aggressive pace without sufficient guardrails creates reputational risks, and the concentration of power in the hands of a single CEO with strong political views is a structural risk that no new acquisition will resolve.

## 14. Executive Summary — Plain Language

### Who Axel Springer Is

A German media holding founded in 1946. The most aggressive and globally ambitious publisher in this analysis. Owns Bild (Germany's largest newspaper), Welt, Politico, Business Insider, Axios, Morning Brew, and — just recently, in March 2026 — acquired The Daily Telegraph for $766M.

In 2024 they sold their profitable classified advertising business (jobs and property portals) for £11.3bn — and now Mathias Döpfner has an enormous war chest for acquisitions. He is spending it fast.

### What They Do Well

They were first in the world to sign a partnership with OpenAI — Bild, Welt, and Politico content trains ChatGPT and appears in responses with attribution. This gave early API access and technological advantages unavailable to competitors.

Hey_ — the AI chatbot for Bild readers — is already used by millions of people. This is not a pilot or a beta. This is a real product at real scale.

The brand portfolio covers the three largest English-speaking markets: USA (Politico, Business Insider, Axios), Germany (Bild, Welt), and now the UK (Telegraph). This is the only publisher in the analysis with genuine global reach.

### Döpfner — The Greatest Strength and the Greatest Risk Simultaneously

Mathias Döpfner controls approximately 98% of the media business. He formulated the strategy (*"Digital is the new print. AI is the new digital"*), he personally makes acquisition decisions, and he speaks at conferences as the ideologist for the entire industry.

But he is also the source of a structural conflict of interests. His correspondence with Bild editors ahead of the 2021 German elections, his public political positions, his declared *"core values"* as mandatory for employees — all of this creates risks for the editorial independence of the brands. In the case of the Washington Post, the owner destroyed reader trust. At Springer, this story has not yet played out in full — but the preconditions are there.

### What Is Not Working or At Risk

The Politico scandal: AI summaries were published on the homepage without review and contained gross errors. Journalists won the arbitration. This is the direct consequence of an overly aggressive pace of AI adoption without sufficient quality controls.

No unified technology product for the market. Springer acquires companies but does not build a platform like Arc XP that other publishers could buy. This is a missed revenue stream.

The Telegraph is the third major acquisition in five years. Each requires integration, time, and money. If the pace of acquisitions outpaces the pace of integration, the company loses operational efficiency.

### One Conclusion

Axel Springer is the most aggressive player in the media industry. More capital than any competitor in the group. Unique global reach. Best AI partnerships. But the speed of movement creates risks that Döpfner publicly acknowledges but does not systematically resolve: AI adoption quality, editorial independence, CEO conflict of interest. By 2028 it will be clear — was this the right pace, or too fast.
