## 1.1 History — From British Occupation to Digital Magazine

### Founding (1947)

Der Spiegel was founded on **4 January 1947** in the British occupation zone of Germany. Its original initiator and sponsor was the British military administration — the magazine was conceived as an instrument of denazification and democratic media-building in post-war Germany. The first issue appeared under the name *Diese Woche* ("This Week"). Following disagreements with the British authorities, the magazine passed to **Rudolf Augstein** — a former military radio operator who was 23 years old at the time.

Augstein renamed the magazine Der Spiegel ("The Mirror") and formulated the editorial principle: *"Sagen, was ist"* — say what is, without censorship, without fear of authority. This principle was tested within its first decade.

### The Spiegel-Affäre (1962) — The Foundation of German Press Freedom

In **October 1962**, Der Spiegel published an article on the weakness of the German armed forces during NATO exercises. Defence Minister Franz Josef Strauss accused the editorial team of high treason. Thirty-six police officers entered the offices, confiscated thousands of documents, and Augstein and several editors were arrested.

This was the harshest attack on press freedom in post-war West Germany. The response was mass street demonstrations — German civil society came out to defend Der Spiegel. Strauss was ultimately forced to resign. The Federal Constitutional Court in 1966 established **the foundational principles of press freedom** in Germany — in large part as a direct response to these events.

For German society after 1962, Der Spiegel is more than a magazine. It is the institution that tested and confirmed the democratic foundations of the country.

### The Unique Ownership Structure

In **1971**, Augstein sold 25% of shares to Gruner+Jahr (now part of Bertelsmann). In his will, he stipulated that heirs must sell 0.5% of shares to each of the two other shareholders, thereby losing their blocking minority. Since then, the **majority owner has been KG Beteiligungsgesellschaft für SPIEGEL-Mitarbeiter** — a company of long-serving Spiegel employees.

This means: long-serving employees receive 50.5% of profits and participate through elected representatives in key decisions — from investments to management appointments.

## 2. How Der Spiegel Thinks as a Publisher

### 2.1 An Investigative Magazine, Not a Daily News Operation

Der Spiegel operates at a different pace from every other publisher in this series. Its competitive advantage is not speed but depth. A typical Der Spiegel piece: a multi-thousand-word feature involving travel to the scene, multiple sources, extensive documentation, and editorial verification through its own *Dokumentation* department.

**Dokumentation** is a unique institution: the largest fact-checking department in Germany and, by some accounts, among the largest in the world (exceeding the New Yorker's). Every sentence, every claim in a Der Spiegel piece passes through Dokumentation before publication. This institution became the ironic victim of the Relotius scandal: it failed precisely because the reporter methodically fabricated the sources and contacts it was checking.

### 2.2 Employee Co-owners — A Unique Organisational Model

Der Spiegel is the only publisher in this series with **genuine employee capitalism** at the ownership level. This means:
- Long-serving employees receive 50.5% of profits
- They participate through elected representatives in management appointments
- The newsroom holds real, not merely declarative, control over editorial policy

Practical consequence: Der Spiegel is harder to change radically from above (as Thomson Reuters can change Reuters), but editorial independence is protected structurally rather than by statutory declarations alone.

### 2.3 The German Market Context — Europe's Hardest Market for Paywalls

Germany is a structurally difficult market for paid news. Only **13%** of German online users pay for digital news — one of the lowest figures among developed economies (Norway: 42%, Sweden: ~30%).

In this context: the subscriber leader is **Bild+** (~700,000+ subscribers) — Axel Springer's tabloid. Der Spiegel trails Bild+ on subscriber volume. All major German publishers (Spiegel, Zeit, FAZ, Welt) compete for the same narrow slice of paying audience.

**Editor-in-Chief:** Steffen Klusmann

In 2019, the magazine and website editorial teams merged — creating a unified newsroom of approximately 500 for all formats.

### 3.2 Dokumentation — A Unique Institution

Der Spiegel's documentation department is the largest fact-checking operation in German-language media. Every published fact, every name, every quote is verified through Dokumentation.

**90% of the archive** — millions of articles spanning 75+ years — is **not accessible on the open web**. This is a unique asset that Der Spiegel began actively monetising through AI in 2024 (see Section 4).

## 4. Video Platform & Technology

### 4.1 Video Player — JW Player (JWP Connatix)

Der Spiegel uses **JW Player** — one of the most widely deployed independent video players in the media industry. This is not a proprietary development like Schibsted's Stream platform; it is an external solution.

What JW Player provides for Der Spiegel:
- Live streaming for press conferences and talk shows
- AVOD monetisation (advertising within video)
- Recommendations engine — autoplay of related content
- Reliability at high load: Biden inauguration coverage generated **2 million minutes of live-streamed viewing**

Documented result over first year: **+17% video views year on year**.

### 4.2 SPIEGEL TV — FAST Channels (December 2025)

This is the most recent significant video development. Der Spiegel launched two **FAST channels** *(Free Ad-Supported Streaming TV — free streaming with advertising, no subscription required)*.

**What FAST is:** think of traditional television — you switch on a channel and watch whatever is broadcasting. You choose nothing, pay nothing, simply watch the stream. Advertising is inserted automatically and generates revenue for the channel. FAST is the same concept, but on the internet. A viewer opens Samsung TV or Amazon Prime Video, finds the "Spiegel TV" channel, and it simply plays — 24 hours a day, 7 days a week.

**Der Spiegel's two FAST channels:**
- **Spiegel TV** — 24/7 mix of magazine features, documentaries, reports, knowledge and history content
- **Spiegel TV action+crime** — police operations, investigations, criminal subcultures in Germany

**Distribution as of December 2025 — 10 platforms:**
Amazon Prime Video, Joyn, Samsung TV Plus, Pluto TV, Titan OS (Philips TVs), LG, Rakuten TV, Xiaomi, wedotv, Whale TV.

**Why this matters strategically:** SPIEGEL TV has been producing documentary and investigative television content since 1988. A vast archive had been sitting either unused or tied to old broadcast contracts with VOX and RTL. Der Spiegel now takes this existing archive, builds a 24-hour stream from it on third-party platforms, and earns advertising revenue with **zero additional production costs**. The content is already made; the salaries were already paid.

Der Spiegel does not build its own streaming platform. Instead, it **distributes content across third-party platforms for free and earns from the advertising revenue share**. The analogy: renting a property through Airbnb — the guests live there, Airbnb takes a commission, the remainder goes to the owner.

### 4.3 farmerswife — Production Planning

SPIEGEL TV migrated from physical whiteboards to the **farmerswife** cloud system for television production planning. This is an operational tool — schedule management, team utilisation visibility, cost control. Not a consumer-facing product, but a sign of operational professionalisation in the production arm.

### 4.4 Video Strategy Comparison

| | **Der Spiegel** | **VG / Aftonbladet** | **Bloomberg** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Video player | JW Player (external) | Schibsted Stream (proprietary) | Proprietary |
| Video strategy | FAST channels on 10 platforms | Own platform | Bloomberg TV + streaming |
| Videofy equivalent | ❌ None | ✅ Videofy | ❌ None |
| Video production | SPIEGEL TV (separate division) | Integrated into newsroom | Studio content |

The key difference: Schibsted built its own video infrastructure and keeps everything within the group. Der Spiegel took the lighter path — JW Player for the website and FAST distribution across third-party platforms. This is cheaper but means dependence on partners and less control over monetisation and audience data.

## 5. Automation & AI — Deliberate and Honest Strategy

### 5.1 Ole Reissmann — The Public Face of AI Strategy

Ole Reissmann has been Director of AI at Spiegel Gruppe since January 2024. Before that: 14 years at Der Spiegel at the intersection of editorial, product and innovation, beginning as a technology and big data reporter. He developed deep expertise in machine learning and audio.

Reissmann is one of the most open and publicly active AI directors in this series. He posts regularly on LinkedIn with commentary on AI experiments. At INMA GenAI Initiative (June 2024): *"What GenAI is good at, in my opinion, is hallucination and stereotypes."* This level of candour is atypical for an AI director at a major media company.

### 5.2 AI Tools in Production

| Tool | Status | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **SEO headline optimisation** | Active | AI assistant for search-optimised headline variants |
| **AI summaries** | Active | Short versions of long-form articles |
| **Internal linking** | Active | Automated internal link recommendations |
| **Gender Report** | Active | NLP analysis of gender balance in publications |
| **AI archive** | Active (2024) | Natural language interaction with 90% of archive not on the open web |
| **ML subscription model** | Active | Predicts likelihood of a reader converting to a subscriber |
| **AI podcast** | Feasibility study | German tech company reads articles, generates podcasts automatically |
| **Browser plugin** | Experiment | Reader-facing AI product (details not disclosed) |

### 5.3 The AI Archive — The Most Distinctive AI Product

This is Der Spiegel's most interesting AI case in this series.

The Dokumentation department holds a database of **millions of articles**, 90% of which **are not indexed on the open internet**. In 2024, Der Spiegel built a tool for **natural language interaction** with this archive.

Example use case (Reissmann, WAN-IFRA November 2024): in the course of a debate about military conscription, the archive retrieved ~250 articles on the topic. *"You don't need to read them all; through smart prompting, you get summaries, timelines and insights into opinion shifts or contradictions between articles."*

This solves a challenge unique to a magazine with 75 years of history: how to make decades of quality investigations accessible to editors in real time. No other publisher in this series has an equivalent tool at this scale of historical archive.

### 5.4 Gender Report — AI Audit of Editorial Quality

An equivalent of Aftonbladet's Gender GPT, with one important difference: Der Spiegel applies it **retrospectively** to years of archive data — not only to current publications.

NLP analysis reveals: who does the editorial team write about — men or women, in which contexts, how does the balance change over time? Reissmann: *"We might find we're focusing too much on older white men, leading to adjustments in our content."*

### 5.5 ML Subscription Prediction Model

Der Spiegel developed and publicly documented (WAN-IFRA, 2023) a machine learning model to predict the probability of a reader subscribing. The model analyses user behavioural data: number of visits, time on page, average article reading time, paywall loads, subscription page visits, number of articles read.

**Key finding:** *"The decision to subscribe is not a sudden, instantaneous action, but an informed decision based on experience with our outlet."*

The model enables segmentation of readers by "affinity-to-subscribe" and optimises the timing and format of the subscription offer.

### 5.6 AI Podcast — Experiment Under Evaluation

Der Spiegel is conducting a feasibility study with a German tech company: the machine "reads" articles and automatically generates podcasts. The only human stage is a script review before text-to-speech conversion. Ad breaks, music and final production are added automatically.

Reissmann (WAN-IFRA, November 2024): *"We're still assessing whether we want to fully implement this — it's currently a feasibility study."*

> **📌 Editorial observation:** "Transformation" is where Reissmann sees the primary AI opportunity: *"Put text into a machine and quickly receive a summary, podcast, translation, or video."* For a weekly magazine with 90% of its archive in closed storage, this fundamentally changes the economics of audio production: instead of hiring a narrator for every archival article — automate. This is a direct analogue of Videofy at VG, but for audio rather than video.

## 6. Partnership with Perplexity — First Strategic AI Deal

In July 2024, Der Spiegel became the **first major European quality publication** to sign an agreement with **Perplexity AI** — the AI search engine competing with Google.

The deal: Der Spiegel content becomes more visible in Perplexity results; Der Spiegel receives a revenue share. Fortune and Time signed parallel agreements.

Reissmann describes this as a "high-profile strategic partnership" and regularly references it at conferences as part of Der Spiegel's positioning within the AI ecosystem.

**Why it matters:** Perplexity is a bet that AI search engines are becoming the primary discovery channel for quality journalistic content. If Google loses share to AI interfaces — the Perplexity partnership gives Der Spiegel a position in the new channel ahead of competitors.

> **📌 Editorial observation:** The paradox is that Perplexity uses AI to summarise content — potentially reducing direct traffic to Spiegel.de if the reader receives an answer inside Perplexity without clicking through. The revenue-sharing agreement is designed to compensate for this loss. But the mechanics of this compensation are not publicly disclosed — by Der Spiegel or by any other publisher that has signed comparable deals.

## 8. The Relotius Scandal (2018) — The Central Episode

This is the only section unique to Der Spiegel in this series, and it requires dedicated treatment.

### What happened

In December 2018, Der Spiegel announced that star reporter **Claas Relotius** had fabricated articles "on a grand scale." Over 7 years of working for the magazine he published nearly 60 articles, at least 14 of which contained wholly or partially invented facts, characters and quotes.

The deception was uncovered by a freelance journalist, **Juan Moreno**, working with Relotius on a joint piece. He became suspicious, then gathered evidence independently — travelling to Arizona to confirm that Relotius's sources did not exist. Only then did Der Spiegel begin its internal investigation.

Relotius confessed. The magazine published a special 23-page investigation under a plain cover.

### Why it was not caught earlier

Relotius methodically created fictitious email addresses and Facebook accounts for "sources" to provide to fact-checkers. **Dokumentation** — formally the world's largest fact-checking department — was checking contacts that the reporter himself had created.

The deeper problem: the editorial team wanted to read such stories. Relotius wrote what the German reader wanted to hear about Trump's America, about refugees, about Guantánamo — narratives confirming existing beliefs. A story too good to be true did not trigger scepticism. It triggered awards.

### Consequences and Der Spiegel's Response

- **Spiegel Standards (2021)** — new editorial code including revised verification processes
- Standards for commercial partnerships and foundation-funded projects
- Public acknowledgement of failures — Der Spiegel left Relotius's articles accessible with a notice referencing the ongoing investigation

> **📌 Editorial observation:** The Relotius case is the direct context for understanding Der Spiegel's AI strategy. Reissmann repeatedly emphasises: AI must not be used to gather information for articles, because it hallucinates. This is the application of the Relotius lesson to the AI era: if even a human "star reporter" could fabricate material for years — then AI, which hallucinates structurally, is even less suited to being a factual source. Documentation and archive access: yes. Writing: no.

## 12. What Der Spiegel Doesn't Have — Forward-Looking Assessment

This section documents the gaps most clearly visible when Der Spiegel is mapped against the full peer group.

### 12.1 Permanent Reader-Facing AI Assistant

**The gap:** This is the primary missing piece. El País has Vera, Aftonbladet has Election Buddy. Der Spiegel has nothing permanent for readers.

Yet everything needed already exists: 75 years of verified archive, the Dokumentation fact-checking department, and a working AI interface to the archive — but only for the editorial team, not for readers.

**What could be built:** "Ask the archive" — readers ask questions, AI answers only from Der Spiegel material. This would be more accurate and safer than most AI chatbots, because hallucination is structurally excluded: the source is closed and verified.

The technology is already proven internally. The only decision missing is whether to open it to readers — and whether to put it behind the paywall (like Vera) or make it free (like Election Buddy).

### 12.2 Videofy — Article-to-Video Conversion

**The gap:** VG developed Videofy and released it as open source in March 2026. Der Spiegel publishes thousands of long investigative features, which are ideal for short video summaries.

Der Spiegel has SPIEGEL TV with a production team, JW Player for distribution, and FAST channels on 10 platforms — but no automated tool converting a text feature into a 30-second video. This is a logical next step, especially since Videofy is already open source and free.

Each archive article converted to a 60-second video becomes new content for the FAST channel stream — at zero additional editorial cost.

### 12.3 AI Podcast as a Permanent Product

Der Spiegel acknowledges this themselves — the experiment has been running since 2024, but the implementation decision has not been made. Yet they have 75 years of investigative archive, most of which has never been in audio format. This is ready-made content for automatic audio conversion.

Aftonbladet's Snabbversions does something analogous for text. Der Spiegel could do the same for audio. Given SPIEGEL TV's established audio-video production capability, the infrastructure cost would be minimal.

### 12.4 Dynamic Paywall

**The gap:** The subscription prediction ML model exists — Der Spiegel documented it publicly in WAN-IFRA (2023). But a dynamic paywall that adjusts the timing and terms of the subscription offer in real time based on each individual reader's behaviour is not publicly documented.

In a German market with only 13% paying for news, this could meaningfully increase conversion. Il Corriere della Sera implemented an equivalent and saw a sharp rise in digital subscribers.

### 12.5 Archive Licensing to External Partners

**The gap:** Currently the archive is used exclusively by the editorial team. But it could become a **licensed product for other media organisations, educational platforms or research institutions** — similar to how Reuters sells its news wire.

75 years of verified German-language content is a valuable training corpus for AI companies. Reuters and Le Monde have already signed such agreements. Der Spiegel has not.

**Revenue calculation:** if Der Spiegel licensed archive access at even a fraction of what Reuters charges for its wire — this could represent a new revenue stream with no additional production costs whatsoever.

### 12.6 Priority Ranking

| Gap | Why it matters | Difficulty |
|---|---|---|
| Reader AI assistant | Infrastructure already built internally | Low — open existing tool |
| Videofy integration | Open source, free to implement | Low — Videofy is public |
| AI podcast | SPIEGEL TV has production capability | Medium |
| Dynamic paywall | ML model already exists | Medium |
| Archive licensing | Highest long-term revenue potential | High — requires legal framework |

## Glossary

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Spiegel+ | Der Spiegel's paid content product: long features, investigations, analysis. Standard price ~€4.99/week; under 30: €2.99/week. |
| Dokumentation | Der Spiegel's documentation and fact-checking department — the largest in Germany. Every published fact is verified here. Became the ironic victim of the Relotius scandal. |
| AI archive | Natural language interface to 90% of Der Spiegel's historical archive (~millions of articles, most not on the open internet). Built in 2024. |
| Gender Report | NLP analysis of gender balance in articles — who is mentioned more frequently, men or women, and in which contexts. Can be applied retrospectively to years of archive data. |
| Spiegel-Affäre | 1962 scandal: Der Spiegel editors arrested for alleged treason after publishing an article on Bundeswehr weakness. Led to the constitutional foundations of press freedom in West Germany. |
| Relotius case | December 2018 scandal: star reporter Claas Relotius fabricated articles "on a grand scale" for 7 years. Largest German journalism scandal since 1962. |
| Mitarbeiter KG | KG Beteiligungsgesellschaft für SPIEGEL-Mitarbeiter — a company of long-serving Der Spiegel employees. Majority owner (~50.5% of votes and profits). |
| SPIEGEL TV | Der Spiegel's television division: documentary films and news programmes on German commercial channels (RTL, VOX, etc.). Founded 1991. |
| FAST channels | Free Ad-Supported Streaming TV — a 24/7 video stream distributed on third-party platforms (Amazon, Samsung, LG etc.), free for viewers, monetised through advertising. Der Spiegel launched two FAST channels in 2025, now on 10 platforms. |
| Perplexity AI | AI search engine competing with Google. Der Spiegel signed a revenue-sharing partnership in July 2024 — the first among major European quality publications to do so. |
| *"Sagen, was ist"* | "Say what is" — Der Spiegel's editorial motto, formulated by Rudolf Augstein. The principle of independent, fearless journalism. |
| AI podcast | Experiment: a German tech company reads Der Spiegel articles and generates podcasts automatically. The only human stage is script review. Still at feasibility study stage (2024–2025). |
| JW Player | Independent video player technology (JWP Connatix) used by Der Spiegel for video hosting, live streaming and AVOD monetisation on Spiegel.de. |
| ePaper | Digital replica of the print magazine. Der Spiegel: growth from 50,000 readers (2016) to 295,000 (2023) — +590%. |
| Dynamic paywall | A paywall that adjusts the subscription offer in real time based on individual reader behaviour. Der Spiegel has the ML infrastructure; a fully dynamic implementation is not yet publicly documented. |
