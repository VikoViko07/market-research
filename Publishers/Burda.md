## 1. Who Burda Is Today

Hubert Burda Media is a German family-owned media holding with €2.74 billion in revenue (2024). The group went through a quiet but fundamental transformation: in June 2025 it split into two independent pillars.

**Burda Media** — all publishing and digital assets (BurdaForward, BurdaVerlag, agency C3, BurdaDruck, BurdaBroadcast). CEO: Jan Wachtel (since January 2026).

**Burda Equity** — investment arm (New Work SE, HolidayCheck, Cyberport, and 30+ portfolio companies across Europe and Asia). CEO: Marc Al-Hames.

A generational transition happened simultaneously: founder Hubert Burda handed control to his children Elisabeth Burda Furtwängler and Jacob Burda in February 2025. Philipp Welte, who defined the group's strategy for 30 years, is leaving. New CEO Jan Wachtel comes from Bauer Media — a name without a public digital-first track record. This creates leadership uncertainty at a critical moment.

**BurdaForward** is the digital core of the group: 14 brands, 41 million monthly unique users (approximately 67% of German internet audience), 400+ employees. Flagship: FOCUS Online, Germany's largest news website. Alongside it: CHIP.de (technology), Finanzen100 (finance), BUNTE.de (lifestyle), NetMoms, HuffPost DE, EFAHRER.com, The Weather Channel DE, and others.

The group as a whole dipped 0.4%. Consumer Media (which includes BurdaForward) grew 3%. Advertisers are migrating to walled gardens — Meta, Google, TikTok.

Subscriptions exist (Focus Digital Abo, TV Spielfilm+), but their share of the revenue mix is minimal. 41 million users with a conversion rate below 1% to paying subscribers is an asset that is working at idle. For comparison: Der Spiegel has around 300,000 digital subscribers with an audience many times smaller.

## 4. How Burda Positions Itself — Three Narratives

### Narrative 1: "Media House of Solutions"
BurdaForward publicly calls itself not a news publisher but *"a media house of solutions"* — a company that not only covers problems but shows paths to improvement. This is called constructive journalism, and it is an editorial principle, not a marketing slogan. Against the background of a general crisis of trust in media, this is a deliberate differentiation.

### Narrative 2: "Technology meets Journalism"
At every conference Burda emphasises: we are not just a publisher, we are a technology company with an editorial DNA. AISSIST is the main proof-of-concept. Thunder CMS is proof of openness and willingness to share with the market. The IBM Case Study 2024 describes BurdaForward as *"the digital media house of the future."*

### Narrative 3: "Bollwerk der verlässlichen Information" (Bastion of Reliable Information)
In response to the wave of AI-generated content and disinformation, Burda positions quality German journalism as a protective asset. Philipp Welte articulated this bluntly: *"Wir haben es mit Wettbewerbern zu tun, die die ökonomische Macht von Staaten haben"* — "We are competing with players who have the economic power of states." This is simultaneously an honest admission and an attempt to occupy the moral high ground.

## 5. Editorial Kitchen: How It Actually Works

### Structural Note

FOCUS Online and FOCUS Magazine are **two separate editorial teams** — deliberately not merged. The magazine (60 journalists + 30 in FOCUS Money) works in Berlin under Georg Meck and Franziska Reich. FOCUS Online works in Munich under Florian Festl. This is a fundamental Burda position: digital and print brands maintain independent identities even within the same holding group.

BurdaForward as a whole breaks into three editorial clusters:
- **News** — FOCUS Online, HuffPost DE, Finanzen100, NetMoms
- **Technology** — CHIP.de, EFAHRER.com
- **Lifestyle** — BUNTE.de, fitFORFUN, The Weather Channel DE

Each cluster operates with its own team and editor-in-chief, but on the shared Thunder CMS platform.

### The "News To Know" Team — The Core Editorial Unit

Inside BurdaForward there is a cross-functional team "News To Know" of more than 50 people, covering the full path from breaking news to first analytical pieces. Key brands: Focus.de, Bunte.de, Chip.de. This is a hybrid structure where journalists work alongside product managers, data analysts, and UX specialists.

### The Real Daily Workflow

1. **Morning dashboard** — the day starts with the AISSIST dashboard: trending topics, yesterday's CTR, reading time by format, WhatsApp channel activity.
2. **Morning editorial meeting** — chief editors and section editors prioritise the day. The filter is simple: a topic must be either timely (breaking news), viral (high sharing potential), or exclusive.
3. **Format decision** — text article, short video via Magic AI Tool, WhatsApp channel post, or all three simultaneously. This is a strategic decision per topic, not a default.
4. **AI layer** — AISSIST suggests headline variants, auto-generates summaries for different platforms (shorter for WhatsApp, longer for the site), performs SEO optimisation.
5. **Final approval** — the section editor approves and publishes.

As Markert, BurdaForward's Director of Innovation Projects, stated: *"We don't need people to write summaries and short news items — but we do need people to verify information, find sources, and deliver information to the newsroom."*

### What Is and Is Not Automated

| Layer | Status |
|---|---|
| Summary generation | Fully automated |
| Translations (DeepL via AISSIST) | Fully automated |
| Interview/press conference transcription | Fully automated |
| Image selection (Black Forest Labs) | Fully automated |
| Video news on CHIP.de (Magic AI Tool) | Fully automated |
| Headlines | Partially — AI suggests, editor chooses |
| SEO tags and categorisation | Partially automated |
| Cross-posting to WhatsApp channels | Partially automated |
| Topic selection | Human only |
| Reporting and interviews | Human only |
| Editorial position and judgement | Human only |
| Fact-checking | Human only |

### Prodigy — The Constructive Journalism Tool

FOCUS Online editor Hanna Klein developed a content classification system using the Active Learning tool "Prodigy" — it labels materials as negative, positive, or "feel good news" and thereby ensures a balanced editorial mix on the site. The technology was developed by data scientists Panos Strouthopoulos and Charalampos Zinoviadis inside BurdaForward in close collaboration with the editorial team. This is the operationalisation of the editorial philosophy: constructive journalism is literally an algorithm.

### WhatsApp Channels: Three Employees, 170 Channels

Simone Wagner, Director Publisher-Network at BurdaForward, explains the logic: *"We have many brands and topic areas that rarely make the front page and therefore get few views. WhatsApp Channels allow us to deliver this content directly to users."*

170 topic channels on FOCUS Online, CHIP, and other brands generate two million additional page views per month — managed by just three employees with AI. AISSIST automates selection, formatting, and publishing. An editor sets criteria ("this channel only gets articles about smartphones under €500"), AI selects and publishes, humans monitor for anomalies.

### Newsletter Structure

**Free editorial newsletters** — digests from FOCUS Online, CHIP, BUNTE. Focus on retention and return traffic. AI-assisted curation, but editorial control.

**FOCUS Plus and exclusive newsletters** — paid tier. Access to special editorial content via account on sites and in apps.

**Focus 2030 paid newsletter** — editorial chief Georg Meck announced a paid daily newsletter based on the magazine's strongest materials, leading to paid content. This is the core of the "Focus 2030" project: building a paid digital audience around the magazine brand, not just the website.

## 7. Strengths

### Technology

**AISSIST — own AI platform.** The only European publisher with a multimodal AI platform of their own development, already licensed to the external market as B2B SaaS. OpenAI + Anthropic + DeepL + ElevenLabs + Black Forest Labs in one interface for 2,500+ employees.

**Thunder CMS — open-source leader.** 2,000+ sites across Europe. Burda created an industry standard and manages the ecosystem around it — strategically closer to WordPress than to an ordinary CMS.

**Magic AI Tool — video from text.** A production solution on CHIP.de. Axel Springer is only considering acquiring Synthesia for similar functionality.

**Prodigy — tone classification AI.** The operationalisation of constructive journalism: an algorithm that influences what appears on the front page.

### Audience and Distribution

**WhatsApp — undisputed DE leader.** 170+ channels, 5M+ subscribers, 2M additional page views per month — managed by 3 staff with AI. No German competitor has reached this scale.

**41M users ≈ 67% of German internet.** FOCUS Online, CHIP.de, Finanzen100 — coverage larger than Spiegel + Zeit + FAZ combined. The largest digital audience among German publishers.

**DLD Munich — pre-Davos networking.** A proprietary conference 5–7 days before Davos. Direct CEO-level access to OpenAI, Microsoft, Alphabet before deals are signed. A partnership pipeline competitors cannot copy.

**Family control = long-term horizon.** No pressure from quarterly KPIs. Elisabeth and Jacob Burda can invest in 5–7-year strategies unavailable to public companies.

## 8. Weaknesses

### Technology Gaps

**No SSAI video player.** Custom player + YouTube embed without server-side ad insertion. Losing 20–35% of video inventory to ad blockers. CPM is half of Arc PoWa. At Burda's scale — tens of millions of euros annually.

**No agentic AI.** AISSIST provides tools for individual tasks. Autonomous multi-step workflows don't exist. NYT is building them, Springer is testing. Burda is only declaring.

**No audience-facing AI product.** WaPo has "Ask the Post AI," Spiegel has a browser plugin with archive context, NYT is testing a personalised podcast. Burda has 41M users and not a single AI product for the reader.

**No own ad server.** Full dependency on Google Ad Manager. WaPo and NYT are independent in their ad stack — a lever in advertiser negotiations and protection against Google's policy changes.

### Business Model and Audience

**Advertising ≈ 95% of revenue.** Critical dependency on a single channel in a declining German digital display market. Advertisers are migrating to Meta, Google, TikTok.

**41M anonymous visitors without conversion.** Subscriptions are below 5% of revenue. Der Spiegel with a far smaller audience has 300K+ paying subscribers. Burda cannot convert traffic into relationships.

**No first-party data strategy.** The majority of 41M are anonymous visits. No systematic data collection funnel. Without this, neither premium advertising nor personalisation is possible.

**SEO dependency under Google AI threat.** FOCUS Online and CHIP.de live on organic search. Google AI Overviews are already cutting CTR on informational queries by 20–40%. Direct channels are insufficient.

## 11. Future Vision — What They Say Publicly

### Five Declared Strategic Priorities 2026–2030

**1. AISSIST as European media infrastructure.** Burda sees itself not just as a publisher but as an AI infrastructure provider for the European media market. AISSIST is already a B2B SaaS. The goal: to become the "Salesforce for publishers" — a platform used by hundreds of European editorial teams.

**2. Agentic AI in the newsroom.** At DLD 2025, Eli Varn spoke directly about autonomous AI agents as personal assistants for journalists. The goal: a system that monitors trends on its own, suggests topics, prepares drafts. Currently a declaration — but a publicly named priority.

**3. Focus 2030 — paid digital audience.** The project of FOCUS Magazine chief editor Georg Meck: paid daily newsletter → paid content → subscription base around the FOCUS brand. An attempt to close the gap between 41M in traffic and near-zero conversion to paying subscribers.

**4. Video and multiplatform.** Magic AI Tool scales beyond CHIP.de to all brands. Vertical video for TikTok and Instagram Reels. WhatsApp Channels as the primary mobile channel. The goal: become omniplatform without proportional cost growth.

**5. European digital sovereignty.** At DLD 2026 the central theme was sovereignty of European data and AI. Burda positions itself as a player building a European alternative to American Big Tech platforms — GDPR-compliant, local data, local partnerships.

## 12. Three Future Scenarios 2027–2030

### Scenario A: "European AI Media Platform" — Probability: ~30%

AISSIST becomes SaaS for 300–500 European editorial teams. Agentic AI launches before 2027. Focus 2030 delivers 500K+ paying subscribers. Group revenue grows 5–7% per year. Burda becomes an infrastructure player, not just a publisher. This requires: new leadership executing decisively, SSAI video player built or acquired, and first-party data strategy launched in 2026.

### Scenario B: "Strong German Player" — Probability: ~55%

Burda retains leadership in Germany. AISSIST remains a niche SaaS. Subscriptions grow slowly. Advertising stabilises at a new level. Profitable, reliable, family-owned — but without global ambitions. The most realistic outcome and a comfortable one — but not the future Burda declares from the DLD stage.

### Scenario C: "Structural Crisis" — Probability: ~15%

Google AI Overviews destroy 35–40% of SEO traffic from FOCUS Online and CHIP.de. The advertising market continues declining. New management (Wachtel) fails to build a subscription model in time. By 2028 — selective brand sales or a search for a strategic partner. Burda Equity remains healthy throughout; the risk is concentrated in the media pillar.

## 13. The Gap Between Ambition and Reality

The most important observation that emerges from this research: **there is a visible gap between how Burda positions itself publicly and what they are actually building.**

On the DLD stage they talk about agentic AI, European sovereignty, and the media of the future. At the product level — no SSAI player, no audience AI product, no first-party data strategy. This is not hypocrisy; it is the typical situation of a company in a transitional period: the narrative outruns the execution.

The critical moment will come in 2027. If by then Focus 2030 has not delivered measurable subscription results and AISSIST has not scaled to the external market — Scenario B becomes a ceiling, not a temporary stop on the way to Scenario A. The leadership change (Wachtel without a digital-first track) makes this question even sharper: the new CEO will have approximately 18 months to demonstrate direction.

**Overall rating: B+.** Potential for A− exists — but only if agentic AI and the subscription funnel launch before the end of 2027. Otherwise Burda remains a very good German publisher without a chance at a global role.
