## 1. Context — Europe's Oldest Paywall at Maturity

Aftonbladet in March 2026 is Sweden's largest daily newspaper and one of Scandinavia's most successful digital publishers. Founded in **1830**, it is one of the oldest publications in this series — older than Le Monde (1944) and considerably older than El País (1976). In digital terms, Aftonbladet is a **paid news pioneer**: the Aftonbladet Plus paywall launched in **2003**, seven years before Le Monde and seventeen years before El País.

But the trajectory matters: Aftonbladet peaked at 300,000 subscribers, then fell back to ~250,000. Subscriber volume has stalled. Growth is coming only from ARPU — raising prices while holding the base stable. This is a strategic choice ("revenue over volume"), but it is a ceiling, not growth.

## 1.1 History — From Royal Bans to Digital Pioneer

### Founding and Early Bans

Aftonbladet was founded in 1830 — and immediately banned. The paper criticised Swedish King Charles XIV John and was shut down. The editorial team responded by launching a new publication under a different name. When that was banned, they launched another. In a short period the paper was banned and renamed **26 times** before the king finally relented. This is not merely a historical anecdote: it explains Aftonbladet's editorial character — independent, popular, unafraid of conflict with authority.

### The Trade Union Period

In the 1950s, Aftonbladet was acquired by **LO** (Landsorganisationen — the Swedish Trade Union Confederation). This is a unique case in this series: neither Le Monde, The Guardian, nor El País has ever been owned by the labour movement. This shaped the paper's political identity — explicitly left-of-centre, the voice of the working class. LO later sold most of its shares to Schibsted but retained one critical right: **appointing the political editor**.

### Schibsted and the Digital Push

In 1996, Schibsted acquired 49.9% of shares. By 2009 — 91%, becoming the majority owner. Under Schibsted's management, Aftonbladet became a digital pioneer:
- **1994** — one of Sweden's first news websites
- **2003** — launch of Aftonbladet Plus — one of the world's first news paywalls

| 1830 | 26 renamings before ban was lifted |

> **📌 Editorial observation:** Aftonbladet's history is that of a publisher that twice led the industry — first in 1994 with an early internet presence, then in 2003 with the first paywall. Had Aftonbladet appeared in this series in 2005, it would have been the undisputed leader. In 2026, it is a proven model that has stalled on subscriber volume. This is the essential context for evaluating its AI investments: they are needed precisely to break out of this plateau.

## 2. How Aftonbladet Thinks as a Publisher

### 2.1 Left-Leaning Tabloid with a Unique Ownership Structure

Aftonbladet is the only publisher in this series with **trade union participation in governance**. LO appoints the political editor — meaning that the editorial line on social and political matters remains explicitly left-of-centre regardless of Schibsted's decisions. For readers: this is a working-class newspaper, the voice of employees. For advertisers: Sweden's largest audience, reaching 40% of the population.

### 2.2 ARPU-First, Not Volume-First

The defining strategic position, articulated by Aftonbladet Plus head Ted Kudinoff: *"At the moment, volume matters less to us than revenue."* This is a fundamental departure from publishers chasing subscriber numbers. Aftonbladet deliberately accepts churn following promotional campaigns rather than retaining subscribers at a lower price.

### 2.3 Freemium-Dominant — Advertising Remains Primary

Despite 20+ years of operating a paywall, advertising remains Aftonbladet's dominant revenue source. Only 6–8% of content sits behind the paywall — the rest is free. This fundamentally distinguishes Aftonbladet from Aftenposten or Le Monde, where subscriptions account for 60–80% of revenue. VG and Aftonbladet are Schibsted's "reach" brands: large free audiences with a small slice of paid content.

## 3. The Editorial Kitchen — How It Actually Works

### 3.1 Newsroom Structure

Aftonbladet employs ~300 editorial staff (figure from Digiday 2018; not publicly updated as of 2025). Editor-in-Chief and CEO: **Lena Samuelsson**. Deputy Managing Editor: **Martin Schori** — who has effectively become the public face of the publication's AI strategy.

The newsroom is organised around two tiers:
- **Main stream** — general news, free, for maximum reach
- **Aftonbladet Plus** — exclusive paid content: investigations, expert journalism, sports products, product tests, lifestyle guides

A dedicated team of 16 worked exclusively on Plus content (2018 data). By 2020, subscription conversion goals were distributed across **all editorial departments** — every desk has daily targets.

### 3.2 AI Buffet — Toolkit for the Newsroom

In October 2023, Aftonbladet created an **AI Hub**: 7–8 people (4 journalists, 2 developers, 1 UX designer). Mission: build tools, test them, educate colleagues. By 2024, **260 journalists** had attended "prompt school" — mandatory AI training.

**The AI Buffet toolkit:**

| Tool | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Snabbversions** | AI summary of every article (3 bullets), embedded in CMS. CTR 43% (53% among 19–36 year olds) |
| **Spånaren** | Suggests follow-up story ideas based on the current article |
| **Buddy Reader** | Proofreading and editorial feedback on text |
| **SEO Super Optimiser** | Evaluates article preview for search performance |
| **Youth Assistant** | Generates simplified fact boxes and timelines for younger audiences |
| **Gender GPT** | Helps journalists find female experts from an internal database |
| **Voice cloning** | Clones the voice of anonymous sources for publication |

### 3.3 Snabbversions — Technical and Editorial Detail

Snabbversions are AI-generated summaries embedded directly in Aftonbladet's CMS. After an article is published, the OpenAI API automatically generates 3 bullets capturing the essence of the piece. They appear below the article headline as a collapsible block.

Key editorial moment: journalists **resisted** at launch. They feared readers would only read the summary and not the full article. When the AI Hub team showed the data — CTR of 43%, with no drop in full readership — resistance ended.

> **📌 Editorial observation:** This is the classic AI adoption story in a newsroom: resistance → data → acceptance. Importantly, Aftonbladet showed the data rather than simply issuing a directive. Martin Schori: *"The reporters wanted users to read the full articles, and not just a three-bullet summary. However, once we showed them the data and the high CTR, we could ensure smooth integration."*

### 3.4 Gender AI Audit — A Unique Editorial Initiative

The AI Hub analysed **100,000 Aftonbladet articles** over one year — plus thousands of social media posts, videos and podcasts. Finding: **a significant gender gap** in source usage (men cited substantially more often than women).

Response:
1. Built a custom GPT to help journalists find female experts from the newsroom's database and external sources
2. Created a bot that monitors gender balance on the homepage in real time

This is a rare example of AI used not to automate content production but to **audit editorial quality**. No other publisher in this series has a publicly documented equivalent.

## 4. Election Buddy — Reader-Facing AI Product

### 4.1 Valkompisen (EU Elections, May 2024)

After experimenting with smaller in-house tools, the AI Hub took on an ambitious project: a chatbot answering reader questions about the European Parliament elections. Risk acknowledged: *"The election's not a very engaging news event for Swedish people"* (Schori). Unknown whether readers would use it at all.

**Technical parameters:**
- Engine: OpenAI GPT-4
- Database: 100+ sources collected and verified by Aftonbladet journalists
- Hallucination ban: instructed to answer only from the verified database
- Language: Swedish, friendly informal tone

**Results:**
- Launch day: 15,000 questions
- Total: **150,000 questions** over several weeks
- 60% of questions were user-generated (not from the pre-prompted list)
- 78,000 users logged in — demonstrating deep engagement

### 4.2 US Election Buddy (October 2024)

The same chatbot adapted for the US presidential election. Results exceeded the EU version:
- **600,000 questions** — 4× more than the European election
- Over **25% of users were under 36** — the audience news media consistently fails to reach
- US Election Buddy was shared with VG for the Norwegian audience

**Editorial impact:** In 2020, Aftonbladet received 9,000 reader questions during the US election — journalists could answer only 300. In 2024, Election Buddy answered **all** questions, freeing journalists for complex analytical work.

> **📌 Editorial observation:** Election Buddy is the closest equivalent to El País's Vera in this series: a reader-facing AI product with a closed corpus, answering only from verified editorial content. The fundamental difference: Vera is a permanent product behind a paywall. Election Buddy is deployed for specific events and is free for all readers. This is a different strategic logic — not monetising AI, but using AI to build audience. The paradox: Vera has zero public metrics since March 2025. Election Buddy has 750,000+ documented questions and the best-evidenced youth audience numbers in the entire series.

### 4.3 Christmas Chatbot — AI as Entertainment

The AI Hub built a Christmas chatbot: readers sent photos of their Christmas tree, AI gave a rating and commentary. This is not a business tool — it is a demonstration that AI in the newsroom can be **fun**. Schori highlights this as essential for building AI culture inside the organisation: when journalists see that AI is not a threat but a tool for interesting experiments, resistance falls.

### 5.1 The AI Hub Principle — Alongside, Not Above

Martin Schori explained the rationale for creating the AI Hub: in a large Schibsted technology organisation, many newsroom AI ideas get lost in the queue of big projects. Solution: create a small, autonomous team **on top of** the main structure that can move fast.

This is fundamentally different from El País's approach (one large product — Vera) or Aftenposten's (integration into existing systems). Aftonbladet chose a **portfolio approach**: many small tools, each solving a specific problem.

### 5.2 +75% Subscription Sales via ML Homepage Model

Aftonbladet built an ML model to predict which articles are most likely to lead to a subscription. Key details:
- From 150+ parameters tested, only **~10–12 actually work**
- The model serves recommendations **only to non-logged-in readers** — subscribers get personalisation based on interest
- Required building proprietary feature store infrastructure (no off-the-shelf solutions existed)
- A/B test against previous model: **+75% subscription sales from homepage articles**

> **📌 Editorial observation:** +75% is the most concrete, measurable AI business result in this peer group specifically for subscription conversion. Aftenposten showed +25% CTR through personalisation. Aftonbladet shows +75% in actual sales. The distinction is critical: CTR is an engagement metric; sales are a business metric. Aftonbladet was also unusually honest: of 150 data points tested, only a dozen mattered. This level of transparency about what does not work is rare in the industry.

## 5.3 Video Platform — Aftonbladet TV on Schibsted Stream

### What Player Aftonbladet Uses

Aftonbladet uses **Schibsted Stream** — the group's own video platform developed internally. The Aftonbladet video product is called **Aftonbladet TV** — the Swedish counterpart to VGTV in Norway. Both run on the same technology platform maintained by a dedicated **Schibsted Video Platform** team of ~10 engineers.

Stream is deployed across **40+ Schibsted newsrooms** simultaneously. This is not a third-party player — it is a proprietary group development, integrated into the CMS and providing a unified monetisation, analytics and distribution standard across all brands.

**Video monetisation model:** pre-roll and 6-second bumper ads. Aftonbladet's head of video Martin Ekelund confirmed that video advertising is an important revenue stream, but has not disclosed figures.

**Aftonbladet video team:** 30–40 people dedicated to video production and editing. The video studio is located at the centre of the newsroom — all journalists have direct access. Approximately **15 live streams per day**.

**Why this matters for Aftonbladet Plus:** Sports content is one of the primary subscription conversion drivers across Schibsted (VG attracted 3,000 new subscribers from a single overnight boxing broadcast). Access to TV4's professional hockey, football and winter sports rights is a direct tool for growing the Aftonbladet Plus subscriber base beyond the current plateau of 250,000.

### Videofy in the Aftonbladet Ecosystem

According to Telecompaper (March 2026), Schibsted offered the Videofy AI tool jointly with TV4 — meaning that automated short news video production from articles is now available not only to Aftonbladet's newsroom but also to TV4's editorial team. This is the first known instance of Videofy being deployed in a broadcast television context.

> **📌 Editorial observation:** The $615M TV4 acquisition is not merely the purchase of a television channel. It solves two problems Aftonbladet could not solve on its own: a deficit of premium video advertising inventory and a deficit of sports rights to drive subscription conversion. Aftonbladet TV on Stream is a solid digital player. But TV4 Play is a full-scale streaming competitor to Netflix and Disney+. The Aftonbladet + TV4 combination packaged together in a subscription bundle could materially shift the competitive balance in the Swedish market. The management challenge — integrating two fundamentally different editorial cultures (newspaper vs. television) — is one Schibsted still has ahead of it.

## 6. Subscription Model — 20 Years of Evolution

### 6.1 Freemium Architecture

Aftonbladet operates a classic freemium model with distinctive parameters:
- **Free:** all news, commentary, basic content (~92–94% of everything)
- **Behind paywall:** investigations, expert journalism, sports products, product tests, lifestyle guides (~6–8%)

Every editorial department has daily conversion targets — a practice in place for 4+ years, now embedded in editorial culture.

### 6.2 Product Line

| Product | Price | What it includes |
|---|---|---|
| **Aftonbladet Plus** | ~$13/month | Exclusive content, investigations, sport |
| **Super Package** | ~$27/month | Plus + Svenska Dagbladet + Omni + Podme |
| **Trial subscription** | 1 SEK/first month | Standard trial |

The Super Package is Schibsted's answer to bundling demand: rather than buying multiple subscriptions, readers get the full Schibsted Sweden media package at double the price of one.

### 6.3 Churn — Transparency No Other Publisher Offers

Aftonbladet is the only publisher in this series to publicly disclose churn structure:
- **Annual churn: 8–10%**
- **After 100 days — churn falls to 3–5%**
- Promotional campaigns generate high initial churn (people come for the deal)
- Strategy: accept promo churn as a given; focus on the "100-day" threshold

Ted Kudinoff: *"Some churn is natural, especially after promotional campaigns. If you want to reduce churn, stop selling."* Cynical but honest: aggressive promotional campaigns fill the subscriber base with unreliable customers. Aftonbladet chose quality of base over volume.

> **📌 Editorial observation:** Le Monde, El País and The Guardian disclose no churn data at all. Aftonbladet names concrete figures — 8–10% annually (3–5% after 100 days). For context: Netflix in 2023 had ~2% monthly churn, equivalent to ~22% annually. This means Aftonbladet is at or better than a leading streaming service. These are strong numbers — which is precisely why most publishers do not disclose them.

## 15. Services Aftonbladet Will Likely Need — Forward-Looking Assessment

### 15.1 Permanent Reader AI Assistant

**Gap:** Election Buddy proved that readers — especially young ones — want dialogue with content, not passive reading. But Election Buddy only runs during elections. The rest of the time, Aftonbladet has no permanent chatbot for readers.

**What Aftonbladet needs:** A permanent "Aftonbladet Buddy" based on the full editorial archive — not just for elections. Sport, crime, investigations, health — Aftonbladet's top content categories generate hundreds of thousands of questions. The technology is already proven. The only decision remaining: make it free (like Election Buddy) or behind the paywall (like Vera)?

### 15.2 Snabbversions 2.0 — From Passive to Interactive

**Gap:** Snabbversions is a passive summary. The reader sees 3 bullets but cannot ask a follow-up question. A CTR of 43% shows the appetite is there — but the conversion from "read the summary" to "became a subscriber" is not documented.

**What Aftonbladet needs:** A "ask a question about this article" button added to Snabbversions — turning it into a micro-Election Buddy inside every article. This is the logical evolution that no publisher has yet built in this embedded form.

### 15.3 ARPU Personalisation of Subscription Offers

**Gap:** All Plus subscribers receive the same product at $13/month. No differentiation by interest profile, reading history, or geography.

**What Aftonbladet needs:** An ML model that personalises subscription offers at the point of conversion. A reader who only reads sport content gets a sports-focused offer. A reader with health interests gets a health-focused offer. The existing ML infrastructure (+75% homepage sales) can be extended for this purpose.

### 15.4 Scaling the Gender Audit

**Gap:** The gender AI audit was conducted once, on 100,000 articles. It changed editorial practices — but it was a one-time project.

**What Aftonbladet needs:** Continuous real-time monitoring of gender, age and geographic balance across the full production process — not just the homepage. The homepage bot already exists; the challenge is systematising it across every editorial decision point.
